# The reference board

This skill builds a reference board a maker can work from without asking a question, and audits a board that already exists. The single idea underneath it is that a board earns the word direction only when it visibly rejects things. A page of pictures someone liked permits every piece of work, so it cannot be wrong and cannot be followed; a page that excludes the wrong candidate permits a narrower set of work, which is the thing a maker needs in order to plan. Every move below is a way of converting liking into exclusion: naming what you are taking from an image, saying where that attribute lands in the deliverable, and writing down what the board now forbids.

## What this skill is for

Use it when someone wants to:

- build a board a photographer, illustrator, designer or image model can execute from with no conversation
- annotate references so each one is read on the attribute it was chosen for
- get out of the sameness trap, where the board looks like every other board in the category
- reduce a sprawling collection to a direction, and know what to cut
- audit a board that looks good and directs nothing, and say which of the two it is
- hand a direction onward to whoever turns it into rules or into a single-image brief

## Confidence

The rules here are practitioner accounts and reasoning from them rather than measured findings: nothing in this file rests on a tested comparison, a rejection rate, or a before-and-after on real client work. Rules marked **(thin)** rest on a single account, or carry a figure inherited rather than derived, and should be read as one person's house style that is cheap to try on one board rather than as settled craft.

## Terms, defined before they are used

Terms that several skills share have one meaning each, in `reference/glossary.md` at the plugin root. Where this section differs from that file, that file wins.

**Reference board, and how it differs from a moodboard.** A moodboard collects images that feel like the project and transmits a feeling. A reference board states, per image, the one attribute being taken from it and where in the thing being made that attribute lands, so it reads as an instruction rather than as a mood. The separating test is exclusion: a moodboard cannot reject a candidate piece of work, because it never said what it was asking for, and a reference board can, because every caption is a constraint. The two can hold the same twelve pictures and still be different objects. This skill builds the second and treats the first as raw material for it.

**Direction, and how it differs from style.** A style is a set of surface properties a piece has. A direction states which properties are required, which are permitted and which are forbidden for this project, written so a third party can be held to it. A style can be described; a direction can be failed.

**Art direction, and how it differs from design.** Art direction decides what the piece should be and judges whether what came back is it. Design makes the page: it arranges elements, sets the type, builds the grid, picks the palette. This file sits entirely on the direction side and never tells anyone where to put something on a canvas.

**Extraction, and attribute.** The extraction is the one quality you are taking from a reference, stated as a specific noun phrase. The attribute is the category that quality falls into: colour or colour combination, composition, texture, material, fabric, asset quality, photography treatment, image treatment, typographic mood, lighting, overall mood, or cultural reference. "Cyan against warm grey" is an extraction; colour is its attribute. Non-visual extractions use the same form: confidence, precision, restraint.

**The ask.** The one sentence naming what the board is asking for, written before any image is gathered and kept visible on the finished board. Without it the reduction step has no criterion.

**Exploration and refinement, and the two boards that go with them.** Exploration is the stage where the direction is still being found, where wrong answers are useful and duplication costs nothing; its artefact is the private board, a loose working surface. Refinement is the stage after a direction is agreed, where the only legitimate change is one that moves the work closer to it; its artefact is the shared board, reduced and annotated, which is what a client or maker sees.

**Cluster.** A named group of references on the shared board, with a note saying how the group applies to the ask, so a reader can tell which references make the same point.

**Anti-reference.** An image of the thing you are deliberately avoiding, placed on the board and labelled as forbidden. It converts a preference into an exclusion nobody has to infer.

**Prohibition.** A clause stating what the direction forbids, phrased so a finished piece either breaks it or does not: "never this blue against black", "no gradient behind type".

**Treatment.** A repeatable set of operations applied to every image in a set: a crop convention, a grade, a grain, a border. It counts as a treatment only when it is written down and applied to the good images too.

**Grade.** Recolouring an image: shifting its hue, contrast and tonal range. A grade moves every tonal value and moves nothing in the composition, so it cannot fix a reference being read on the wrong attribute. On a board it has one use, harmonising a reference that jars with the others.

**Crop.** Choosing a rectangle inside an image and discarding the rest. On a board a crop is an annotation device: cropping a reference down to the attribute you are taking removes the parts that would be read instead.

**Specification, and the two artefacts that carry one.** A specification is four things rather than one: reference, plus attribute taken, plus the location in the deliverable it governs, plus magnitude. "This metal, for the hands, flat rather than rounded, semi-matte" is a specification; "this metal, nice texture" is a caption. A **callout sheet** carries one by putting the reference and the thing being made on one page with arrows onto the governed detail. A **packet** is the post-concept artefact, one per executing discipline rather than one for the project, so a photographer's and a fabricator's share a direction and contain different pages.

**Latitude.** How much interpretation the maker is invited to supply. A rough sketch plus heavy reference invites a lot; a callout sheet invites almost none. The board's form sets it whether you chose it or not, so choose it.

**Maker.** Whoever makes the thing: a photographer, illustrator, designer, fabricator, image model or agent. Used throughout to mean the party who cannot read your mind.

## The principles

1. **Write the ask in words before gathering a single image**, because without a term to search the first images you find become the brief and every later reference is judged against them instead of against the project. Name the subject, the feeling, and the one quality the piece must have. Checked by the ask test.

2. **Every inclusion carries a named extraction, or it comes off**, because a board constrains by telling people what to do, so a reference nobody can account for widens the range of work the board permits instead of narrowing it. "It looks good" leaves nothing to take and nothing for the maker to do. Checked by the extraction test.

3. **Name the attribute you are taking, not the image you like.** The mechanism is recombination: specific extractions combine into something that copies none of them, where at whole-image level there is nothing to combine and the only move left is duplication. An extraction that will not reduce to one of the attribute categories above is decoration. Checked by the bulldozer test.

4. **Keep two boards and never let the private one be seen**, because a viewer shown the working surface reacts to material you had already discarded, after which you are defending references you do not want. Checked by the two-board test.

5. **Reduce until every surviving reference has a nameable extraction, then reduce again by cluster.** The mechanism is dilution: each unaccounted image adds a direction the board permits, so more references mean less clarity rather than more. The trap is volume creep, pulling from everywhere until you hold dozens of references and no memory of what you took from any. Checked by the extraction and cluster tests.

6. **State the prohibitions the board implies, as clauses**, because a maker will not infer an exclusion and an implicit rule produces work that is defensible against the board and wrong against the direction. Generate them with an elimination pass: put candidate colours, type and treatments against the board one at a time and write down each rejection. Checked by the prohibition count.

7. **Annotate per image, anchored to the part of the deliverable the attribute governs**, because a bare reference is read on an arbitrary attribute and a caption naming only the attribute still leaves the maker guessing where it applies. Top-line, not an essay. Checked by the bulldozer test and the specification ladder.

8. **Make sure the maker can plan and resource the work from the board alone.** A direction has a logistics tail: a photographer needs to know what gear to bring and what to book, a fabricator needs the material, a model needs the attribute and its magnitude. A board nobody can plan from produces improvisation on the day. Checked by the maker-planning test.

9. **Rank by area: the references most central to the direction take the most space.** **(thin)** The mechanism is geometric. Area scales with the square of linear size, so a reference at twice the width of its neighbours holds four times the area and reads as rank, while one at 1.3 times the width holds 1.3 squared, about 1.7 times, and reads as an accident. Make the dominance obvious or do not attempt it. Checked by rank against area.

10. **Include a retrievable source for every reference.** **(thin)** The reason is generative rather than legal: it lets the maker go deeper in the same vein, and lets you chase a reference back when you need a second one like it.

11. **Give textures and materials their own cluster.** Boards drift toward composition, colour and type, because those are what the category's output shows, and surface is the attribute that most often decides whether a finished piece reads as expensive. A named cluster makes its absence visible.

## How to source so the board is not the category average

The sameness trap has a specific shape: two people on the same project research separately and come back with the same references, not similar ones, from the same corner of the same platform. The failure sits in the input rather than the execution, so no amount of craft downstream recovers it.

**Work in three circles, and know which one you are in.** The first circle is principles that are always true for your work. The second is category research, meaning what competitors and the obvious platforms are already doing. The third is out-of-category material plus your own archive. Original work sits in the overlap, and only the third circle cannot be reproduced by a competitor, because it does not come from the category at all. **(thin)**

**Two diagnoses of the trap, with different fixes. First, the feed is a convergence machine.** You are shown what others in your taste bracket are shown, so the starting point for everyone working on a similar project is close to identical. The fix is not to avoid the tool: it is excellent for the second circle, which is work you have to do anyway in order to know what to avoid. It is a hazard only when it is also doing your third circle.

**Second, what you find is an end state.** A search returns somebody's final answer with the working thrown away. You cannot source the trial and error that produced it, so copying it reproduces the result without the reasoning, and the reasoning is the part that would have transferred.

**Run the adjacent leap**, which means searching for the quality underneath the category instead of for the category. Strip the design away from the ask and name the abstract noun: speed, warmth, permanence, cheapness, care. Search that noun in a field obsessed with it and uninterested in your subject, so a fast delivery brand goes to pit crews and sprint starts rather than to rival delivery brands. **(thin)**

**Then go to the source, not to another designer's reading of it:** brutalist architecture rather than brutalist graphic design. The argument is generation loss, the degradation of copying a copy. Another designer's brutalist poster is already one interpretation away from the buildings, so referencing the poster gives you their reading plus yours.

**Build the third circle on purpose.** Interrogate your archive by asking why you kept a thing: the answer is the principle worth reapplying, not the object. If a space-agency diagram is doing something for your project it is probably not the rocket, it is the precision and the restraint. Write three specific reasons at the moment something stops you, because at board time you will reconstruct a reason that fits the board you have already built. **(thin)**

**Three further moves, worth keeping because they work differently from the leap.** Category avoidance as an input, meaning you build the board partly in order not to resemble the category when the positioning requires it, which produces anti-references directly. Differentiation at production rather than at reference level: a deliberately conventional setup plus one funded anomaly, one rented object, location or material nobody in the category has used, where the appeal is that the cost is explicit. **(thin)** And the anti-reference itself, which is the most executable of the three because it turns taste into something a maker can check against.

**When you are empty, stop scrolling.** Refilling from the thing that emptied you returns you to the second circle.

## Annotating so the reference is read on the attribute you meant

An unannotated reference will be read on the wrong attribute, and the cost is the idea rather than the image. The failure has two forms.

**The maker takes the wrong thing.** A photograph of a bulldozer handed to a modeller for a robot design, with no caption. The modeller cannot tell whether to notice the size, the weathering, or the way the paint sits on the metal, so they take one of the three and it is not the one you meant. Nothing about an image announces which of its properties is load-bearing.

**The viewer kills the idea.** On the client side the same gap costs the direction rather than the detail. The client reacts badly to something that was not the part you were referencing, you end up defending an image you did not care about, and from behind that position the idea usually dies.

**The form that fixes both: annotate per image, and anchor each annotation to the part of the deliverable it governs.** That is the callout sheet, and it answers the two questions a reader has, what am I looking at and where does it land. It also closes the objection that judging a reference in a vacuum is a beginner error: a caption saying where the attribute applies is not a judgment on the image in isolation. Write top-line, because a reader parsing your caption is not looking at your reference.

**Keep both units.** A cluster note states intent for a group and constrains nothing; a per-image annotation constrains one thing. Cluster notes alone tell the maker what you hope for, and captions alone tell them twelve unrelated facts.

## The ladder from caption to specification

Boards reach different levels of executability, and the rung you are on tells you what will come back. Strongest first:

1. **Specification.** Reference plus attribute plus location plus magnitude, on a callout sheet. For one robot: hydraulics at a named joint, two metal references for the elbow, flat rather than rounded metal for the hands, a base of semi-matte painted military metal, surface dirt with its degree, the rule that heavier dirt concentrates lower down, and a desirable defect, meaning a paint bleed that is wanted rather than tolerated. Latitude near zero. **(thin)**
2. **Per-component instruction.** A packet giving reference plus instruction per part, no shared context assumed, colour expressed as prohibitions: never this blue with black, never straight white, this black only with the cream. **(thin)**
3. **A named direction with a written definition.** One sentence a third party can be held to, plus a short gloss. It survives the meeting and can be quoted back.
4. **Per-image captions.** The attribute is named, the location is not, so the maker decides where it goes.
5. **Per-group notes.** Intent stated, nothing constrained.
6. **Bare images.** Whatever comes back is as likely to be right as not.

**Two cautions when importing the top rung.** A specification presumes the concept exists, so it cannot replace the exploratory half of boarding: you cannot specify a direction you have not found. And it has no client in it, so the approval layer is absent. Take the precision from the top of the ladder and keep the sign-off and the cost conversation from the board stage.

**Prohibitions are the part a board of liked images structurally cannot express:** a collection can say what it wants and has no vocabulary for what it refuses. A board that has produced none is diagnosed, not unfinished.

## Directing an image model or an agent

Nothing in this skill's basis addresses image models: every account assumes a huma maker who can ask what you meant. The material below is constructed by transfer from the human-maker-at-a-distance case and is marked thin for that reason. **(thin)**

An image model is a maker with no project context, no memory of the brief, and no ability to ask a question. That is the constraint a fabricator working from a packet has, with one difference: the fabricator emails you when a colour cannot be matched, and the model produces something and presents it as an answer.

- **The packet is the right shape, not the board.** Reference plus per-component instruction, no shared context assumed, and the verdict delivered by whether what comes back is right. An unannotated reference handed to a model is read on an arbitrary attribute, exactly as a human reads it, except that nothing surfaces the ambiguity.
- **Specify the outcome, not the method.** Describe the visual result and leave the technique to the maker. A described outcome can be measured against; a described process cannot.
- **Prohibitions carry more weight here than anywhere else.** A model will not infer an exclusion and has no professional instinct to violate, so every unstated rule is a coin flip.
- **Prefer sourced references over generated ones on any board you intend to keep.** A generated image has no retrievable source, so it cannot be chased back to whatever it was imitating. That breaks principle 10, and the break is invisible later, when the board is the only record of why.

## How to work

### Mode A: building a board

1. **Write the ask in one sentence:** subject, feeling, and the one quality the piece must have.
2. **Decide which artefact you are making and the latitude that goes with it,** exploratory board, packet or callout sheet, and write the choice down.
3. **Do the second circle first and label it category research.** Use the algorithmic feed here, where it is a retrieval tool rather than a hazard.
4. **Name the quality underneath the ask and run the adjacent leap on it:** one search term, outside the category, at the source.
5. **Pull from your archive**, which is the third circle.
6. **Gather wide on the private board**, where duplication and mess are correct.
7. **Write three specific reasons per reference as you capture it**, not later. **(thin)**
8. **Reduce.** Cut every reference whose extraction you cannot name in a noun phrase.
9. **Group the survivors into named clusters** with a note per cluster, and reduce again inside any cluster making the same point twice.
10. **Check textures and materials are present as their own cluster**, and add them if not.
11. **Add the anti-references**, labelled as what is being avoided.
12. **Annotate per image, anchored to the part of the deliverable it governs**, with the source for each.
13. **Name the direction in one sentence with a short written definition.**
14. **Run the elimination pass and write down the prohibitions it produces.** Put candidate colours, type and treatments against the board one at a time; every rejection is a clause.
15. **Rank by area**, with the dominance obvious rather than marginal, per principle 9.
16. **Technical pass before anyone sees it:** no pixelated references, no accidental crops, no unintended tonal outlier, one treatment across anything you have graded.
17. **Price the direction** on the board: materials, named photographers, locations and effects all commit somebody to spending.
18. **Run the tests table in order.**

### Mode B: auditing a board

1. **State what the board appears to direct, cold, in one sentence, before reading a single caption.** If you cannot, that is the first finding, and it is the only step that is spoiled by doing the others first.
2. **Run the tests table in order** and stop at the first failure that makes the rest moot. The table runs cheap and decisive before expensive and arguable, so a collection never consumes a full specification review.
3. **Report** in the order given under "Writing the output".

## Tests

Run in order. Each verdict is decidable from this file alone.

| Test | Catches | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Ask** | A board with no stated target | Read the ask off the board. Fail if there is no single sentence naming subject, feeling and required quality, and stop the review there. |
| **2. Extraction** | Referencing by attraction | For each reference, name the one quality taken, as a specific noun phrase mapping to one attribute category above. Fail on "it looks good", on a paragraph, and on any reference with no caption. The failures are the cut list. |
| **3. Three reasons** | References kept on one thin reason | Count the specific named reasons per surviving reference. Fail below three. Harsher than test 2, and applied at capture time rather than at board time. **(thin)** |
| **4. Bulldozer** | Annotation that does not disambiguate | Point at one reference and ask someone who did not build the board what they are supposed to notice. Fail if their answer names a different attribute than your caption does. |
| **5. Two-board** | Exploration material shown as direction | Count uncaptioned references and duplicates of the same idea. Fail if either is present: that is the private board. |
| **6. Cluster** | A pile posing as a structure | Count named clusters and their notes. Fail if any reference sits outside a cluster, or any cluster has no note saying how it applies to the ask. |
| **7. Prohibition count** | A collection with captions | Count the written prohibitions. Fail at zero. At one or two, name what the elimination pass did not cover. |
| **8. Wrong-candidate** | A board that cannot refuse anything | Put a deliberately wrong option against the board, set in a type or colour you know is off, and ask a third party whether it belongs. Fail if they say it could. |
| **9. Blur** | Outliers that will derail the conversation | Apply an actual blur, not a squint, so the result is shareable. List what still jumps out and mark each item intended hero or mistake. Fail on any mistake, and fail if you cannot say which it is. |
| **10. Rank against area** | Hierarchy asserted but not built | List references by importance, then by area. Fail if the orders differ, or if the top reference is under twice the linear width of the next tier, since 1.3 times the width is 1.7 times the area and will not read as rank. **(thin)** |
| **11. Maker-planning** | A board that cannot be worked from | Hand the board to whoever will execute and ask what they will bring, book, buy or set up: the gear and location list from a photographer, the material from a fabricator, the prompt from an image model's operator. Fail on a vague answer, or on two people writing substantially different prompts from it. |
| **12. Ten seconds, no context** | A board nobody can read | Show the board for ten seconds to someone outside the project, remove it, and ask what the project is and what the finished piece will be. Fail if the answer does not match the ask. Void if that person built the board, so delegate it. The interval is a fixed figure chosen here with no measurement behind it. **(thin)** |
| **13. Specification ladder** | A board weaker than its job needs | Name the rung, 1 to 6. Fail if it is weaker than the latitude written down at Mode A step 2. |
| **14. Affordability** | A direction nobody has funded | List every reference implying a cost: material, location, named photographer, effect, rented object. Fail if any is unpriced on the board. |
| **15. Vendor** | Everything the other tests miss | A third party with no context executes from the board alone and the artefact comes back right or wrong. Slow, expensive, unambiguous, and the only test here with a hard external verdict. Fail if you had to explain the board first, because the explanation is the part that does not survive a handoff, a holiday or an agent. |

## Anti-patterns

- **Collection masquerading as direction.** References that do not reference anything. *Fix:* run test 2 and rebuild from the survivors.
- **Referencing by attraction.** Adding what you like. *Fix:* the extraction must be a noun phrase before the image goes on.
- **Volume creep.** Dozens of references, no memory of what you took from any. *Fix:* Mode A steps 8 and 9.
- **Showing the private board.** Fails test 5. *Fix:* make the second board rather than tidying the first.
- **The vibes-only board**, which gets agreement on nothing, and **the tactics-only board**, correct constraints with no feeling to aim at, so the work is compliant and dead. *Fix:* the ask sentence plus one mood cluster, and captions plus prohibitions, respectively.
- **Role collision.** A direction board drifting into element-level design decisions, or a design board drifting up into direction. Both end with people pulling different ways because nobody can tell which decisions are settled. *Fix:* name the altitude and hand the lower one to the design skills below.
- **Over-explanation.** Heavy vocabulary pulling attention onto the caption. *Fix:* one clause per reference.
- **Duplication.** Lifting part of a reference straight into the work. That is tracing, and it is what happens when the extraction was never named.
- **Judging in a vacuum.** Rejecting a reference on an attribute you were not borrowing. *Fix:* anchored captions, which state what is being judged.
- **Scrapbook death.** An archive of good references that never becomes a board. *Fix:* the ask sentence, which gives the archive something to be searched against.
- **The unfunded direction**, failing test 14, and **generated references on a board you intend to keep**, failing principle 10 because they cannot be chased back.

## Applied

### Auditing a board that looks good: 47 references, one page

**Before.** A dense page of 47 images for a premium food brand. Harmonised, pleasant, no captions except five cluster titles. The brief is a packaging and launch campaign direction.

**Diagnosis, computed.**

- **Test 1 fails.** No ask on the page. Reconstructing one from the client email gives "premium but not cold", which is two feelings and no required quality.
- **Test 2.** Extraction nameable on 18 of 47 references, so the cut list is 47 minus 18, which is 29 images, 62 percent of the board. That is the headline finding: the board permits far more work than its author intends.
- **Test 3.** Of the 18 survivors, 11 carry three reasons and 7 carry one. Three across 18 survivors is 54 reasons required against 40 present, so a quarter of the surviving reasoning is missing.
- **Test 6 fails.** Five cluster titles, none with a note, and 12 references sit outside all five.
- **Test 7 fails at zero.** No elimination pass was run, so nothing on this page can reject a candidate.
- **Test 10 fails.** The intended hero is 1.3 times the width of the second tier, so by the square it occupies about 1.69 times the area and reads as a layout accident. Twice the width would give four times the area.
- **Test 11 fails.** Asked what they would book from this board, the photographer says a studio and a stylist, which is what they would have said without it.

**Verdict:** a collection with cluster titles.

**After.** The repair is not a redesign of the page.

1. Write the ask: "Premium, warm, hand-made at scale. The required quality is visible human handling."
2. Keep the 18 survivors and caption each with attribute plus location: "hard side light, pack photography only, not the lifestyle frames".
3. Run the elimination pass against the 18. It produces four prohibitions: never a gradient behind type, never the category's chalkboard serif, never straight white on the pack, never a styled hand with no visible work on it.
4. Rebuild rank with the hero at twice the width of the second tier, so four times the area.
5. Re-run tests 8 and 11. The wrong-candidate test now passes, because the chalkboard serif is forbidden in writing and a third party can see it is excluded.

The board is now rung 4, per-image captions with locations, and the audit says that rather than calling it a direction.

### Building a board for a client carousel direction

The ask: "Fast delivery that feels precise rather than frantic. The required quality is controlled speed."

1. **Circles.** Twenty minutes of rival delivery brands on the private board, labelled as things to avoid. Then the leap: controlled speed sends the search to pit crews, sprint starts and surgical instrument trays, taken from photographs of actual pit stops rather than from motorsport-inspired graphic design. Then two archive images, both kept for a single bright accent against near-neutral grey, so that reason becomes a cluster rather than two images.
2. **Clusters, with notes.** Colour, one bright accent against neutral. Composition, hands in motion with the object still. Treatment, hard light with no softening. Texture, brushed metal and matte card. Anti-references, three frames of frantic delivery imagery labelled forbidden.
3. **Annotations anchored to the deliverable:** "hands in motion with the object still, cover slide only"; "brushed metal, the backgrounds of the evidence slides in the middle of the carousel, never the cover". That anchoring is what makes the board most of a prompt already.
4. **Prohibitions from the elimination pass.** No motion blur on the product. No more than one accent colour per slide. No stock smiling courier. No gradient behind type.
5. **Price it on the board.** The hard-light cluster commits someone to a photographer with strobes rather than available light.

**If the board is delivered as post-sized slides, its captions have to clear the type floor.** The figures come from `reference/canvas-assumptions.md` in the design plugin, which fixes the canvases at 1080 x 1080 and 1080 x 1350 and sets the downscale factor, meaning the ratio between a working-file pixel and what a viewer actually sees, at a nominal 3x with a 4x stress case. A caption at 24px renders at 24 / 3 = 8pt nominal and cannot be read at all, which is how an annotated board arrives looking unannotated. At 48px it renders at 48 / 3 = 16pt nominal and 48 / 4 = 12pt under the stress case, so it survives both. Use the stress-case floor, because a board gets read on a phone in a reply thread more often than on the desk it was built on.

### The same board, directing an image model

**Before.** The board is pasted into a prompt wholesale: five clusters, no locations, no prohibitions. Returned candidates are motion-blurred couriers, which is the category average, because the category average is what an unconstrained model produces.

**Diagnosis.** Rung 5 against a job needing rung 2, so test 13 fails, and test 11 fails in its model form: two people write different prompts from this board.

**After.** One page per slide archetype, each carrying reference plus attribute plus location plus magnitude, with all four prohibitions restated on every page, because a model carries nothing between pages. Outcome rather than method: "hard side light, single source, hard-edged shadow" rather than a lighting setup. Check returned candidates against the prohibitions first, since those are mechanical, before anybody argues about the picture.

## Where this skill should lose

**When the direction has to become rules a maker can be failed against.** Use `ad-rules`, which turns a direction into clauses with thresholds and prohibitions. The handoff is explicit: its input is this file's output, so a board with no prohibitions gives it nothing to work with. Close this file when the question stops being what the direction is and becomes what counts as breaking it.

**When one image has to be specified before it exists.** Use `ad-brief`, which specifies a single picture with a shot list, acceptance criteria and approval gates. A board directs a body of work. The two look similar at rung 1 of the specification ladder, and the test for which you need is whether you are describing a set or one frame. One frame is the other file. The chain runs board, then rules, then brief, then critique.

**When work has come back and needs judging.** Use `ad-critique`. Nothing in this file audits a finished piece against a board: it builds the direction and does not verify against it.

**When the question is which picture, how it is cropped, and what it gives up so the type can be read.** Use `dsn-image` in the design plugin, which owns choosing and judging a photograph in a layout, rescuing a poor supplied one, setting a claim over it, and building a moodboard as a design-level document. The split is altitude: this file decides what the whole body of work asks for and forbids, and that one decides which photograph goes in a specific slot and what happens to it there. Close this file and open that one as soon as the subject is one image on one canvas. The dependency runs one way, so do not expect a pointer back.

**When a colour reference has to become an actual palette.** Use `dsn-palette` to construct and test the set, and `dsn-color` for what a colour is doing in a frame. This file's colour cluster names an extraction, "one bright accent against neutral", which is a constraint rather than a palette. Deriving a palette out of a photograph has no method in either plugin, so sample candidates by eye and then test them with the palette skill's build procedure: the sampling is guesswork and the testing is not.

**When a typographic-mood reference has to become a typeface.** Use `dsn-typeface`. A board may say "bold, editorial, slightly condensed" and stop, and that is the deepest type direction this file supports. Weights, metrics, pairing logic and scale are the design skill's.

**When legibility or accessibility is the question.** Use the design skill that measures contrast against the published accessibility specification, `dsn-contrast`. Legibility as a board input is absent from this skill's basis entirely, so a board can pass everything here and still produce unreadable work. If the direction constrains colour, run that check before the direction is approved rather than after a piece fails it.

## What an agent can run, and what it cannot

**Can run, mechanically:** tests 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 13 and 14, plus the cut-list and squared-area arithmetic, flagging generated or unsourced references, and describing what a board appears to direct, which is a genuine no-context proxy as long as it has not been shown the ask.

**Cannot run:** tests 4, 12 and 15, because the first two need a naive human and an agent that has read the captions cannot be surprised by them, and the third needs a real maker. Also: whether the adjacent leap left the category, which needs knowledge of what the category is producing this month; whether an extraction is worth taking, since nameability is mechanical and being worth naming is editorial; and whether a reference was taken or copied, because nothing here locates that line. Delegate tests 4 and 12 rather than skipping them, since both are void when run by whoever built the board.

**Gaps in this skill's coverage:** no procedure for revising a board mid-project, versioning it, or deciding whether the board or the work is wrong when they diverge; nothing on being overruled after a board is approved; no reference count for a shared board, because the caption is the limit rather than a number; nothing on rights, licensing or credit beyond the retrieval reason for sources; nothing on boarding motion, sound, copy tone or interaction behaviour; nothing on boarding inside an existing brand system, where the job is protecting equity rather than finding a direction; no procedure for stakeholders who disagree; and no account of when not to make a board at all.

## Writing the output

Plain words, full sentences. Name the actual reference, the measurement and the location rather than an abstraction: "29 of 47 references have no nameable extraction, and 12 of them sit outside every cluster", not "the board lacks focus".

Report an audit in this order:

1. What the board appears to direct, stated cold in one sentence, written before any caption was read.
2. References whose extraction cannot be named, listed, with the cut-list count and its share of the board.
3. The prohibitions the board implies, or the statement that it implies none.
4. Blur-test outliers, each marked intended or mistake.
5. Whether rank matches area, with the area arithmetic shown.
6. What the maker could not plan from this board, in their own words where possible.
7. The rung on the specification ladder, against the rung the job needs.
8. One verdict: a direction, a direction with named gaps, or a collection.

Name the tier each finding comes from. A missing caption, a zero prohibition count or a failed area ratio is mechanical and settles the matter. A reading, such as whether the adjacent leap went far enough, is offered as a reading with the fix as an option. Say explicitly when a recommendation rests on a rule marked **(thin)**, and say when a move is cheap to try on one board, because one real handoff outweighs everything in this file.

Sources, corroboration, the disagreements between accounts, the claims that did not survive scrutiny, and the full list of what no source covers are in `references/provenance.md`.
