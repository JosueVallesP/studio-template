## The parent hypothesis, assessed

The hypothesis taken into mining was that a board is an argument in which every image is a sentence, tested by whether you can feel the project in under ten seconds, with volume as the named failure. It is **verbatim accurate to A1, and all three components come from that one video.**

| Component | Channels | Status |
|---|---|---|
| A board is an argument or a decision, not inspiration | A1, B2 | Two channels, and they mean different things |
| Every image is a sentence, individually justifiable | A1 only | **House style, and actively contradicted by B** |
| Feel the project in under ten seconds | A1 only | House style, **and not observable as stated** |
| More references means less clarity | A1, B1, B2, D | **Craft** |

**"Argument" means two different things.** A1 means an argument addressed outward, where the board persuades a client and each image is a premise. B2 means an argument conducted inward, where the board is where you fight with yourself until only the defensible survives. Both land on reasoning per inclusion and willingness to cut. The outward sense sets the presentation bar; the inward sense sets the editing bar. Both are kept.

**What the hypothesis missed:** it is framed entirely around persuading a viewer. Two channels frame the board instead around enabling an executor, which is a different and more testable standard, and for directing an image model it is the more load-bearing one.

**Two incompatible ideas of what a board is for.** A and B treat it as a persuasion device that protects a direction through approval. C and E treat it as a specification an executor works from, with the verdict delivered by whether the returned artefact is right. The skill takes the second as load-bearing for directing a designer, photographer or image model, and keeps the approval machinery from the first. The design-focused sources cover the second framing least.

## Material stripped from the body in the legibility pass

- **The Comic Sans demonstration (B2).** Type set in Comic Sans against the board obviously does not belong, not because it is bad but because it is not in the same league. This is the clearest demonstration in the corpus of a board rejecting a candidate, and it is the origin of the skill's wrong-candidate test. The same channel frames the board as a gate whose job is to close off ideas.
- **The generation-loss argument (A2).** Stated in the corpus as going to brutalist architecture rather than to other brutalist graphic design, because another designer's interpretation is already one step removed from the source. The skill keeps the argument and names it generation loss, which is the skill's label, not the source's.
- **The funded-anomaly example (C).** A rented object at around five hundred dollars that nobody in the category had used. The move is kept; the attached virality claim is refused below.
- **The remove-the-image test (A2).** Cover each reference and write one sentence on why it worked; failing that, you do not understand it well enough to use it and will copy the surface instead of the substance. Folded out of the skill's tests table because the extraction and three-reasons tests catch the same failure with decidable verdicts, and because the claim attached to it is the weakest reasoning in the corpus, recorded under claims refused below.
- **Counts and channel attributions.** Every corroboration figure that was previously inline in the body now lives only in the tables above.
- **Constructed material, labelled as such in the body.** The image-model section, the ten-second test repair, the specification ladder's six rungs, the squared-area arithmetic for rank, and the caption-legibility arithmetic. The last of those is the only place the skill quotes a pixel figure, and it cites the design plugin's `reference/canvas-assumptions.md` for the canvas sizes and the two type floors rather than asserting its own.

## Claims refused

- **"Mood boarding is an art and a science."** No science appears in twenty-four minutes.
- **Tenure as argument.** Three separate channels open by citing fifteen or twenty years of experience in place of a mechanism.
- **"One intentional thing can set anything apart."** Folklore: no mechanism, no counter-example, no failed case.
- **"It will be inherently more viral."** A causal claim about an unreleased piece of content. The production spend is real; the virality is prophecy.
- **"Guaranteed" to make a shoot go better.** The underlying mechanic is sound; the word is not.
- **Image size works psychologically to command attention.** The *practice* of ranking by area is a usable editorial mechanic and is kept. The psychological claim behind it is asserted.
- **Physical objects carry memory a screenshot cannot.** Asserted, no mechanism. Kept separately from the same source's *physical fidelity* argument, which is mechanistic and defensible: print under real light differs from a backlit screen, and a screen will not tell you how large a graphic must be on a garment or which print process a texture needs.
- **Generated imagery is "the average of the average."** The one position any source takes on AI, offered with no mechanism and **explicitly deferred** by its own author to a future video.
- **Faster approval later.** Asserted as the board's payoff with nothing behind it.
- **An unsourced headcount at a well-known brand**, cited to establish that a specialist colour role exists and deployed in support of a colour-consulting service.
- **"Because I kept it, it works."** The weakest reasoning in the corpus: the author reports that he held on to something, treats having kept it as proof the technique works, and extends it with the claim that other people are likely to react as he did. n=1, with the sample being the person making the argument, generalised to an audience.

**Every outcome claim in this corpus is an assertion by a practitioner selling the method.** No source presents a before-and-after, a rejection rate, a revision count, or a post-mortem on a board that failed.

## Commercial interest

Four of five channels sell education or products. C's video is substantially a walkthrough of specific tools; B2's sponsor sells AI tooling, which is commerce rather than a position on AI. B also recommends a specific application while disclosing a partnership with its maker.

**And A's case is sharper than ordinary sponsorship, because the sponsor read is placed where it does the argumentative work.** Both of A's videos site the read at the point where the argument needs support, and A2 says so in as many words: that this is where the sponsor fits in properly, and that it is the same principle he has just been teaching. **A claim whose supporting example is a paid placement is not a claim.** The surrounding material is still the richest in the corpus; it just cannot be read as evidence at those junctures.

E has no commercial content, is also the only source producing a true specification, and is the most precise source on annotation, which is reason to weight a ten-minute video above several longer ones. The same inverse relationship between commercial load and rule quality found in `ad-rules`.

## Gaps: what no source covers

1. **Checking finished work against the board.** No verification procedure anywhere. The closest are a colour board retained as a reusable reference tool, and a demonstration of a board rejecting a wrong type choice. Neither is a pass over a finished piece. **The corpus builds the direction and does not audit against it**; that half of the task is constructed, and `ad-critique` and `ad-rules` carry it.
2. **Revising a board mid-project.** Nothing on divergence, versioning, re-approval, or deciding whether the board or the work is wrong.
3. **What to do when a client approves a board then asks for off-direction work.** The gate is defined; being overruled is not addressed.
4. **A count for the shared board.** Direction only, fewer rather than hundreds, never a number. The only figure is fifty, cited as a trap.
5. **Rights, licensing and credit.** One source requires URLs for retrieval rather than attribution. Nobody asks what putting a living designer's or named photographer's work on a client board creates, legally or in expectation, though one source notes it creates a financial obligation. One source cheerfully observes that a particular designer's work appears on eight of ten boards he has worked on, without comment.
6. **Directing an image model.** Total absence across five channels. The skill's section on it is labelled as construction from transferable parts.
7. **Boarding anything non-visual.** No motion timing, no sound, no copy tone, no interaction behaviour. The motion designer in the corpus boards only stills.
8. **Typography beyond vibe.** The deepest type direction in the corpus is a preference for bold, possibly serif, somewhat editorial. No weights, metrics, pairing logic or scale. One source has a typography board and never opens it.
9. **Legibility, contrast and accessibility as board inputs.** Absent entirely.
10. **Boarding for a brand that already has a system.** Every design example is a new identity or a campaign. Nothing on refresh, on protecting existing equity, or on boarding within established rules.
11. **Multiple stakeholders who disagree.** One source's elimination session assumes a single voice; another names the condition that everyone must pass the gate without supplying a procedure.
12. **Time and cost of building a board.** One figure in the corpus.
13. **When not to make a board.** Universally assumed necessary. No source names a project where a board was the wrong move.

## The annotation vocabulary

Consolidated from A1 across three statements, and the single most reusable artefact in the corpus: colour and colour combination, composition, texture, material, fabric, asset quality, photography treatment, image treatment, typographic mood, lighting, mood, cultural reference.

Non-visual extractables added by A2: confidence, precision, restraint.

E's specification dimensions, which go past attribute to constraint: base material, per-part material, a geometry qualifier such as rounded against flat, surface condition and its degree, the spatial distribution of that condition, and desirable defects.

## Known weaknesses

- **The sameness material, which is the most valuable section, is one channel.** Its out-of-category instruction and its extract-the-quality instruction are corroborated; its algorithm theory, its archive theory and its three-circle structure are not.
- **The ten-second test needed repairing to be a test at all.** The repair is this skill's.
- **The strongest specification in the corpus comes from VFX**, where the executor is a modeller inside a pipeline. The transfer is argued rather than demonstrated.
- **Two channels put unsourced generated images on client boards**, which collides with the one provenance rule in the corpus, and no source notices.
- **Nothing here has been run on live client work.**
