### What this corpus can and cannot establish

**There is no majority to be had.** With three channels, nothing reaches three-channel agreement unless all three agree. Exactly one item arguably does, and the three mean different things by it.

**Channel A's six videos are one voice, not six.** They repeat the same toolkit vocabulary across videos, which reads as corroboration and is not. Marked as internal repetition wherever it occurs, never as agreement.

**Channel B's two videos share one sponsor and one format**, so their process agreement is weaker than two independent demonstrations would be: part of the agreed sequence is the sponsor's workflow.

**Channel C is n=1** and carries most of the method. It is the only source primarily about making an instruction another person can execute, the only one from outside brand and marketing, the only one with no commercial content, and the only one proposing a test an image can fail.

**Four of the five passages this skill is built on come from channel C.** That is the honest state of the corpus. The six videos that supply a toolkit instead of a rule set supply correspondingly little.

### A pattern worth stating

The two videos with the least commercial content contain the corpus's two best-formed rules, the greyscale test and the camera spec. The two with the heaviest sponsor segments contain the most unevidenced assertion per minute. Commercial load and rule quality run inversely across this corpus.

## Corroboration

**TWO CHANNELS, the best available in this corpus**

| Claim | Channels |
|---|---|
| Write the direction as a list and gate every artefact against it | B, C. Both state the artefact, its derivation and its use, from different fields. **A does not count here**, since it produces a menu to pick from, which is a different object |
| Sample values from a source; do not guess them | B, C. Arrived at independently and for different reasons: B to tie colour to an aspirational set, C because colour perception is demonstrably unreliable |
| Annotate each reference with the specific attribute wanted from it | B, C. A treats a reference as raw material to be transformed rather than as a source of named attributes, so A is partial at best |
| The direction must start from a stated problem, not a look | B, C, with B carrying it. A starts from references and devices and is explicit about it |
| A brief needs a fixed field list | A, B, but different artefacts under one name. A's fields are logistical; B's are substantive |
| Luxury reads as restraint, space and absence of logo | A, B, **both luxury-framed, both reached by interpreting other people's finished work.** A convergent reading, not evidence |

**THE ONLY ARGUABLE THREE-CHANNEL ITEM**, and the three mean different things: coherence comes from one treatment repeated across every asset. A means a recognisable motif across channels for recall; B means one texture pass for set cohesion; C means asset-to-list conformance. Recorded as a convergence, not a shared mechanism.

One other three-channel item is near-vacuous: ground the direction in real-world reference. Nobody disagrees and the claim excludes nothing.

### Rules the skill marks (thin), and why

Every item in the single-source lists above that survives into `SKILL.md` carries the marker. Two additional markers are not single-source but inherited rather than derived:

- **The four-to-eight clause count.** Both bounds come from one well-formed instance of six clauses. The shape of the constraint is reasoned in the skill body; the numbers are not.
- **The 51-point luma separation floor** in the worked example. No source supplies a separation figure of any kind. The skill states the arithmetic, which is rerunnable, and marks the chosen floor as house style.

### What the skill's own subject owes to construction

Two sources produce a rule artefact; neither explains the grammar, how to phrase a clause, how to choose a threshold, or how many clauses are enough. The six clause forms in `SKILL.md` were reverse-engineered from well-formed instances. That is construction from evidence rather than received craft, and the confidence line in the body says so.

### The one natural experiment, recorded here rather than in the body

Channel B's two brand builds share the same maker, sponsor, format and runtime. One writes a six-clause rulebook halfway through; the other never writes one. Every unreasoned decision in the pair lands in the build with no rulebook: a material included because the category always includes it with the reason replaced by a rhetorical question (`dpdVukWZCho 00:17:34`); a mark placement left open because the criterion could not be articulated (same timestamp); a palette changed and a logo dropped mid-build with no criterion and no record of what they replaced (`dpdVukWZCho 00:04:30`). The build with the rulebook has arguments in those places instead, because the clauses give it something to argue against.

**This is the strongest available support for the skill's premise, and it is n=1 on a natural experiment nobody designed.** Suggestive, not proven. What it suggests is that the rulebook's value is not better decisions but visible unreasoned ones.

## Disagreement worth keeping

**Consistency versus clarity, a direct conflict about which wins.** C subordinates consistency explicitly and twice, and accepts visible inconsistency in a studied example where background outlines are simply dropped. B makes coherence the goal and runs a consistency pass over the whole set, looking for anything that stands out. Both are coherent for their medium: C's work must stay legible during play, B's must read as one campaign. The skill carries C's ordering because it is the one with a test attached, and notes B's pass as the set-level operation it is.

## Claims refused

- **"Inherently sculptural and luxurious without a logo present."** *Inherently* carries the whole argument. The type specimen for the unfalsifiable clause.
- **Process and craft content shifts price acceptance.** Asserted repeatedly in one channel with no evidence, and it is also the premise of the content business making the claim.
- **A four-part model of luxury loyalty.** No source, self-cited.
- **"Make it viral"** as an instruction.
- **National-character claims** standing in for rules: whimsy as the heart of one country's luxury.
- **Restraint is "exactly why it reads as expensive"** (`LmGJ5A0vFV0 00:11:06`). A causal claim about audiences drawn from one brand. Keep the instruction, discard the explanation.
- **"Space feels expensive, clutter feels cheap."** Dual status: an excellent testable rule as "leave this much space", an unsupported claim as a statement about what people feel.
- **A premium brand "flipped it somehow."** The source concedes the mechanism is unknown one sentence before using it as a premise.
- **A playbook called impeccable in a video that then concludes the brand has fallen short on its own stated non-negotiable.** A self-cancellation inside one video, and the second of two in this channel; the other concedes that the method it has just taught is inimitable without the specific people at the centre of it. **Both cancel the lesson they were teaching, and in both cases the concession arrives after the playbook has been sold.**
- **Folk neuroscience about attention.** Give people something that does not make sense in their brains; unexpectedness immediately engages the viewer's mind; a visceral connection to seeing everything iced over. No evidence, and "almost all" is unexamined.
- **A section on deliberately inducing shame, fear, anger or ego**, prefaced by a hedge about morality that substitutes for evidence that it works.
- **"Cognitive dissonance" as an art-direction tool.** The term is misapplied, voiceover against image is counterpoint, and no effect is evidenced.
- **An adoption bell curve** with no source, used to license copying an exhausted format, whose entire evidential base is one video's view count.
- **A posting-ratio model** admitted on camera to be arbitrary and then used as a model.
- **Red draws the eye more than green across cultures.** Channel C's only unevidenced psychological claim, recorded for fairness. Her shape-to-meaning mapping is also convention asserted as fact, though she does demonstrate the affordance case, which the colour claim never gets.
- **Survivorship reasoning**, the corpus's largest junk category. One video is built entirely on four already-enormous brands with the playbook reverse-engineered, then destabilises its own ground by keeping an exemplar after disqualifying it on both value and conduct, and finally concedes the method is not transferable without the specific people at the centre of it, a sentence that cancels the lesson it just taught. Another derives "a formula for emulating luxury" from a single brand selected for being unprecedented, that is, for being unrepeatable.
- **Post-hoc rationalisation**, treated separately from survivorship: intent read out of work the source had no part in, including invented internal deliberations stated in the brand's own voice and derived from an ad library, which shows what ran and not why.

**The distinction the skill preserves:** reading observable properties off an artefact and converting them into a production spec is legitimate, and the camera read does exactly this, claiming no intent. Inventing the brand's reasoning is not.

## Gaps, what no source covers

1. **How to write a rule so it can be tested.** The subject of this skill. Two sources produce a rule artefact; neither explains the grammar, clause phrasing, threshold choice or sufficient count. The clause forms in the skill are reverse-engineered from instances.
2. **Numbers of any kind**, outside four instances. No type scale, no grid in units, no contrast ratio, no tolerance, no focal length, no duration. A direction with no measurable quantity cannot be audited, and this corpus almost never supplies one. Every figure in the skill body is therefore either derived in place or taken from the design plugin's shared canvas file, which is itself a stated assumption rather than a measurement.
3. **Enforcement across people.** No review gate, no approval criteria, no arbiter, no escalation when maker and director disagree. The nearest thing is one source's snipping-tool-and-arrows practice, which never says who decides. Channel A's briefing systems specify an asset's *route* and never its *standard*.
4. **What happens when a rule is broken.** Zero instances of enforcement in nine videos. Nobody shows a rejection.
5. **Rule conflict, and formats that cannot honour a rule.** Zero.
6. **When and on what grounds to change a rule.** One silent change with no criterion. No discussion of rule lifespan or revision.
7. **Telling a rule from a preference.** Never posed as a question.
8. **Negative rules.** Three in nine videos. No source treats proscription as part of a direction.
9. **Measuring whether the direction worked.** No output is tested against anything but the director's taste. No blind read, no comparison, no reader test. The one non-taste instrument in the corpus is the greyscale check. Channel A cites performance only as unexamined view counts.
10. **Directing AI image models.** Never addressed as direction anywhere. AI appears only as material and production tooling, plus one unexplained refusal. One build's philosophy is anti-generation, which is a stance rather than a method. **The most common art-direction executor today is absent from the corpus**, and the skill's section on it is labelled as construction from transferable parts.
11. **Legibility, small-size rendering, accessibility.** C's clarity work is anchored in gameplay function and does not generalise cleanly; B dismisses legibility once. Accessibility: zero mentions in nine videos. The skill defers contrast ratios and the accessibility floor to the design plugin rather than inventing them.
12. **Motion, timing and sound as governed dimensions.** Motion appears as "video style"; no easing, duration or cut rule. Sound appears twice, both times as atmosphere.
13. **Production and material specification.** A tin, a capsule and a storefront are designed with no substrate, finish, dieline or print spec.
14. **Cost and feasibility as a constraint on a rule.** One instance in nine videos.
15. **Rule survival across change.** Change of maker: one source. Change of format: demonstrated by one, discussed by nobody. Change of product or medium: not addressed at all. The format-change test in the skill fills a gap no source attempts.

## Known weaknesses

- **Three channels, six of nine videos from one, and that one produces a toolkit rather than a rule set.** The skill's spine therefore rests on three videos, one of which is n=1 from another industry.
- **The best material comes from game development**, where clarity has a functional arbiter that brand work does not have. The skill states this limit rather than importing the certainty.
- **The clause grammar is constructed, not sourced.** It is the most likely thing in the skill to be wrong.
- **No source demonstrates a rejection**, so the gate, the central mechanism of the skill, is untested in the corpus as well as in practice.
- **Channel A's material is largely excluded** after mining. That is a defensible result, but it means the plugin paid for six videos of curation to establish mostly what not to carry.
- **One rule's trigger is not testable**, and the skill says so in its confidence line: which artefact you approve of is taste, so principle 2's output is testable and its input is not.
- Not run on live client work.
