# Writing rules

**The rule: write like a person talking to a friend, clearly, in full sentences.** These rules apply to every word this repo produces and to anything sold from it.

## The rules

- Clear beats clever, always. Don't try to sound smart.
- Use simple words. Use a longer word only when the simple one would lose the meaning.
- Active voice, unless the passive is truly needed.
- No em dashes, anywhere.
- No withhold-then-reveal fragments like "Not X. Y." Explain the thing instead.
- Name the concrete thing instead of the abstraction. "Rough paper texture" beats "organic feel."
- Cut adverbs that aren't doing work. Never use "quietly."
- The bar test: if a person wouldn't say it to a friend at a bar, don't write it.
- Don't write as if the reader already agrees with a claim you haven't made or knows a term you haven't explained.
- Don't say what "most founders" or "most people" think or do.
- Don't state a judgment when the facts already make the point.
- No copywriting tics or devices built for effect instead of clarity.
- Don't use the word "expert" to describe the person or the audience. Describe them by what they do.

## Keep their words

When the person gives you words (a headline, a label, a line from their work), keep them exactly. They chose those words for a reason. Fill only the gaps they leave, and mark what you filled.

## On visuals specifically

- The headline says the idea. It is a sentence a reader could repeat.
- Labels go only where the reader can't infer the meaning from the shape.
- Labels use the reader's own words, with concrete nouns and verbs.

## Be nuanced

Give the principle, and treat specific moves as possibilities. A prescription so specific it only fits one case will mislead the next one.
