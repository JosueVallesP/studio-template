# Generic tells

**The rule: a piece fails if it would look at home in anyone's feed.** Generic means visible sameness. No single tell makes a piece generic, and a piece can avoid every tell below and still be generic. Run the sameness test first; use the list only to explain what you see.

## The sameness test

1. **Swap test.** Put another person's name on it, someone in the same field. Does it fit them just as well? If yes, it's generic, however polished.
2. **Default test.** Could you get this by giving the tool a one-line prompt with no brand context? If yes, the brand isn't in it yet.
3. **Source test.** Point to the line in `brief.md` or `direction.md` that each main choice comes from. Choices with no source are the tool's defaults.

## Tells seen often as of October 2026

This list goes stale. Each time models improve, a new look becomes the default. `cd-learn` adds and removes entries from real rejections; date every change.

**Images**
- Glossy photoreal renders with even, perfect lighting and no real place in them.
- Purple-to-blue or orange-to-pink gradients as the background.
- Isometric 3D scenes, floating blobs, glass panels.
- Stock business photos: handshakes, laptops on desks, people pointing at whiteboards.
- Surreal "concept" images where the concept needs a caption.

**Layout**
- Rounded cards with soft shadows for every block.
- An icon in a circle beside every point.
- Everything centered, everything the same size.
- The same two or three fonts every tool picks by default.

**Frameworks and diagrams**
- A 2x2 whose axes could fit any topic ("effort and impact," "short and long term").
- Pyramids, funnels, icebergs and Venn diagrams used because they're familiar, not because the idea has that shape.
- A circle of four arrows labeled with four verbs.
- Numbered "pillars" with an icon each.

**Social formats**
- A first slide that's a hook in giant type with one highlighted word.
- "X things I learned" layouts with a numbered list and nothing else.

**Copy on visuals**
- Words that sound like a pitch: "unlock," "game-changer," "level up," "the secret."
- Headlines that tease instead of saying the idea.

## When a tell is fine

A tell is fine when the direction calls for it on purpose and the piece earns it. A 2x2 is fine when the two axes are really the two forces in the person's idea. Note the reason in the review so the next reviewer doesn't flag it again.
