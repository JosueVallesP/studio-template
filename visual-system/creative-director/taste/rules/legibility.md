# Legibility

**The rule: the visual makes complete sense on its own, to a stranger, on a phone, even if nobody reads the post around it.**

## Stand alone

- The headline states the idea in plain, concrete words. It doesn't tease, and it doesn't need the post to explain it.
- Labels use words the reader already knows: "posts" and "body of work," not concept names like "stock" and "flow" that need defining.
- If the visual needs a caption to make sense, it isn't done.

## Read at the size people will see it

- Check at the size the format spec gives. For a feed image, that's about 360 pixels wide on a phone. For a page or a PDF, it's the printed or on-screen page. Anything you can't read there is too small.
- One place for the eye to start, made obvious by size, contrast or position. Then a clear path through.
- Fewer words beat smaller words. Cut before you shrink.

## Tests

1. **Delete the labels.** With every word gone except the headline, can you still see the relationship? If all that's left is a row of boxes, the shape isn't doing the work.
2. **Stranger test.** Would someone who hasn't heard the explanation say the idea back after one look? Describe what a first-time reader would most likely conclude, and fix any gap.
3. **Wall test.** Pinned on a wall with no caption, would it bring the idea back a month later?

## Where this comes from

These tests come from Josué's visual-philosophy method. `formats/` files set the exact sizes for each platform.
