# The interview

One question at a time. Ask only what the documents didn't answer. Keep their words. When an answer is vague, ask for an example: a real moment, a real client, a real piece of work.

## Order

Go bottom up, the way the brief is laid out. Each layer rests on the one below, so a weak answer low down means the layers above it will be guesses.

### 1. What they do and the proof

- "In plain words, what do you do and for whom?"
- "What's the piece of work you're proudest of? What happened because of it?"
- "What do you know that took you years to learn and most people in your field still miss?"
- "What can a stranger check about you? A book, a result, a body of writing, clients, years in the field."

Push: if they give an adjective ("I'm very strategic"), ask for the time it showed.

### 2. Values

- "What do you refuse to do, even when it would pay?"
- "When have you walked away from work, and why?"

Push: values are few and rarely change. If they list six, ask which two they'd keep if they could only keep two.

### 3. Behaviors and points of view

- "What do you believe about your field that people in it would argue with?"
- "What do you do differently from others who do your work, and why?"

### 4. Philosophy and thesis

- "How do you see your field? If you drew it, what would be at the center?"
- "What's the claim about the world your work rests on? Finish this: the world would be better if more people understood that..."

### 5. Vehicle and content

- "Where does your work reach people today? Where do you want it to?"
- "What do you publish, and what do you wish you published?"

### 6. Who it's for

- "Whose opinion of you matters most to your work? Describe them by what they do."
- "What do they need to believe about you before they'd call you?"

### 7. What it should feel like

- "When someone sees something of yours, what should they think and feel? Give me three to five."
- For each vague one: "Trustworthy like a family doctor, or like a Swiss bank?" Keep going until the word points somewhere.
- "Which one matters most?"

### 8. What to avoid

- "Who in your field do you never want to be confused with? What about them?"
- "What would make you cringe if you saw it with your name on it?"

### 9. What stays, and your own pictures

- "Is there a mark, a symbol or a colour you want kept, whatever else changes?"
- If yes: "What does it mean to you? You can skip this one." The meaning often decides the direction, so ask once and take no for an answer.
- "Can you send photographs you have taken, or that show your life and work? Ten to twenty is plenty."
- "May an image be made with an image model when it starts from one of your stories or photographs?"

Write the answers in `brief.md`. The photographs go in `assets/photos/`, each with one line saying where it came from.

## Signs the interview isn't done

- The proof of caliber is all adjectives.
- The thesis could belong to anyone in their field.
- The associations are words every brand uses: innovative, trustworthy, bold.
- You can't name one thing about them a competitor couldn't also claim.

If you hit these and the person has no more time, write what you have, list the gaps under Open questions, and end on `Brief paused`.
