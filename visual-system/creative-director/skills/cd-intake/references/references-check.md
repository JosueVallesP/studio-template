# Collecting and judging references

What someone likes and what fits them aren't always the same. This step collects their taste and then checks it against who they are.

## What to ask for

1. **Brands they like, and why.** "I like Aesop" can mean the restraint, the type, the tone of voice or the shops. The why matters more than the name. Ask "what about it?" until the answer is something you could see on a page.
2. **Brands and people they dislike or don't want to be confused with.** This often tells you more, and faster.
3. **People, not only companies.** "Whose personal presence feels like the level you're at, or want to be seen at?" For a personal brand this is often the more honest reference.
4. **Pieces, not only brands.** One specific post, cover, site or poster beats a whole brand, because it shows exactly what caught them.

Save images in `<brand>/references/`. Record each one in `references.md`.

## How to judge fit

For each reference, write what it signals (from what you see, not what the brand says about itself), then check three things against `brief.md`:

1. **Audience.** Would the people in "Who the brand is for" trust this signal on this person?
2. **Caliber.** Would borrowing this look make the person seem bigger, smaller or louder than their proof supports? See `<plugin>/taste/rules/caliber.md`.
3. **Truth.** Is the quality the person likes in this reference actually true of them? A loved look that claims something untrue is a costume.

Mark fit as yes, partly, or no, with the reason.

## Saying no kindly

When a look they love doesn't fit them, say so with the reason, and say what in it can carry over. For example: "The restraint you like in that brand fits you. The black-and-gold luxury cues don't; your clients are technical founders who'd read them as selling." Never just drop a reference silently.

## When their references are thin

If they can't name references, or every one points the same generic way, offer two or three entries from `<plugin>/taste/references/library.md` that match the associations in the brief, and ask how each one feels. Record their reactions as references.

## The Reading paragraph

End `references.md` with two or three sentences: what their references share, where their taste and their fit agree, and where they don't. This paragraph is the bridge into direction.
