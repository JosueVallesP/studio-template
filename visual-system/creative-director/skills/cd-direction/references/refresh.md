# Refresh mode: protect the equities, update the rest

The person likes their brand, but it feels dated or no longer matches where they are. The risk is changing the parts people recognize and keeping the parts that are tired. Mastercard's 2016 refresh is a useful example to share: it kept the two overlapping circles and modernized almost everything around them.

## 1. Find the equities first

Before any change, fill `equities.md`. An equity is something people already recognize the brand by: a color, a mark, a way of laying out an idea, a recurring image, a headline style.

For each one, name the evidence it's recognized: people mention it, it appears on every piece, the person's audience uses it to describe them. "The person likes it" alone doesn't make an equity.

Decide for each: keep it exactly, or keep the idea and update how it's executed.

## 2. Name what's tired

From the brief, in the person's words first: what feels dated, and why. Then your reading: which choices date it (a font tied to a specific era, a stock image style, a layout trend), using `<plugin>/taste/rules/generic-tells.md` where relevant.

## 3. Check for a contradiction

Look at whether the existing brand already carries a contradiction, even a weak one. If it does, sharpen it using `references/contradiction.md` steps 3 to 7, keeping the equities on whichever side they belong. If it doesn't, decide with the person whether a refresh is the moment to add one, or whether that makes it a rebrand.

## 4. Write the direction

Fill `direction.md` as in new mode, with two additions:

- Under **Locked and flexible**, every equity marked "keep exactly" is locked.
- Under **Change history**, version 1 notes what changed from the old brand and what stayed.

Add a row to the Tests table: "Recognition: would someone who knows the old brand recognize the new one?" It must pass.
