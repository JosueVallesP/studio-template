# The contradiction (new and rebrand modes)

Adapted from Josué's `genius-of-contradiction` skill. The idea: the most interesting people and brands blend opposites that are both true of them, and when the two sides collide they create a third thing people feel. A random mix of styles means nothing when the sides meet. A good contradiction does.

**The rule that keeps it from being random:** start from the target association in the brief, then find two opposite sides already true of the person whose collision produces it. Work backwards from the feeling to the pair. Never pick a pair because it looks cool and then hunt for a reason.

## 1. Confirm the target association

Take the associations from the brief. Check the main one is specific enough to design for. If it isn't, sharpen it with the person before going on.

## 2. Find tensions that are already true

Look for places where the person is honestly two opposite things:

- **Where they came from vs. what they do now** (a former musician who now runs a fund).
- **How they work vs. what they deliver** (slow, handmade thinking for a fast industry).
- **Their field's look vs. what they believe** (a field that looks corporate, a person who thinks it shouldn't).
- **Their audience: who they are vs. who they want to become.**
- **Their place or history vs. where they're going.**
- **The channel vs. the substance** (lasting ideas that show up in a fast feed).

Write three to six candidate pairs. For each, write the evidence from `brief.md` that both sides are true. Drop any pair where a side has no evidence. A side the person only wishes were true is a costume, and people can tell.

If there's no real tension, say so. Two honest options: build one side into the work first, or use the field's tension (looking unlike their field for a reason they actually believe).

## 3. The collision test

For each pair write: **[Side A] + [Side B] = [what people feel].** Keep pairs whose result lands on or near the target association.

Then the felt test: would a stranger notice this contradiction in two seconds of looking, with nobody explaining it? If not, it's wordplay. Wordplay can live in the copy. It can't carry a visual identity.

Pick one pair. Two at once reads as confusion. (If two pass every test, show both to the person in the proposal and let them choose.)

## 4. Set the ratio

One side is the **base**, the other the **accent**, roughly 80/20. At 50/50 the sides cancel out. Name which shape it is:

- **Familiar base, surprising accent.** The base looks like the person's field; the accent is the twist that makes it stand out.
- **Surprising base, grounding accent.** The base is already unlike the field; the accent ties it back to what the person actually is, so it reads as belonging instead of lost.

## 5. Visual codes

For each side, fill the Visual codes table in `direction.md`: type, color, texture and material, imagery, composition, the mark, motion. Concrete enough that two designers would choose alike. "Elegant" isn't a code. "High-contrast serif, wide margins, one muted color" is.

## 6. Clash points

Assign each side to specific layers. The clash should show in one or two places, not everywhere. Everywhere else, the base runs the show. Examples of clash points: refined serif type over rough photocopied texture; a strict grid holding loose hand-drawn marks; clinical layout with poetic copy.

## 7. Tests

Fill these rows of the Tests table:

1. **Truth:** each side has evidence.
2. **One sentence:** a friend at a bar would get it.
3. **Direction:** the collision points at the target association.
4. **Removal:** take the accent away. With a familiar base, the brand should start to look like everyone in the field. With a surprising base, it should lose its tie to who the person is. If nothing changes, the accent isn't doing anything.
5. **Trust:** the accent never undercuts what the audience needs to believe.
6. **Felt:** a stranger would notice it without an explanation.

If a test fails, go back to the step it points to.

## Failure modes

- **Costume:** a side that isn't true.
- **Mush:** a 50/50 ratio.
- **Contradiction everywhere:** every element mixes both sides.
- **Wordplay:** only works when explained.
- **Pair first:** opposites chosen for looks, meaning invented after.
- **Lost outlier:** a surprising base with no grounding accent.

## Rebrand mode

Add the old brand to the thinking: what it signaled, and the specific reason it stopped fitting. The new direction should answer that reason directly. Write it in one line under "The contradiction."
