# Provenance: dsn-composition

Everything about where the composition knowledge came from. `SKILL.md` carries what to do; this file carries what it rests on. Quotes are from auto-generated captions and are reproduced with their timestamps so they can be rechecked.

An independent check of `SKILL.md` against the transcripts, written by a different agent and not using this file as evidence, is in `references/audit.md`. Read that before trusting any attribution here.

## Corroboration: five videos, three channels

**Weight agreement by channel, not by video.** Three of the five primary guides are Satori Graphics, and two of those reuse the same scripted segment close to verbatim: the six-level movement-and-flow ladder appears in `PKfZ1gnVJ44`, `GT-K0CrrgfU` and `WLI5ckPofrk` with near-identical wording. So the six-level ladder is **one source repeated three times, not a consensus**.

The real count is three channels:

So these tutorials supply mechanics the canon lacks, and the canon supplies the judgment the tutorials lack. Neither half is sufficient. Mechanics alone produce competent generic work, which is exactly what a tutorial-trained process outputs.

## The convergence the whole skill turns on

All three channels open on the same first question, phrased three ways:

- "where do I want my audience to look at first" [`r6LPNRVhGKA` 00:02:30]
- "without a focal point A Design Falls flat" [`yUvGHNzqG7M` 00:02:37]
- "without hierarchy your viewers won't really know what to look at first" [`aQI95mVeDXw` 00:11:16]

Kittl demonstrates the failure destructively: shrink the title, enlarge the kicker, and "just with a couple of tweaks we've completely destroyed the hierarchy" [`aQI95mVeDXw` 00:13:53].

**Three levels, three vocabularies.** Satori: hook → secondary detail → finisher [`GT-K0CrrgfU` 00:03:45], and separately attract → intrigue → deliver [`FpwZTl3dCZE` 00:11:10]. Envato: focal point → supporting → extra information [`r6LPNRVhGKA` 00:06:58]. Kittl counts to four or five on a film poster but *ranks* them, and the top three carry the structure [`aQI95mVeDXw` 00:12:38]. No source derives three, which is why `SKILL.md` labels it a convention.

**Two carriers of difference** is the most consequential convergence in the set, and also the move most often taught as single-variable. All three channels enumerate contrast across multiple properties [`GT-K0CrrgfU` 00:15:05 onward; `aQI95mVeDXw` 00:08:00; `r6LPNRVhGKA` 00:28:18]. Satori's sharpest demonstration is a single Nike poster carrying three at once: colour, shape (angular field against the curve of the shoe), and texture (grainy ground against a smooth product) [`GT-K0CrrgfU` 00:18:08–00:19:35]. The two-carrier *rule* is derived here, not taken from a source: one carrier fails silently on recolour, print, shrink or a changed background, and size plus one non-size property survives all four.

## Presets: convention or derivation

- **Rule of thirds.** Stated reason: the four intersections are "where the viewer's eyes will go to naturally first" [`r6LPNRVhGKA` 00:14:02]. **Asserted by both sources that teach it and derived by neither, a convention.** The reason that does hold: a thirds grid makes dead-centre placement structurally unavailable, and off-centre placement is what creates lead room and asymmetric weight, which both sources independently tie to direction and energy. Keep it as a forcing function against centring; discard the claim about where eyes land.
- **Golden ratio, ~1.618.** The number is standard mathematics and verifiable outside these transcripts; the *design* claim attached to it is derived by nobody here. Both Satori and Envato raise it and both immediately hedge: "or so says the theory" [`PKfZ1gnVJ44` 00:16:15]; "Loosely based on the golden ratio" and "the ratio doesn't exactly dictate where each element should go" [`r6LPNRVhGKA` 00:15:47]. Kittl omits it. Convention: a starting point to adjust, not a constraint to satisfy.
- **Rule of odds (three, up to five).** Stated reason is avoiding clutter [`r6LPNRVhGKA` 00:14:57], which does not survive its own examples, since both place the middle subject dead centre, a symmetry move rather than a decluttering one. The stated reason is a convention. A derivation that does work, and this is a reading rather than the source's claim: an even number of equal subjects creates a tie the eye cannot break, so the group reads as a field; an odd number leaves one unpaired element that becomes the focal point by default.
- **60/30/10 colour split.** Kittl presents it, then reading its own example concedes the accent is "debatably a little more than 10 percent" [`aQI95mVeDXw` 00:32:03]. A rule its own demonstration cannot satisfy is a convention. The principle underneath is sound: one dominant, one supporting, one accent, the three-level hierarchy applied to colour. Keep the structure, drop the percentages.
- **Baseline multiples.** **A real derivation, and the only preset in the set that is one.** Whole-number multiples of the base leading put every baseline in every column on a shared rule, so cross-column alignment survives type-size changes. The specific figures given (a 28pt heading over 14pt body) are caption-level and arbitrary [`PKfZ1gnVJ44` 00:13:01]; the whole-number relationship is the mechanism.
- **Horizontal = calm, vertical = bold, diagonal = action** [`yUvGHNzqG7M` 00:18:57]. Single-source, asserted, no mechanism. Convention. The defensible residue is narrow: diagonals are the only one of the three not parallel to a frame edge, so they read as unresolved relative to the frame. That much is observable; the emotional vocabulary is not.
- **Arnheim's structural net**: a centre point slightly above true centre, corner-to-corner axes, attention at the midpoints [`yUvGHNzqG7M` 00:16:49]. Single-source, and the attribution is mangled in the captions. Arnheim is a real figure with real work on the structural skeleton of a frame, but **this specific rendering is Satori's and should not be cited as Arnheim's without checking the primary text.** Usable as a placement hypothesis to test by eye, not as a law.

## Where the sources disagree, and the decision rule

**1. Clever layout versus the plain frame.** Satori's corpus optimises for engagement and memorability: layered flows, implied motion, deliberate disruption, temporal pacing. Vignelli's position is the direct opposite: full bleed over clever layout, because inventive page architecture "looks obsolete very fast." Neither is wrong about what it optimises. **Rule: the compositional trick is a dating mechanism, so price it against the piece's lifespan.** Ephemeral pieces can carry it; anything reused as a template should not. Do not average, because a half-clever system is the worst of both, since it dates without earning the attention.

**2. Satori's friction versus the canon's "confusing is a failure."** Satori teaches friction as seasoning [`PKfZ1gnVJ44` 00:25:40]. Maeda's position is that work only its maker can read is simply a failure, and that confusion is never sophistication. **Rule: Maeda, using Satori's own escape hatch.** Satori concedes the mechanism needs clearly defined clean zones, which is the reconciliation it half-states and never names: friction is admissible only where it costs the reader nothing they need. In client work that is close to a prohibition, because almost every zone in a post graphic carries something.

**3. Where composition starts.** Satori starts from the message and the medium, then selects a grid. Envato starts from the frame and a catalogue of techniques, namely thirds, golden triangle, golden ratio, framing and horizon line, and verifies against it with a Photoshop crop overlay [`r6LPNRVhGKA` 00:30:41]. Satori optimises for appropriateness, Envato for compositional correctness. **Rule: Envato's catalogue is a diagnostic, not a generator.** It can tell you whether a composition landed on an attention point; it cannot tell you what the composition should say. Run as a generator it produces well-composed pieces with nothing to communicate.

**4. Add or remove.** Satori adds mechanisms; Kittl adds principles, including variety as its own named requirement so "you don't forget to include these things" [`aQI95mVeDXw` 00:27:09]; Envato names simplification once, briefly. The canon's position is that subtraction is mandatory and the pass did not happen if nothing came out. **Rule: subtraction, and read the additive bias as a production artefact**: a thirty-minute video about composition is paid for mechanisms, not for removals. The bias is structural, not a mistake by any of the three.

**5. Variety versus narrow grammar.** Kittl treats variety as a principle to include. Vignelli's argument is that every extra permitted variable is a place a system can be operated inconsistently. **Rule: split by who operates it.** Kittl's variety is correct for a one-off a designer builds and ships. For a template someone else fills repeatedly, Vignelli governs, and the right number of permitted variables is the smallest number that still distinguishes the levels.

## Unreliable claims and caption damage

Auto-generated captions mangle terms and numbers throughout. These are flagged because several would be embarrassing to repeat.

- **"about 65 of humans or visual Learners and it takes about 30 milliseconds for us to identify images"** [`r6LPNRVhGKA` 00:00:48]. The opening claim of the Envato course. Both figures are unsourced and the visual-learners claim is widely discredited learning-styles folklore. **Do not repeat either number.** Carried into `SKILL.md` because it sets the register for every perception claim in the set.
- **"99% of designers don't consider this temporal flow"** [`PKfZ1gnVJ44` 00:08:31]. Rhetorical, no basis offered.
- **"studies show that Simply Having a contrasting color on the CTA can dramatically increase its clickability"** [`I4C-Smqsf7Y` 00:05:39]. No study named.
- **"the F or desired pattern"** [`GT-K0CrrgfU` 00:04:29]. The second term is not readable. Do not guess it. Note that eye-tracking patterns are named once across five guides, in passing, with no source.
- **"This grid divides the page into four equal vertical sections"** describing column grids generally [`PKfZ1gnVJ44` 00:13:55]. Column grids are not inherently four columns; a slip between the general case and the on-screen example.

## Canon-tier material behind SKILL.md's judgment gates

These come from the judgment layer, not the tutorials, and `SKILL.md` folds them into Mode B and the anti-patterns.

- **Review order: semantics, syntax, understandability, then anything visual.** Vignelli's order. Semantics is whether the arrangement says the true thing about the subject; syntax is whether the composition's own rules are applied identically everywhere; understandability is whether an unaided reader gets it. His diagnosis is that when design does not work, it is usually because it is not internally consistent.
- **Irrelevance is the first fault.** Rand's ordering as Maeda reports it: bad design is irrelevant first, superficial second, pretentious third.
- **A composition can lie.** Vignelli's Central Park, a long park drawn as a square because the diagram needed it, and Maeda's two identical payroll charts reading "up" and "stable" depending only on scale, are the same finding reached independently.
- **Where you started determines whether the output is generic.** Starting from the format's existing examples produces category-shaped work by construction.
- **Bierut:** the right resolution's only defect is that nobody will think you were clever; already-old-looking is the only cheap route to not dating; and the 40,000-permutation identity failure, where every variant was equally valid so no variant could mean anything, and the operator reused one anyway.
- **Vignelli's Heller rule:** the brand name is set at one size on every box, whatever the box size, so a small box reads as the same brand. The furniture check in `SKILL.md` step 9 is this rule.
- **Scher's cover-the-logo test:** mask the mark; if recognition lived entirely in the mark, the composition is carrying no identity.
- **Encina:** name the hierarchy, then remove, and keep removing after it feels right.

## Which rules carry a (thin) marker in SKILL.md, and why

`SKILL.md` marks a rule **(thin)** with no explanation. The reasons are here. "Thin" covers three cases: the rule rests on one source, it rests on one channel wearing three names, or its number was inherited rather than worked out.

**Thin because the ladder they appear to agree on is one script.** The six-level movement-and-flow material is one source repeated three times, as recorded at the head of this file. Every rule in `SKILL.md` descended from it is thin regardless of how many videos state it: principle 4 (ratio and viewing size decide the flow), principle 5 (one grid break), the cap of two cue types on one path, the friction rule, the pattern-subordination rule, the active and passive negative-space split, the temporal-pacing reading, and the thumbnail test including the ultimate test. The thumbnail test is thin and kept anyway, because it is the only test in the file that reproduces the real viewing condition instead of modelling it.

**Thin because one source states it.** Principle 1 (exactly one focal point, where the deepest source in the set states the opposite), principle 6 (subtraction, named once as "editing back"), lead room, the destructive edit, the direction emotions, and the structural net.

**Thin because the number was inherited, not derived.** Principle 2's three levels (no source derives three), the rule of thirds, the golden ratio, the rule of odds and its three-to-five figure, and the colour split behind one dominant, one supporting and one accent.

**Not thin.** Principle 3 (two carriers of difference), the asymmetric-weight resolution, repetition as binder with one break as focal point, and the baseline-multiple rule. The first three have independent cover across the three channels; the fourth is the only real derivation in the set.

## Constructions of this skill, not source claims

These were stated in the body as "derived here" or "by observation here" and are recorded here instead. All of them are this skill's own, in no transcript and in no shared reference file.

- **The two-carrier rule itself.** The sources enumerate contrast across many properties; none says two. The mechanism is this skill's: one carrier fails silently under recolour, one-ink print, shrink or a changed background, and size plus one non-size property survives all four.
- **The cap of two cue types on one path.** The source warning is about the number of competing paths and gives no number.
- **The count of one grid break, the claim that two breaks read as absence of a grid, and the single irregularity budget shared across grid break, pattern break and style collision.** The source gives the shape and no count.
- **Four levels in a feed image collapsing into two**, and anything about feed rendering at all. The sources are silent on platform rendering.
- **The odd-group mechanism** (an even number of equal subjects creates a tie the eye cannot break). The source's own stated reason is decluttering, which does not survive its examples.
- **The blob-count test.** Nothing in the composition set states it. The nearest relative is a squint test for grouping, which lives in the hierarchy source set.
- **The flip test.** Mirroring the frame is in no source in the set.
- **The greyscale test, the stress render at 4x, the grid-closure sum, the contrast-floor test and the furniture sweep.** All mechanical checks added by this skill against the shared canvas file.
- **"Be remembered"** as a fourth purpose in Mode A step 2. The source's purpose question names informing and selling and demonstrates shock.
- **Two frequency claims, relabelled as observations and then dropped from the body entirely:** that the shouting-match failure is the commonest in client content, and that the longest-real-content failure is where carousel templates usually fail. No source in the set counts or ranks failures.

## Not covered by any of these sources

- **Any measurement at all.** No source tests a composition against an outcome. Every judgment is "does it look balanced." Treat the whole set as craft consensus, not evidence.
- **Contrast as a measurable quantity.** All three channels teach contrast and none gives a ratio, a threshold, or an accessibility standard. No mention of WCAG by anyone.
- **Line length and measure.** Satori notes lines should not be too long or too short [`PKfZ1gnVJ44` 00:12:17] and gives no figure. No characters-per-line range anywhere.
- **Typographic judgment beyond four style categories.** Kittl's serif/sans/script/display taxonomy is where type instruction stops. Nothing on pairing, optical sizing, or spacing beyond "use a baseline grid."
- **Right-to-left and non-Latin reading order.** Envato flags left-to-right as specific to Western cultures and then builds on it anyway.
- **Reflow and responsive layout.** Satori's transferability section is a squint test, not a method. Nothing on breakpoints or how a grid should change between formats.
- **Motion and sequence.** Satori mentions that motion "elevates" a focal point and gives no mechanics. Nothing on timing, easing, or composing for a loop.
- **Chart and data composition.** Entirely absent from all five. The canon supplies the lying-chart warning; no tutorial supplies the layout method.
- **Eye-tracking evidence for any of it.** Reading patterns are named once, in a garbled aside.
- **Composition at architectural or environmental scale.** All five are screen-and-print at page size.
- **How to tell a strong composition from a stupid one.** Every source assumes it. This is the irreducible gap the canon also names, and it is why these mechanics need a human holding them.
