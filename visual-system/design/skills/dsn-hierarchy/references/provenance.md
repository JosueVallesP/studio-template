# dsn-hierarchy: provenance

Everything about where the knowledge in `SKILL.md` came from. The skill itself
carries the uncertainty flags that change what you do; this file holds the
source list, the corroboration analysis, the disagreements with their decision
rules, the convention-versus-derivation labels, the unreliable claims, and what
these sources do not cover.

An independent check of `SKILL.md` against the transcripts, written by a
different agent and not using this file as evidence, is in
`references/audit.md`. Read it alongside this one: where the two disagree, the
audit is the primary record, because this file is self-attestation by the agent
that wrote the skill.

## Corroboration warning

**Four of the five transcripts behind this skill are the same channel, and two
of the three hierarchy-specific ones are.** Satori Graphics carries most of the
skill, with DesignSpo as the only independent voice.

An earlier version of this file and of `SKILL.md` put Satori's share at "roughly
60 to 65 percent". **Retracted.** That figure was an estimate with no method
behind it and no way to reproduce it. The qualitative statement above is what
the source set supports: most of the skill rests on one channel, and the
single-source attributions below are the auditable form of it.

- The **spine** is corroborated across two independent channels: hierarchy is a
  ranking decided before styling; only one element can be first; contrast is the
  means and is relative to the field; the working tool set is size, weight,
  colour, position, space and typeface; white space is required for the rank to
  be legible; red is the most attention-grabbing colour; the hook need not be
  the most important element; F and Z placement in left-to-right cultures.
- **Satori alone:** the micro/macro white space split, the doubling spatial
  scale, silhouette-not-bounding-box spacing, alignment connecting across a gap,
  fused points of interest, dynamic counterbalance and its anchor requirement,
  the cover test, the squint test for grouping, the whole movement-and-flow
  ladder, temporal flow (impact, linger, pause), implied motion, deliberate flow
  disruption, the centred-larger-in-a-symmetric-group trick, the odd-number
  convention, and the extra contrast channels (shape, texture, style,
  psychology, emotion, concept).
- **DesignSpo alone:** the single most important corrective in the skill, that
  task-relevant information outranks designer-controlled salience. Also the
  explicit decoupling of rank from importance, cohesion as a formal requirement,
  the ten ranked contrast channels, the 4.5 figure and the colour-vision caveat,
  the minimum-sufficient-contrast principle, and the restraint rules on faces
  and imagery.

Treat Satori-only material as one experienced practitioner's method, not craft
consensus.

## Where the sources disagree

1. **Who sets the rank, the designer or the reader's goal?** Satori's model is
   entirely designer-controlled: attract, intrigue, deliver, a visual trap
   [4QNEDhrsRLo 00:00:42, 00:01:19]. DesignSpo puts task-relevant information
   second in his whole contrast ranking, above every channel the designer
   controls except motion, and says plainly that people often do not look at the
   big obvious thing, they look for what matters to them [kK1TOpI948o 00:02:50].
   **Decision rule: classify the reader, then apply the matching model.** Cold
   reader, Satori holds. Tasked reader, DesignSpo overrides. Do not average. A
   carousel needs both models in one artefact, applied per slide.

2. **How big a contrast step.** DesignSpo: the minimum sufficient difference, a
   headline "only has to be a little bit bigger" [kK1TOpI948o 00:06:44]. Satori:
   save your strongest contrast for the most important thing [5yjYe9aDSBM
   00:08:48], and he endorses surreal or jarring scale differences [HmgzcaH-uv8
   00:01:27]. **Decision rule: DesignSpo's principle, Satori's magnitude,
   selected by viewing size.** Smallest step that survives the real viewing
   condition, which in a compressed feed thumbnail is a large step and in a
   document is a small one.

3. **How many flows.** Satori: multiple simultaneous paths, a main route plus
   micro-routes, explicitly to increase time on the design [WLI5ckPofrk
   00:03:10]. DesignSpo: anything that competes destroys primacy, and primacy is
   the whole point [kK1TOpI948o 00:00:33]. **Decision rule: one flow where
   attention is not yet won, layered flows where it is.**

4. **What the F and Z patterns describe.** Satori has the F starting top-left and
   extending right with content left-aligned beneath [4QNEDhrsRLo 00:08:49].
   DesignSpo has the Z as left-right then top-bottom and the F as top-bottom then
   left-right [kK1TOpI948o 00:13:41–00:14:16], which is not the usual account of
   either. **Decision rule: discard the mechanism descriptions, keep the
   placement agreement.** Both land in the same place: in a left-to-right
   culture, rank 1 top-left or optical centre, rank 3 bottom-right. Verify the
   pattern names against usability research before repeating them to a client.

5. **Importance versus noticing** is a conflict inside Satori rather than between
   the channels. He says the hook need not be most important [4QNEDhrsRLo
   00:11:20] and elsewhere equates bigger with more important [00:05:58,
   00:15:38]. DesignSpo resolves it [kK1TOpI948o 00:13:09]; the skill follows
   DesignSpo.

## Convention versus derivation

- **Three ranks** is a convention. The derivation behind it is that each rank
  needs a visibly distinct expression and one frame carries about three
  distinguishable steps.
- **The 4 / 8 / 16 / 32 and 8 / 16 / 32 / 64 numbers** are Satori's convention
  [5yjYe9aDSBM 00:14:52]. The doubling is the mechanism: a 10 percent gap
  difference does not read as a rank difference, a 100 percent one does.
- **The 2x feed step and 1.3 to 1.5x close-reading step** are derived here from
  the shrink test. **No source supplies a type scale or a size ratio at all.**
  Satori's 4/8/16/32 is spacing only.
- **4.5:1** is real and checkable against WCAG 2.1 AA for normal text, with 3:1
  for large text and UI components. DesignSpo cites WebAIM's 4.5 and extends it
  from text legibility to general element prominence [kK1TOpI948o 00:05:37], 
  that extension is his convention, not a standard.
- **The greyscale test** is derived from DesignSpo's luminance and colour-vision
  caveat [kK1TOpI948o 00:06:11]. No source states the test.
- **F and Z** are conventions with a plausible basis. Neither source cites
  eye-tracking, and the F pattern's actual provenance is usability research
  neither mentions. Satori's own hedge is worth preserving: not every viewer
  reads this way, it is "a streamlined Avenue that most viewers will be able to
  use" [4QNEDhrsRLo 00:08:49].
- **The odd-number-of-objects rule** is a convention with no reason given
  [4QNEDhrsRLo 00:07:22]. The symmetry-plus-dominant-centre arrangement
  [00:11:57] is the part that does the work.
- **The ten-channel contrast ordering**, which `SKILL.md` points here for, runs
  strongest to weakest: motion, task-relevant information, white space around a
  focal point, humans and faces, colour, size, weight, imagery, added elements,
  misalignment [kK1TOpI948o 00:02:14–00:09:37]. It is one practitioner's
  heuristic, not a measurement. Only the top two come with a stated reason:
  motion reads as threat, and goal-directed search beats designed salience.
  Everything below them is asserted in sequence with no comparison, no test and
  no citation, and the supporting appeal to brain scans is caption-level. The
  usable residue is in `SKILL.md` under "Contrast channels"; the ordering itself
  should not be quoted as a ranking to anyone else.
- **Rank 1 plus rank 2 reconstructing the sentence** comes from the canon's
  verbalisation rule, applied to rank here rather than stated by any source.

## Caption-level and unreliable claims

Every figure in these transcripts is auto-captioned. Specifically flagged, do not
repeat as fact:

- Satori's 4pt / 8pt / 32pt spacing example [5yjYe9aDSBM 00:14:52], the
  illustrative numbers, notably 4pt between body lines, do not describe real
  line-height settings. Keep the ratio structure, discard the figures.
- Satori's "99 percent of designers ignore this" on temporal flow [WLI5ckPofrk
  00:07:46], rhetoric, not a figure.
- DesignSpo's appeal to brain scans supporting the contrast ranking
  [kK1TOpI948o 00:02:14–00:09:37], caption-level and unverifiable.
- "The chart that places colour in order", referred to but never named
  [kK1TOpI948o].
- Satori's space-and-emotion table [5yjYe9aDSBM 00:17:14] is shown on screen and
  is not recoverable from the transcript. The surrounding claim, Apple's
  generous space reads premium, a punk flyer's tight space reads energy, is
  recoverable; the table is not.
- Both channels put red first as the most attention-grabbing colour [4QNEDhrsRLo
  00:02:06; kK1TOpI948o 00:06:11], but both explain why only vaguely, and
  DesignSpo concedes the theories differ.
- Names and product references are mangled throughout all five transcripts.

## Not covered by these sources

- **Type scale.** No modular scale, no size ratio. The step figures in the skill
  are derived from the shrink test.
- **Measurement.** No source tests whether a hierarchy worked. No eye-tracking,
  no A/B results, no reader recital. Every judgement in all three is the
  designer's own eye. The recital test in the skill, hand it to a person and ask
  what they looked at, in order, is the only test touching a real reader, and
  **none of the sources runs it.**
- **Motion.** DesignSpo ranks it the strongest channel available and then says
  nothing about building hierarchy with it. Nothing here governs video, animated
  carousels or scroll-triggered motion.
- **Accessibility beyond one ratio.** Semantic heading order, screen-reader
  reading order, alt text, focus order: absent. The one colour-vision concession
  is a passing caveat [kK1TOpI948o 00:06:11]. Use the `dsn` accessibility
  material instead.
- **Hierarchy in data graphics.** Which series to emphasise, axis prominence,
  annotation weight. The canon's `visual-communication` covers it; these three
  do not.
- **Right-to-left and non-Western reading order.** Both channels say "reverse the
  pattern" and neither goes further.
- **Platform rendering.** Compression, dark mode inversion, LinkedIn's crops and
  downscaling. Not addressed by any source, and it is the condition the work
  actually ships into.

### Derived in the legibility pass

New arithmetic introduced by the legibility pass, derived from
`reference/canvas-assumptions.md` rather than from any source.

- **The 36px and 48px file floors**, from a 12pt readable minimum times the
  nominal downscale factor of 3 and the stress factor of 4.
- **The stress-safe type ladder 48 / 72 / 144px** and the **nominal-only ladder
  36 / 54 / 108px**, built from those floors using the inherited 1.5x and 2x
  steps.
- **The spacing scale 36 / 72 / 144 / 288px**, derived from the requirement that
  a step land on a whole point after division by both 3 and 4, which makes the
  base a multiple of 12. This replaces the unsourced 8 / 16 / 32 / 64 and
  answers `canvas-assumptions.md`'s objection that the 4 and 8 unit convention
  does not transfer to a non-integer downscale.
- **The margin rule**, that the margin is at least the largest between-group gap
  in the frame, with the 144px margin and 792px content column that follow on a
  1080 canvas.
- **The large-text threshold in file pixels**, 18pt rendered times the downscale
  factor, used to decide where 3:1 is permitted instead of 4.5:1.
- **The measure arithmetic** in the carousel example, from an average character
  advance near half the type size.

All figures in both worked examples are illustrative. Neither is a real piece.
