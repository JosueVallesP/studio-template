# Provenance: dsn-sequence

Everything about where the knowledge in `SKILL.md` came from. Nothing here tells you what to do; it tells you how much to trust each instruction and who said it.

## The search failure, stated plainly

Carousel design was searched twice, in two separate waves. Both waves returned tool tutorials and nothing else: Canva walkthroughs, NotebookLM slide generation, Keynote animation. Not one result taught sequencing as a craft problem. **The conclusion to carry forward is that the material does not exist on YouTube**, not that the search was weak. Adding a third wave of carousel queries would be a waste; if this skill is ever upgraded, the place to look is print design education, trade publishing and comics, not social media tutorials.

So this skill was built from a substitute corpus on purpose:

1. **Editorial and magazine sequencing** for the mechanics of holding many frames together, especially under non-expert operators. Four video sources, all print, listed below.
2. **The studio's storytelling canon** (`craft-skills/craft-canon/references/storytelling.md`) for the shape of a sequence: promise, progress, payoff, the turn test, currency alignment, magnitude variety, and the closure choice.
3. **Two finished sibling skills**, `dsn-hierarchy` and `dsn-balance`, for everything inside a single frame.

A reader deserves to know which of the three a given instruction came from, so `SKILL.md` flags them inline and this file totals them.

## The four sources

### 1. Envato (Laura Kyung), "10 Tips for Designing High-Impact Magazines"
`JGeIMOpYDsY`, 47m, ~127k views, 2022.

The only source that treats a whole publication rather than one page. Uniquely supplies:

- **consistency as a trained language**, which is the single most valuable sequencing idea in the four: repeat a treatment long enough and the reader learns to read it, so "every time there is a circle then there is a quote" [00:35:03]. This is what makes a carousel's body template an asset rather than a constraint.
- the licence for one non-repeating frame: the contents page is where a magazine explores, precisely because that section appears once [00:11:49]
- cover-to-interior continuity through repeated type, colour, grid and photo treatment [00:32:39]
- a leading element at the page edge whose job is to make the reader turn [00:38:52]
- the spread, not the page, as the design unit [00:37:30]

**Weakness: it is a template showcase.** Every example is an Envato Elements product and the vendor is named throughout. The analysis is real but every piece of evidence is a thing being sold, and no example is a published magazine with a known reader. Treat the judgements as one art director's reading of stock layouts.

### 2. Yes I'm a Designer, "How to Get Better at Composition and Creative Layouts?"
`OGgvDBFx3-A`, 31m, ~281k views, 2021.

The only source that defines pacing operationally. Uniquely supplies:

- **pacing as density alternation**: "you don't want to have too much busy things in one place you want to have some breathing space next to a very dense area" [00:16:27], demonstrated as dense text block, then void, then image, then a smaller text block
- **rhythm as repetition and alternation**, specifically alternating two accent colours across a layout so the eye is carried [00:29:03]
- three thumbnail sketches minimum before the application opens, with the claim that it is fast [00:02:10]
- the professional norm of presenting three near-finished versions rather than one [00:21:16]
- repeated visual cues (an inverted drop cap echoing an inverted headline) as what ties separated elements together [00:15:00]
- a type-emphasis step of two weights rather than one, so the contrast registers [00:15:44]

**The honest limit, and it matters for this skill: his pacing is within one spread, not across spreads.** He is pacing a reader's eye across two facing pages in a single glance. Applying that to frames a reader sees one at a time, seconds apart, is an extrapolation, and `SKILL.md` makes it at the rest position. Roughly four minutes of the runtime is a membership pitch.

### 3. Organized Adviser (Katie Moreno), "Yearbook Layout Basics"
`u5p_s63NwwU`, 21m, ~40k views, 2022.

**The lowest-production source here and the most directly relevant, because it is the only one addressing the actual problem: many frames, a fixed template, and non-expert operators who did not design it.** That is the crew problem exactly. Uniquely supplies:

- the spread as one unit with an element required to cross the gutter [00:01:14]
- **the two-guide template**: a horizontal eye line in the middle third and a vertical axis, both invisible, both acting as anchors every element sits on or hangs from, deliberately off centre so one quadrant is larger [00:02:59, 00:04:04]
- a dominant element at **two to three times** everything else, placed first, and named as the single most common thing missing from beginner layouts [00:05:12]
- fixing caption width at one column so no operator has to decide one [00:08:34]
- **content-first template design**: "make sure your design is driven from that content and that it's not the other way around" [00:10:58], with the worked counter-example that a layout needing mixed photo orientations fails for golf, where every shot is a vertical
- sketching on paper because operators forget to leave room for captions and then drop them in as an afterthought [00:09:07]
- a printed per-spread checklist attached before the spread is handed in [00:19:32]
- break-the-rules-once-you-know-them, with the break tied to the publication's theme rather than to a mood [00:16:32]

**Weaknesses:** 1,308 words over 21 minutes, slides built in Google Slides with one layout she concedes she could not build as intended, and a product funnel at the end. Several figures are her studio practice rather than measured anything: five to seven photos per spread, the two-to-three-times dominance ratio, the middle-third eye line. Treat all three as conventions.

### 4. Dave Conrey, "Magazine page design fundamentals"
`BAbKX4US7jk`, 18m, ~65k views, 2021.

The only source that shows the same content at three deliberately different intensities and then names what stayed constant. Uniquely supplies:

- **styles rather than instances**: a headline is 48pt because that is the publication's headline style, so it stays 48 even where one headline would sit better larger [00:04:04]. This is the cleanest statement of why a body template holds a set together.
- **the escalation demonstration**: boring but sound, then adventurous, then unconstrained, with the constants identified afterwards as hierarchy, spatial relationships and type styles [00:14:15]
- **the handoff mechanism**: cut the body copy down to one paragraph, because "I only want enough to get people's interest and then lead them with that arrow over to the next page" [00:09:27], and then **remove the arrow on a spread**, because position already supplies the affordance. The second half is what makes it useful rather than a tip about arrows.
- continuity as a reader requirement, stated as a complaint: "where's the continuity I need some continuity" [00:14:59]
- withholding identification to create a question the reader has to turn the page to answer [00:11:10]
- a book recommendation worth noting: Samara, *Making and Breaking the Grid*, whose two halves are the grid and then its deliberate violation, which is the same structure as the template-plus-one-break rule

**Weaknesses:** the shortest source, roughly three minutes of channel housekeeping, and his anti-snapping practice is explicitly a personal choice he attributes to a David Carson masterclass and does not defend. Do not carry it.

## Corroboration

**Three or four channels independently:**
- **The spread, not the page, is the unit.** All four say some version of it, two of them repeatedly. The best-corroborated claim here.
- **A fixed structure carried across many frames is what makes a set read as one thing.** All four.
- **Sketch before the application opens.** Two sources state it as a working rule, both independently claim it is fast, and neither sells a sketching product. Good evidence.
- **One dominant element per frame.** Three sources, with only one supplying a ratio.

**Single-source, flagged in `SKILL.md` at the point of use:**
- Consistency as a trained language (Envato only).
- The eye line and axis as a two-guide template, and the two-to-three-times ratio (yearbook only).
- Styles rather than instances (Conrey only).
- Pacing as density alternation, and the three-versions norm (Yes I'm a Designer only).
- The affordance being removable when position supplies it (Conrey only).

## Where the sources are silent, and the skill is a transfer

This is the section to read before trusting any of it.

- **Nothing here is about progression of meaning.** All four sources sequence a reader's *eye*. None of them asks whether frame four says something frame three did not. That question, which is the spine of this skill, comes entirely from the canon. The claim that a sequence has a shape and that continuation is the common failure is **not a finding from these sources**; it is the canon's turn test applied to a format the canon does not discuss.
- **Nothing here is about one frame at a time.** Print readers see a spread at once and can look back a page for free. A carousel reader sees one frame, then the next, and cannot easily return. Every instruction about seams, open loops and the want position is extrapolated across that gap.
- **No platform rendering.** All four are silent on it, as are all the other design sources in the plugin. Pixel figures derive from `reference/canvas-assumptions.md`, which is itself a reasoned assumption with its derivation shown.
- **No reader testing.** Every judgement in all four sources is the designer's own eye. The recital test exists because of this gap, not because a source proposed it.
- **No optimal length.** Nobody here or in the canon says how many frames a sequence should have. The six-to-twelve range is Josué's working range for the format, not a derivation.
- **No magnitude scale.** Neither the sources nor the canon say how much bigger a late turn should be than an early one. The minor / moderate / irreversible ladder is a sorting instrument, not a measurement.

## The canon's contribution, and the one conflict carried forward

From `craft-skills/craft-canon/references/storytelling.md`:

- **The ordered diagnosis**, which is the most important thing this skill borrowed: the turn test first (does anything actually change, a question a rewrite cannot answer), then the alignment test (is the promise in the currency the body pays in). The order exists to catch a specific failure: re-signposting a sequence where nothing turns produces a bait hook, and that is the most likely failure in any AI-assisted pipeline, because sensation of progress is what a generator has in surplus.
- **Promise, progress, payoff** as three separate objects that have to be in one currency.
- **Magnitude variety**, which the canon states at feed level and this skill applies within a deck. That application is an extension, since the canon explicitly says the rule has little to say inside a single short piece.
- **Pass D, the material gate:** whether anything changed is confirmed by the person it happened to, never proposed on their behalf.

**The conflict carried into position 6.** Sanderson optimises for satisfaction, a reader who feels paid. Dicks deliberately withholds it and optimises for continuation, a reader still carrying the piece. These are not reconcilable in one ending. The skill takes the canon's decision rule unchanged: reply-driven social content takes Dicks and ends on the want; anything whose job is to establish credibility takes Sanderson and resolves, because an audience needing resolution to believe you reads withholding as evasion.

## Sibling dependencies

- **`dsn-balance`, the operator rule.** Tension is tuned per instance and detunes silently under substitution, evidenced by a single demonstration in that skill's sources. This is what decides the hand-tuned against templated split, and `dsn-balance` already states the carousel consequence (first slide tuned, rest from a stable template, hand-tuning slide seven means the template is wrong). This skill carries that consequence and adds where the one permitted break goes, which is my derivation. **How far the operator rule reaches is audited in `dsn-balance/references/audit.md`**; that pointer was carried in `SKILL.md` before the legibility pass and lives here now, since a reader of the procedure cannot resolve it.
- **`dsn-hierarchy`.** Two ranks per body frame, the 2x rank 1 to rank 2 step in feed conditions, the cold-against-tasked reader switch around frame five, and the CTA at rank 1 only on the last frame. The tasked-reader claim is flagged in that skill as single-source and uncorroborated; it is carried here with the same caveat.
- **`reference/canvas-assumptions.md`.** 1080 x 1080 and 1080 x 1350, 3x nominal downscale, 4x stress. The 36px working-file floor and the 25 percent strip render both derive from it.

## The per-canvas arithmetic, and what licenses each figure

Every number in `SKILL.md`'s Applied section is derived from `reference/canvas-assumptions.md` plus one stated choice. **None of it is a source finding, and no source in this corpus or any other in the plugin says anything about platform rendering.** Recorded here so each figure can be challenged at its premise rather than at its result.

|---|---|---|

| 135px top and bottom margin, portrait | 1350 / 10 | Chosen so live height lands on 1080 and the two canvases differ by a single clean figure. |
| 900 x 900 and 900 x 1080 live areas | Canvas minus margins | Follows from the two rows above. |
| The 180px portrait surplus | 1080 - 900 | Arithmetic. The only budget difference between the canvases. |

The whole chain is checkable and none of it is measured. Correcting `reference/canvas-assumptions.md` by the method that file describes, posting one real graphic with a ruler of known pixel widths and measuring it on the target device, invalidates or confirms all of it at once.

## What is not covered at all

- **Video and animated sequences.** Nothing here applies to timed reveals or motion.
- **Sequences longer than about twelve frames.** The position map degrades: a twenty-frame deck needs a chapter structure nobody here teaches.
- **Threads and newsletters**, where the reader can see several units at once and scroll back freely.
- **Whether the claim in the sequence is true.** Shape tells you nothing about truth. That is `argumentation`.
- **Acquisition.** Frame 1 stopping a scroll is treated here as a hierarchy and balance problem. Why someone stops at all is `short-form-writing` and the hook skills.

## Changed by the cross-skill numeric reconciliation

The shared canvas file fixes sizes and downscale factors and fixes nothing about margins, columns or gutters. The framing is recorded in `reference/canvas-assumptions.md`.

- **Margins and live area.** Now says the 90px side margin is derived here from 1080 / 12 rather than inherited, and that every figure in the section rests on it and on the 36px gutter, so a piece built on another skill's margin recomputes rather than imports.

They are in fact derived here, and that file closes a different triple: a 72px margin, a 24px gutter and 136px columns. The passage now says each set starts from a different margin and each closes, that grid derivation is owned there, that a project needing one grid takes the triple from there and recomputes the frame budgets, character ceilings and spans here against it, and that running one file's column width inside another's margin is the thing to refuse.

The two floors in test 12, the checklist and the agent-capability list already carried both the 36px and 48px cases and were left alone.
- **Two remaining bare floor mentions.** The type-scale passage now says "36px nominal-case floor", and the handoff to `dsn-icon` now gives both figures instead of 36px alone. The 1.333 minimum ratio and the 36 / 51 / 72 / 102 / 144 / 204 scale are unchanged.
