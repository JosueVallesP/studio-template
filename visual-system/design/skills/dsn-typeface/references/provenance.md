Where the skill comes from, what each source uniquely gave, where they disagree, and which figures are derived rather than sourced.

Four channels, no shared host, no shared sponsor except Envato promoting its own marketplace. That is better independence than most design topics get. The view spread is extreme, from 667,000 to 41, and views carry no signal about quality here: the 41-view source is the only one that names horizontal stretching as a fault, and the 667,000-view source carries the two clearest factual errors.

## What each source uniquely contributed

**Flux Academy** is the spine. It is the only source with a procedure rather than a taxonomy: write the brief down, start from a go-to list, look for one upgrade, trial and pair, test and test again. It is also the only source that treats licensing, glyph coverage, language support, weight range, width range and drawn-versus-faked italics as selection criteria [00:16:29, 00:25:10 to 00:26:48]. It supplies the only pairing mechanism in the set, the near-match clash [00:34:46], and it is the only source that notices and resolves the distinct-versus-complementary contradiction [00:37:10]. It gives the evaluation-by-comparison method, setting a candidate beside the category classic to make the divergence visible [00:29:39]. All of this is single-channel.

**Envato** is the reference layer. It is the only source with a trait-level account of legibility: x-height, character width, weight, design traits, stroke contrast, counter size [00:22:15 to 00:24:46]. It is the only source covering font file formats and variable fonts [00:19:39 to 00:22:15], the only one giving leading and measure figures, and the only one distinguishing drawn italics from oblique cuts with the reasoning [00:18:47]. It is also the only source that hedges a legibility claim honestly, saying serifs are "generally believed to enhance legibility" [00:23:50].

**Satori Graphics** contributes the role-first opening question, where on the design the face sits, before anything about mood [00:00:00]. It is the only source proposing a time-delayed review, leaving the piece for an hour or two and returning with fresh eyes, plus asking a colleague [00:07:34]. That is the closest anything in this set comes to testing on a reader. Its pairing advice is the weakest in the set.

**LiteGraphix** is mostly a restatement of category names and the two-or-three-font cap, with one thing nobody else says: do not stretch type, because "it kills professionality" [00:13:20]. The reason is not given, and the real reason is mechanical, so the skill keeps the rule and supplies the mechanism. The source also advises beginners to cap at two fonts rather than three, which is a reasonable tightening of the shared convention.

## Corroboration analysis

### Taught by three or four channels, treated as craft

- **The brief, the mood and the audience drive selection.** All four. Flux [00:00:44], Satori [00:00:42], Envato [00:31:52], LiteGraphix [00:12:39].
- **Decide the role slots before the faces.** Three: Flux [00:02:15], Satori [00:00:00], Envato implicitly through its display-versus-body split [00:16:25].
- **Two or three typefaces per layout, maximum.** All four. Flux [00:33:56], Envato [00:35:08], Satori [00:01:25], LiteGraphix [00:11:54]. Most corroborated claim in the set and the one with the least mechanism: every source gives the reason as confusion or mess. Kept as a strong convention, with the mechanism restated as a cap on roles rather than on files.
- **Two weights or widths of one family is the safe default pairing.** All four. Flux [00:32:29], Satori [00:04:13], Envato [00:34:20], LiteGraphix [00:03:28].
- **Display, script and slab faces cannot carry body text.** Three: Envato [00:16:25], LiteGraphix [00:08:47], Flux [00:05:55].
- **Do not mix two faces from the same category that look alike.** Two independent, and the only pairing claim with a mechanism. Flux [00:34:46], Envato [00:35:55].
- **One decorative or display face per layout.** Two: Flux [00:35:35], LiteGraphix [00:09:42].
- **Test by setting real copy at real size and looking at it on the device.** Three in direction, one in procedure. Flux [00:28:58, 00:43:58], Satori [00:07:34], LiteGraphix [00:13:20].
- **A taller x-height reads better at small sizes.** Two: Envato [00:23:00], Flux [00:11:40]. No source cites evidence; the mechanism is plausible, since more of the letterform is available at a given size.

### Taught by one channel, treated as house style

- The written brief on paper before the computer opens (Flux).
- The curated go-to list of twenty to sixty faces grouped by category, built on Vignelli's canon (Flux [00:03:34, 00:04:23]). The claim that a small set covers about 80 percent of projects is his estimate, not a measurement.
- Licensing and budget as gating constraints (Flux [00:16:29]). Single-source, but it is a legal fact rather than a taste claim, so it carries anyway.
- The glyph, language, weight-range and width-range checklist (Flux [00:25:10 to 00:26:48]). Envato corroborates the italic distinction and the weight-range point separately.
- Evaluating by comparison against a category classic (Flux [00:29:39]).
- Weights at least two steps apart when pairing inside one family (Flux [00:32:29]). No source tests it. Treated as house style in the skill, with the instruction to verify it at the real size.
- Choosing one face first and then finding its partner, rather than hunting for a pair (Flux [00:40:10]).
- Building a named type system for the pair (Flux [00:36:19]).
- Trait-level legibility detail: character width, mid weight, neutral design traits, stroke contrast, counter size (Envato).

- Leave the piece for an hour or two and return with fresh eyes (Satori [00:07:34]).

### The balance, stated plainly

Roughly half of the skill's procedure is Flux Academy alone, because it is the only procedural source. The constraint items it supplies, licence, glyphs, weights, widths and italics, are single-channel but factual rather than aesthetic, which is why the skill leans on them. The caps and the category behaviour are multi-channel. All four pairing conventions the skill rejects come from the non-procedural sources. A reader should lean hard on the constraint sheet and the caps, lean moderately on the shortlist method, and treat the pairing advice in the sources with suspicion unless the skill restates it with a mechanism.

## Where sources disagree, and the decision rule

**Does category predict personality?** Envato asserts type psychology by analogy with colour psychology, and maps serif to traditional, formal and serious, sans to clean and contemporary [00:31:52, 00:32:42]. Satori and LiteGraphix assert the same mapping in shorter form. Flux argues against it at length, with a worked case of a current consumer-electronics brand setting headlines in a short-serif face to read as new and design-led [00:09:32 to 00:12:26], and separately places these judgements on an objective-to-subjective continuum rather than treating them as facts [00:24:20 to 00:25:10]. Flux argues with evidence, the others assert. **Decision rule: category predicts legibility behaviour, which is mechanical; category-to-personality mapping is a shortlist filter, not a prediction about a reader.**

**Should pairings contrast or harmonise?** Satori says contrast, "opposites attract" [00:02:51]. Flux says both, and names the contradiction [00:37:10]. **Decision rule: structure wants distance, proportion wants agreement. One rule on two axes.**

**How small can body copy go?** Envato says 8 to 11 points with 12 slightly too big [00:25:34]. That is print-book convention and it is actively wrong on screen and dangerous in a feed. **Decision rule: every size figure belongs to a medium. Re-derive with the small-size render.**

**How many fonts?** Three channels say two to three. LiteGraphix says two for beginners, three once experienced. Taken as two to three, with the stricter reading preferred when nothing in the piece needs a third.

## Conventions versus derivations

**Conventions from the sources, kept as conventions:**
- two or three typefaces per layout, one display face maximum
- leading at 1.25 to 1.5 times the type size (Envato [00:26:22])
- measure at 45 to 70 characters (Envato [00:27:12])
- body copy at 8 to 11 points, print only (Envato [00:25:34])
- weights at least two steps apart (Flux [00:32:29])

**Derived in this skill, not given by any source:**
- the feed floors. An earlier version of this file derived them from a 40 percent ratio, giving body at or above 36 pixels, captions at or above 30 and headings 90 to 140. That ratio has been superseded by the shared 3x downscale in `reference/canvas-assumptions.md`, which fixes a 12 point rendered floor and therefore 12 x 3 = 36 authored pixels for every slot including captions, with 12 x 4 = 48 as the floor for anything that must survive the 4x stress case. The old 30 pixel caption figure is stale and should not be reused.
- the heading band of 85 to 114 pixels for a 36 pixel body. Derived by taking 4 points of rendered separation as the minimum visible step, so 4 x 3 = 12 authored pixels at the bottom of the scale, which rules out a 1.125 ratio (36 to 40.5, a 1.5 point rendered difference) and gives a 1.333 ladder of 36, 48, 64, 85, 114. The 4 point threshold is a working default chosen here.
- the x-height compensation formula: target size x (reference x-height / candidate x-height), worked at 36 x 0.52 / 0.46 = 41 pixels. No source gives a method for compensating, only the observation that x-heights should match.
- the measure target of 35 to 50 characters at feed size. Derived from column width rather than from the downscale ratio: 1080 minus two 80 pixel margins is a 920 pixel column, and at an average character width near 0.5 of type size that holds 920 / 18 = 51 characters at 36 pixel type and 920 / 24 = 38 at 48 pixel type. The 0.5 character-width figure is a working default for a humanist sans.
- the weight floor of regular at body size and medium at caption size, derived from compression behaviour rather than from any source.
- the centred 1080 by 1080 safe area inside a 1080 by 1350 frame, derived from grid thumbnail cropping. No source discusses social crops at all.
- the mechanism behind the stretch rule, that scaling multiplies one axis while a drawn condensed cut redraws both. LiteGraphix gives the rule with no reason.
- the ambiguity check on `Il1`, `0O` and `rn`. Flux mentions `l`, `I` and `1` as an objective legibility fact [00:24:20]; the set and the test are assembled here.
- the compression test. No source mentions platform recompression.
- the glyph-table-not-specimen instruction, and the fallback-substitution reason for treating a missing glyph as a rejection.

## Factual errors in the sources

- Envato credits "william caslen in the 18th century" with the first sans-serif printing type [00:10:53]. The usual attribution is William Caslon IV, around 1816, so the following century. The same passage dates informal sans-serif letterforms to the fifth century BC, which is garbled.
- Envato's body copy range of 8 to 11 points is presented as a general rule of thumb with no medium attached [00:25:34]. It is print-specific.
- Envato states that font families "don't usually come in different widths" [00:19:00]. True of many families and false of the ones worth shortlisting, which is exactly why width range belongs on the constraint sheet.
- LiteGraphix's car and playlist analogies for typeface versus font are muddled in both directions and should not be repeated.

## Commercial interest

Envato's course recommends Envato Elements fonts, Envato font duos and Envato type designers, and uses Envato marketplace assets as every category example. Flux recommends a platform it discloses working with, and discloses it. Both disclosures are in the transcripts. The font duo recommendation is the one place where a commercial interest and a methodological claim coincide, which is why the skill classes duos as folklore rather than as advice.

## Not covered by these sources, and so not covered by the skill

- Type scales and modular ratios. No source supplies one. Covered by `dsn-hierarchy` as a derived figure only.
- Optical sizing, and why a face drawn for text differs from the same family's display cut.
- Hinting, rendering differences across operating systems, and screen rasterisation.
- Non-Latin scripts beyond the instruction to check glyph coverage.
- Variable font axes beyond weight and width.
- Accessibility standards for type: minimum sizes, dyslexia-related research, user font scaling.
- Web font loading strategy, FOUT and FOIT, and fallback metric matching.
- Any reader testing. No source in this set tests a typeface on anyone.

## Changed by the cross-skill numeric reconciliation

The shared floor is two figures, not one: 36px at the nominal 3x downscale and 48px at the 4x stress case, both delivering about 12pt rendered. A floor quoted without its case is incomplete. The framing is established in `reference/canvas-assumptions.md`.

- **Feed checklist, slot-size line.** Previously required every slot at or above 36 pixels with no case named, which understated the file's own computed floor passage two sections earlier. It now names 36 as the nominal case and 48 as the requirement for a piece held to the stress case. The computed floor passage already carried both figures and is unchanged, as is the x-height arithmetic at 36 pixels, which is a nominal-case worked example.
