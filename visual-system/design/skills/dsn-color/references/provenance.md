**Channel balance.** Six sources, four channels. Satori Graphics supplies three of six sources, 55 minutes of the 197-minute total, so 28 percent of runtime. The painter supplies 64 minutes, 33 percent of runtime, and most of it does not apply to screen work. The two remaining channels, each one video, carry the systematic colour-theory curriculum. In practice the usable corpus is four independent voices with one of them tripled, which is weaker corroboration than the five-channel `dsn-grid` corpus. Where `SKILL.md` says "every source" it means all six; where it says "one channel", read Satori unless named.

**View counts carry no signal.** The most-watched source by a wide margin is the least transferable one, and the most rigorous single source, `Tznnrjkhp7E`, has the fewest views of the six.

**Sponsor content.** Two of the three Satori videos contain paid segments inside the runtime: `OB-XwRg1qTc` has roughly a minute on an AI notepad, `HonJiqr7eqY` roughly a minute on an AI design tool. Neither affects the colour teaching, but the effective teaching runtime is shorter than the durations above.

## What each source uniquely contributed

**`wtICBD0Ke_E`, the painter.** Not a colour theory masterclass despite the title. It is a demonstration of using black in oil paint, ending in a promotion for a branded paint tube the teacher has his name on. The colour wheel appears once, dismissed as "a new rule" rather than the only one. Two things in it are load-bearing anyway: the statement of value over hue at 00:07:15, and the relational point at 00:13:57 with a demonstration at 00:15:37 of one mixed green reading brighter or duller depending on its neighbour. Also supplies the mid-tone ground argument at 00:19:36, which `SKILL.md` repurposes for the feed condition.

**`Tznnrjkhp7E`, 4 The Creatives.** The most systematic coverage: the full harmony set, tint, shade and tone with definitions, the 60-30-10 ratio and its four-colour variant, value contrast for legibility, consistency of saturation across a product set, and RGB against CMYK. Also the longest unsupported colour-meaning list in the corpus, roughly eleven colours over twelve minutes.

**`HKtTNOgfhAU`, Satori.** The only source that cites anything: two studies at 00:04:02 and an A/B test at 00:06:55. Also the only source that states the better claim, that saturation and contrast carry the feeling rather than hue, at 00:04:47, and the only one to name cultural variation twice, at 00:07:34 and 00:20:28. Supplies the red-and-green tuning demonstration and the per-scheme cautions about monochrome, complementary, triadic and split complementary.

**`2QTHs7QSR9o`, Envato.** Supplies the two runnable procedures: converting an image to black and white to expose value differences at 00:02:20, and laying a black rectangle over a palette in Color blend mode to compare saturation and value at 00:16:40 onward. Also the clearest additive and subtractive statement, that RGB combines to white and CMYK subtracts from white paper.

**`HonJiqr7eqY`, Satori.** The best single framing of the three dimensions and the diagnosis that beginners move only hue. Supplies the greyscale verdict at 00:02:36, the dominant, supporting and accent role model, the temperature hedge at 00:00:51, colour relativity at 00:10:27 and edge vibration at 00:11:19.

**`OB-XwRg1qTc`, Satori.** Supplies the perceptual job list: hierarchy, attention control, rhythm, grouping, focal point, depth, temperature, visual weight, value, and colour as a code. Also the single most important statement against intrinsic meaning at 00:04:17, and the category-convention argument with the energy-drink shelf at 00:08:05.

## Colour psychology: the full accounting

Four of four sources assert fixed hue meanings. Evidence offered across all six videos:

1. **`HKtTNOgfhAU` 00:04:02.** "in 1974 K.W Jacobs demonstrated red to be more stimulating than green and green more stimulating than blue"; and a 1981 study attributed to Richard Küller on EEG readings and heart rates. Neither is given a journal, sample, or method. Both concern physiological arousal, not graphic design decisions. Treat as suggestive of arousal differences, not as support for any meaning.
2. **`HKtTNOgfhAU` 00:06:55.** A HubSpot test where "the red button outperformed the green button by 21" percent click-through. One site, one audience, one pair of colours, and no control for which colour was the page's existing convention. Not generalisable.
3. **Everything else is illustration:** brands and film posters that used a colour, cited as proof that the colour means something. `Tznnrjkhp7E` runs roughly twelve minutes of this. The only near-evidence inside it is a quoted McDonald's executive saying red "felt aggressive and shouty" and that red had become the category default, which is a statement of rationale and also an argument for convention rather than intrinsic meaning.

Overreach to reject outright:
- **`HKtTNOgfhAU` 00:06:09:** red stimulation producing memory because "it's like mini trauma". Invented mechanism, no citation.
- **`HKtTNOgfhAU` 00:07:34:** "women tend to prefer softer more pastel colors whereas men prefer Bolder colors", unsourced, hedged only as an average.
- **`HKtTNOgfhAU` 00:09:38:** critics of colour psychology are "ignorant" because "psychology especially in terms of color is used by pretty much every major brand". Argument from ubiquity.
- **`HKtTNOgfhAU` 00:17:33:** the Cadbury case. A reported 78 percent of people hearing about the brand on Facebook were female, therefore purple works because purple is feminine. The outcome is used to prove the premise.

Against all of that, two sources undercut intrinsic meaning from inside the genre, and those are the ones `SKILL.md` builds on: the cultural inversions at `HKtTNOgfhAU` 00:07:34 and 00:20:28, and `OB-XwRg1qTc` 00:04:17 stating that the association is trained by category precedent rather than inherent.

## Where sources disagree, and the decision rule

- **Who invented the colour wheel and when.** `2QTHs7QSR9o` says Newton, 1666. `Tznnrjkhp7E` says Newton, early 1700s. Both plausibly refer to the same work at different dates. Rule: do not repeat a date. Nothing in the skill depends on it.
- **Primaries.** `Tznnrjkhp7E` and `2QTHs7QSR9o` state that red, yellow and blue cannot be made from other colours, as fact. Both also teach RGB later in the same video without reconciling the two. Rule: RYB is a subtractive convenience, and the skill says so in the painting table.
- **Number of colours a system can produce.** `Tznnrjkhp7E` gives over 16 million for RGB and "over 16,000" for print. Rule: no figure of this kind appears in `SKILL.md`.
- **What a "tone" is.** `Tznnrjkhp7E` and `2QTHs7QSR9o` say hue plus grey. `HKtTNOgfhAU` 00:26:51 says hue plus white plus black, which is the same thing stated awkwardly. No conflict; the skill uses hue plus grey.
- **The direction of simultaneous contrast.** `HonJiqr7eqY` 00:10:27 says a desaturated orange "will feel warmer and lighter when surrounded by a vibrant bright color, and it will appear darker and more saturated around a cooler tone". That is a garbled or inverted statement of the effect. The phenomenon is corroborated by the painter's demonstration; this source's account of which way it pushes is not reliable. Rule: the skill asserts only that the surround changes the colour, and prescribes the three-ground test rather than a predicted direction.
- **Whether complementary pairs are inherently pleasing.** `Tznnrjkhp7E` says they are "more pleasing to the eye". `HKtTNOgfhAU` says they "can be jarring on the eyes" and need tuning. Rule: Satori, because the claim comes with the tuning demonstration that settles it.

| Number or rule | Status |
|---|---|
| Lightness contrast required for legibility | **Derived.** Perceptual, and testable by greyscale conversion |
| One dominant colour with the rest subordinate | **Derived.** Follows from accent isolation; if everything is loud nothing leads |
| Saturated near-complementaries at equal lightness vibrate | **Derived.** Observable |
| 60-30-10 and 60-20-10-10 | **Convention.** Disclaimed by the source teaching them. The derived part is the ordering, not the percentages |

| Warm advances, cool recedes | **Partly derived,** never isolated from value and saturation in any demonstration available |

## Changed or dropped by the legibility pass

**The saturation sweep method was replaced.** The earlier `SKILL.md` gave the sourced procedure as the test verdict: lay a black rectangle over the set in Color blend mode and fail if one member reads hotter [2QTHs7QSR9o 00:16:40]. The audit had already recorded that this cannot work, because the overlay collapses the frame to lightness and a lightness-only frame cannot show which member is more saturated. The test now fails on a saturation spread above 25 points between non-accent members, which is decidable from a picker. The overlay survives as a lightness check only, and was cut from `SKILL.md` for length rather than on accuracy grounds. **The 25-point figure is this skill's own default, not inherited.**

**Working numbers introduced by the pass, all extrapolation, none sourced.** Marked **(thin)** in `SKILL.md` where they carry a rule, and listed here so they can be corrected in one place: saturation bands per role, dominant S8 to S20, supporting S15 to S30, accent S70 to S90; lightness gaps of 12 points between adjacent regions, 20 points between a supporting panel and primary type, and 25 points between text and the shape behind it; the S60 threshold defining a saturated element for the isolation and vibration tests; the 12-point lightness window and 120 to 180 degree hue window defining edge vibration operationally; the 25 percent area and 15-point lightness pair defining two dominants; the accent band of roughly 0.2 to 3 percent of frame area; and the 48px working-file floor for a coloured mark that must survive the 4x stress reduction. Each is a default chosen so a reader can return a verdict, and a different figure would be equally defensible.

**The relational principle lost its attribution, as intended.** The pass strips the credit recorded in the audit, that the painter introduces "no color exists in a vacuum" as Josef Albers restated rather than as his own arrival. The concept stays in `SKILL.md` as simultaneous contrast, the attribution lives here only, and the point the audit made still holds: the claim reaching both the pigment and the screen side is one twentieth-century teacher restated twice, not two independent arrivals.

**The channel accounting, the corroboration counts and the full colour-psychology evidence list were removed from `SKILL.md`** and are recorded above. `SKILL.md` now carries a confidence paragraph and inline **(thin)** markers in their place.

## Figures and claims deliberately left out

- All hex values. No source states one that was checkable, and a hex quoted off a transcript is unverifiable.
- All wavelength and gamut figures.
- The colour-count figures for RGB and CMYK.
- `Tznnrjkhp7E` 00:16:22: "when objects are further away in real life, they will always appear lighter". Atmospheric perspective stated as a universal. True often, false at night and against a bright sky.
- Pigment names, mixing recipes and product recommendations from `wtICBD0Ke_E`, including the sponsored tube the video exists to sell.
- Software click paths. All six sources spend runtime on InDesign, Illustrator and Photoshop menus. Those age out and are not mechanisms.
- Named palette tools, which three sources recommend. They are not knowledge and `dsn-palette` is the place for tooling if it wants them.

## Not covered by this skill or its sources

Accessibility contrast standards and conformance levels; colour-blind simulation beyond greyscale and redundancy; colour management, profiles and gamut mapping; print reproduction beyond the single fact that screen colour is more saturated than print; spot and Pantone work; colour in motion, video grading and transitions; data visualisation scales, including sequential and diverging ramps; colour tokens, theming and dark-mode systems; gradients and blend modes as a subject; and palette construction, which is `dsn-palette`.

No source addresses the condition that actually governs Josué's output, which is a graphic rendered small inside an app background the designer does not control. The relational principle that makes that problem tractable is sourced; the settings in the applied section are not.

## Changed by the cross-skill numeric reconciliation

The shared floor is two figures, not one: 36px at the nominal 3x downscale and 48px at the 4x stress case, both delivering about 12pt rendered. A floor quoted without its case is incomplete. The framing is established in `reference/canvas-assumptions.md`.

- **Delivery checklist, floor line.** Previously quoted 48px with no case attached, which read as a second floor contradicting test 11 in this same file. Test 11 and the reduction arithmetic already carried both figures correctly, so the checklist was the only defect. It now applies the 36px nominal floor to every coloured element carrying meaning and the 48px stress floor to anything that must be found. No value changed and no arithmetic is downstream of it.
- **Verified and left alone.** The warning that a greyscale read is not a contrast-ratio check, because relative luminance and HSL lightness disagree, is carried here in the terms and in the handoff to `dsn-contrast`, and that file carries the same warning in its own terms, its test 3 and its failure list. The two do not conflict, so neither was edited.
