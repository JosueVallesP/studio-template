# dsn-color: independent audit

Audited 2026-11-06 by an agent that did not write `SKILL.md` or
`references/provenance.md`. Every claim was checked against the transcripts,
never against the provenance file. All six transcripts were consulted:
`color__wtICBD0Ke_E`, `color__Tznnrjkhp7E`, `color__HKtTNOgfhAU`,
`color__2QTHs7QSR9o`, `color__HonJiqr7eqY`, `color__OB-XwRg1qTc`.

Headline: the attribution of this skill is in good order. No invented figures,
no thresholds, nothing attributed to a source that says nothing like it. The
defects are three attributions that misstate what a source argued and two
citations landing one block away from the words they quote.

## Mislabelled

Most damaging first.

### 1. The painting source is given a prohibition it spends an hour rejecting

`SKILL.md`, crossover table: **"Black plus yellow makes green, so never darken
with black | No. A property of specific pigments."**

The painter does not say never darken with black. He says do not rely on black
to darken, and he says the opposite of the prohibition explicitly. The video
exists to argue for black: "today what I want to talk about is black. The reason
why I want to talk about black is because that was a color I was told at my art
school ... never to use" [wtICBD0Ke_E 00:00:47]. His own formulation is "the
thing about black is you don't want to rely on it to darken ... They should have
said, 'Use black, but don't use it just to make something dark'"
[wtICBD0Ke_E 00:02:27], and later, in the middle of the lemon demonstration,
"I'm not saying you can't use black, but maybe you don't rely on the black"
[wtICBD0Ke_E 00:06:20]. The green is real and is his point: "look what color I
get when I darken with black. You get green. That's why they don't want you to
rely on black because black is a color" [wtICBD0Ke_E 00:04:30].

The skill's verdict on transfer is unaffected, because lowering lightness on
screen does not turn yellow green either way. What was wrong is the sentence
attributed to the source.

**Fixed.** The row now states the painter's actual rule.

### 2. The relational principle is attributed to independent convergence when the painter cites Albers

`SKILL.md` principle 3: **"'no color exists in a vacuum' [wtICBD0Ke_E 00:13:57].
The painter and the designers reach this independently."**

The quote is exact and the timestamp is right. The independence is not. The
painter introduces the idea as somebody else's and names him: "I'm going to go
to 20th century to Joseph Albers and Joseph Albers color theory, which is that
no color exists in a vacuum" [wtICBD0Ke_E 00:13:57]. He demonstrates it himself
afterwards, so the demonstration is his, but the claim is Albers restated, and
the Satori statement of colour relativity [HonJiqr7eqY 00:10:27] is the same
lineage reaching the designer side. Two restatements of one twentieth-century
teacher are not two independent arrivals, which is what the sentence claims and
what it is doing work as: the skill calls this crossover "load-bearing" partly
because two traditions are said to meet on it.

**Fixed.** The sentence now records that the painter credits Albers.

### 3. A table of ideas "from the painting source" contains one the painting source never mentions

`SKILL.md`, crossover table: **"Red, yellow and blue are primaries nothing else
can make | No. True of pigment, false of screens. Two sources state it as
universal."**

The table's own column heading is "Idea from the painting source". The word
"primary" and its variants appear zero times in `color__wtICBD0Ke_E`. The two
sources that assert it are `Tznnrjkhp7E` and `2QTHs7QSR9o`, which the row half
admits by saying "two sources" and never names. A reader working down the
column will attribute it to the painter.

**Fixed.** The row now names where the claim comes from.

## Derived but not labelled as derived

1. **"A restrained set with a few moments of high intensity beats abundant
   intense colour."** The painter describes the contrast without ranking it: "if
   you take out the modern pigments or the high chroma pigments, you start to
   have a subtler palette. And a number of old master paintings are in fact
   subtler palettes with moments of color. And we in the 20th and 21st century
   have grown up with abundance of colors" [wtICBD0Ke_E 00:10:24]. The
   preference is implied by the whole video and stated outright by nobody. Mild,
   and the row's verdict is unaffected.
2. **Saturation sweep verdict, "Fail if one member reads hotter."** The method is
   the source's: "creating a black rectangle and setting the transparency to
   color" to "test the saturation" [2QTHs7QSR9o 00:16:40]. The tension is
   inherited from the source, which calls a value-collapsing overlay a
   saturation test. A frame collapsed to value cannot show which member is
   hotter, only which is lighter. Left as is because changing it would be
   rewriting the test rather than correcting an attribution.

## The accusation against the highest-view source, checked

The claim under audit, from `SKILL.md` line 8 and stated flatly in the
provenance: the most-watched source is **"Not a colour theory masterclass despite
the title. It is a demonstration of using black in oil paint, ending in a
promotion for a branded paint tube the teacher has his name on."**

**The accusation holds, on all three parts.**

1. **It is about black, by the teacher's own statement of subject.** "Today what
   I want to talk about is black" [wtICBD0Ke_E 00:00:47]. And at the midpoint,
   describing his own video: "what I'm really trying to talk about here is black,
   about the use of black and how great black is. But this is a demo of maybe
   why not to use black" [wtICBD0Ke_E 00:07:15].
2. **It is oil-paint practice, not colour theory.** The runtime is mixing
   demonstrations, pigment names, imprimatura, grisaille and glazing. The colour
   wheel appears once in 64 minutes, and he sets it aside rather than teaching
   from it: "Chromatic wheels, that's what it tells us and that's the rule. Only
   that isn't the rule that painters used for thousands of years. It's a new
   rule. It's a great rule, but it's not the only rule" [wtICBD0Ke_E 00:11:58].
   One correction to the provenance's wording: he does not dismiss the wheel, he
   calls it a great rule and denies it is the only one.
3. **It ends in a product promotion with his name on the tube.** "I was very
   happy when Tuscan Pine reached out to me to express interest in my ideas for
   colors ... I'm honored to have the Wellington vert noir and tubed"
   [wtICBD0Ke_E 01:02:53], and the closing line of the video is "the Wellington
   vert noir, I think is a one more great tool to add"
   [wtICBD0Ke_E 01:03:37].

So discounting a source with over 1.7 million views is justified here, and the
provenance's broader rule that view counts carry no signal is sound on this
evidence.
