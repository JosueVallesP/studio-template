### Quality warning on the longest source

`gmHcYvlsFvM` was curated because it was the longest and most comprehensive-sounding. It is a live class with continuous audio failure; the screen share fails so the planned demonstration never happens; roughly a third of the transcript is microphone management and an unrelated conversation after the class ends. Its content is branding in general rather than identity systems, it names elements in a list, then spends forty minutes on logo taxonomy. **Do not weight it as primary.** Its three genuine contributions are the logo-removed test, the word-extraction move, and the strategy/identity/visual-identity distinction. This is a curation lesson: duration was used as a proxy for depth and it failed.

## Claims refused

- **Consistency causes commercial success.** "The best brands on the planet are the ones that are most consistent and have most success," asserted twice with no evidence, by the source whose next slide sells a session. **This is the single most load-bearing unevidenced claim in the corpus**, because every consistency rule in every source is justified by it. Never cite it as a reason.
- **Colour psychology.** Asserted as cultural and behavioural association with no mechanism, study or falsifiable example, with the reader routed to the same author's other video. Note the irony: that author's own palette-recall demonstration undercuts the strong form of the claim.
- **Typeface psychology.** Fonts "evoke different emotions," asserted.
- **Values read out of geometry.** A teardown channel attributes freedom, creativity and class to rounded edges and a bow-tie shape, with no access to any brief. The observation that the shape repeats and carries recognition is sound and well-evidenced by their own footage; the meaning attribution is post-hoc interpretation presented as design intent.
- **Children's brands require playful mixed primaries**; purple shows luxury "whatever." The hedge is doing the work of an argument. The adjacent observation that fintech is conventionally blue is a defensible claim about convention and is kept separately.
- **"Branding builds trust. Debate with it. It will build trust."** A dare substituted for evidence.
- **Good packaging means people will buy at any price.** No mechanism, no case, and it supports the price of the class it appears in.
- **Unverifiable income claims** used as proof of method.
- **Canvas ÷ 25 for margins.** Provenance is "I think" he saw it in some brand guidelines and it "sort of just stuck around." Keep the move, refuse the constant.
- **Type scale stepping by 1.618.** The name of the number is the entire justification, and it conflicts with the same author's own type ratios.
- **A line-height ratio measured in pixels off a screenshot** of another studio's render, then promoted to a rule of thumb.
- **Specific colour-proportion percentages.** The idea of governing by proportion is a genuine and under-used contribution; the numbers are invented.
- **A nine-post social mix with an asymmetric tolerance band** that does not parse into a rule.
- **An 8-second attention span, 12 for millennials.** "I think a study says" is the citation; this figure is well-known circulating folklore.
- **Motion durations of 600ms and 250ms.** "Deemed" by whom is never stated. They sit near common UI-motion guidance, so keep them as defaults to test rather than discarding them.
- **Authority-by-quotation.** Two business-book definitions quoted across two videos, their tension resolved by asserting both are true. Repeating a quotable definition is not corroboration. The accompanying three-word framework is an acronym over three ordinary steps, named after its author and sold beside the templates.
- **Haviv, on logos costing millions to change.** No figure, no source, and it is the premise justifying the service his firm sells.
- **Haviv, on flags being the best identifiers because they have been around a long time.** Circular: distinctive things survive, survival is the evidence of distinctiveness. Survivorship is the only metric he offers and it is unavailable at design time.

One honest confession that reclassifies a whole system, and should be read as a model rather than a flaw: "figure out what looks good first and then reverse engineer the maths behind it" [IAdxjZcFm78 00:49:57]. Reverse-engineered notation makes a private judgment transferable. It is not a derivation.

One caution on provenance of the rules themselves: the same author generates guideline prose with Figma AI from deliberately vague input, by his own description doing it "all the time." Some stated rules in his delivered document are therefore machine-expanded from a gesture, which bears on how much each reflects a decision.

## Gaps: what no source covers

1. **How to invent a distinctive asset. The largest hole.** The corpus proves non-logo assets carry recognition and explains why they can, and gives no procedure for generating one. The nearest thing is deriving a pattern from the logo, which is the opposite of an asset that survives the logo's removal.
2. **Accessibility beyond eyeballed contrast.** No WCAG, no ratio target, no colour-vision deficiency, no tooling, conspicuous in a corpus this concerned with colour pairings.
3. **Trademark clearance as a workflow step.** Only the canon source, and only as a story beat. Every instructional source delivers marks to paying clients without mentioning it.
4. **Governance over time.** Who owns the document, who approves a change, how drift is detected, how a version is retired. A version number on a cover is the only gesture in twelve sources, and nobody audits live material against the guidelines.
5. **Measuring whether an identity worked.** One source introduces brand salience as a metric and then measures nothing. The canon source's evidence is survival at fifty-seven years, a lagging indicator. No leading indicator is proposed by anyone.
6. **Tokens and engineering handoff.** The chain from identity designer to developer is described purely as obedience, with no artefact format. Nobody mentions tokens, a shared source of truth, or what happens when the document and the codebase disagree. `dsn-tokens` covers this from a different corpus.
7. **Sonic identity.** Asked once, never answered.
8. **Naming.** Asserted to matter, no method to generate or test one.
9. **Internationalisation.** The best observation in the corpus arrives almost by accident: a slanted bar and its angle survive translation into another script, so recognition does not depend on the Latin wordmark. Nobody, including its source, develops it. No multi-script pairing, no RTL, no culturally variable colour.
10. **Environmental and spatial application.** Again observed in passing and never taught: shop fixtures and interiors carrying a brand's shape language.
11. **Sub-brand and portfolio architecture.** One badge variant for a sub-section is the extent of it.
12. **Motion implementation detail.** Three easing concepts and two durations, then it stops. No curve values, no duration token set, nothing on reduced-motion preferences.
13. **Font licensing scope.** Purchase links only. Nothing on desktop versus web versus app, seat counts, or the client's agency needing the font.
14. **Dark mode as a system** rather than a second palette. Built by one source with no mapping rules; the failure case is raised by another and left unresolved.
15. **Scoping the work.** Price tiers are sales mechanics, not estimation. Nobody says how long a system takes or how to scope by touchpoint count, despite one source's own framing inviting exactly that.
16. **What to do when the strategy is wrong.** Every source treats a signed strategy as fixed. Only the canon source depicts the case where the client's self-understanding is the obstacle, and his answer, refuse for six months, is not a method.

## Known weaknesses

- **Eight voices, and the most complete mechanics come from one of them.** The layout system, the colour ramps, the proportional margin and the type scale are one channel, sponsored, with self-admitted reverse-engineered numbers.
- **The canon tier is one 19-minute conference talk.** It carries the regime distinction and the vessel thesis, which is a lot of weight for one source, though it converges independently with `dsn-mark`'s canon source, and that convergence is the strongest thing in either skill.
- **The only non-funnel channel is also the one that reads meaning out of geometry** with no access to briefs. Its observations are strong and its interpretations are not.
- **The motion section is entirely one voice** and is marked as such in the skill.
- **No source audits a live identity in the wild.** Every case study ends at delivery or at a teardown of a brand that already succeeded.
- Not run on live client work.

## Changed by the cross-skill numeric reconciliation

The shared floor is two figures, not one: 36px at the nominal 3x downscale and 48px at the 4x stress case, both delivering about 12pt rendered. A floor quoted without its case is incomplete. The framing is established in `reference/canvas-assumptions.md`, which also records that nothing shared fixes margins, columns or gutters.

- **Body type, from the floor up.** Previously called 36 pixels "the absolute floor" without naming a case, then showed 48 as the figure that survives the stress case without naming it a floor. It now names both floors, says they deliver the same 12pt, and says that every later mention of the floor states which one it means. The two later mentions, 23 pixels being below the 36 pixel floor and body sitting at 48 on the floor, are now unambiguous without being reworded.
- **The measure.** Now says the 128px margin is derived from this file's own spacing unit rather than taken from the canvas file, that every measure figure rests on it, and that `dsn-grid` closes a different column on 72.
- **Values unchanged.** 1080 - 256 = 824 still closes, as do 824 / 144 = 5.7 characters at 288, the 137-rounded-to-136 reverse derivation, the two-level resolution of 120 and 48 at a ratio of 2.5, and the spacing unit of 64 with its 64 / 128 / 192 / 256 / 320 scale.
- **One remaining bare floor mention.** The two-level resolution now says body sits at 48 pixels on the stress-case floor rather than "on the floor". No value changed.
