## Corroboration analysis

**Taught independently by several channels, so treat as real mechanisms:**

- **Weight is optical, not geometric.** Envato [00:00:43], Canva [00:00:46], Satori [00:01:27]. Three channels, two of them with no shared lineage.
- **The register break.** Satori contrast [00:04:32, 00:04:35, 00:06:41, 00:08:10], KOBEL [00:03:08], Aziev's broken-rule frame, Envato's variety framing [00:18:02]. Four sources in three domains, including one outside graphic design. The best-corroborated mechanism here.
- **Subtractive weight / isolation.** KOBEL [00:10:28] and Satori [00:04:35, 00:14:43] arrive at it from different directions (a room, a page).
- **Learn the rule before breaking it.** Aziev [00:04:07] and Envato [00:00:43] state it independently.
- **Repetition then one break.** Satori [00:00:44, 00:04:32] and Envato [00:06:30].

**Single-channel, so treat as house style until corroborated:**

- The four-fields check, including diagonal and foreground/background: Canva only [00:01:38].
- Near-symmetry as the worst position: Canva only [00:05:56].
- The counterforce requirement and payment in another currency: Aziev only [00:06:39, 00:10:10]. Load-bearing in `SKILL.md` despite being single-source, because Aziev also demonstrates the failure case [00:09:22]; that demonstration is the strongest internal evidence in the set.
- Displacement as a separation layer: Aziev only [00:10:10].
- The grounding element: Envato only [00:13:16].
- "Cousins, not siblings": Envato only [00:16:21].
- Asymmetry resolving through hierarchy: Satori only [00:19:12].
- Drop anchor as an ordering rule: KOBEL only [00:11:47].

**This skill's own inferences, not source claims:**

- Applying repetition-then-one-break *across slides in a sequence* rather than within one frame.
- The operator rule as a general gate on templating and crews. Derived from Aziev [00:09:22], where restoring space destroys the effect.

- Platform crop behaviour for feed thumbnails. Not covered by any source; verify on the platform.

## Where the sources disagree, and the decision rule

### 1. Whether asymmetry or symmetry is the default

Canva says asymmetric designs "are usually preferred" because symmetry is predictable and offers no surprise [00:23:08]. Aziev says the reverse: tension is risky, not for beginners, learn the rules first [00:04:07]. Envato sits between: understand the principles, then break them [00:00:43].

**Decision rule: they are answering different questions, and the conflation is the error.** Canva's "asymmetry" is asymmetric *balance*, which still resolves. Aziev's "tension" is a composition left deliberately unresolved. Those are different points on the axis, with different costs and different failure modes. Asymmetric balance is a safe default and Canva is right about it. Unresolved tension is not a default and Aziev is right about that. Neither source draws the line; drawing it is what makes the pair usable, and it is why `SKILL.md` opens with a three-point axis rather than a binary.

### 2. What the word "tension" means

Four incompatible uses:

- Canva: the attention-pull of a contrast accent [00:15:41, 00:20:11]
- Aziev: a composition held in an unresolved state [00:03:12]
- Envato: variety [00:18:02]
- KOBEL: a register violation [00:03:08]

**Decision rule: Aziev's is the specific, mechanically useful sense, and this skill uses it.** The others describe emphasis and contrast, which are real but are different jobs.

**Caption note:** in the Canva passage around [00:20:11]–[00:21:00] the transcript alternates between "a tension" and "attention" within a few lines, so which word was spoken is unrecoverable. Either reading supports the same mechanism, and the ambiguity is a fair illustration of why the two concepts get merged.

### 3. Whether method removes iteration

Satori claims design principles remove trial and error, and that trial and error "just wastes your time" [00:28:27]. The canon holds the opposite: cheap iteration is the structural advantage of the medium [Maeda, craft design.md rule 24].

**Decision rule: the canon wins, and Satori's claim is self-refuting here specifically.** Perceived weight is optical and cannot be computed from the file, so the one thing balance absolutely requires is looking at renders. Principles narrow the search; they do not replace it.

### 4. How an unresolved frame gets held together

Not a disagreement, three tools:

- Aziev: a counterforce, a stronger grouping signal [00:06:39]
- Canva: a counterweight on the opposite side [00:13:33]
- KOBEL: an anchor that governs everything else [00:11:47]

**Decision rule, by symptom:** if the reader cannot tell what belongs to what, you need a counterforce. If the frame tips, you need a counterweight. If nothing dominates, you need an anchor. Diagnose before reaching.

## Numbers: convention versus derivation

**No source in this set gives a single ratio, step scale or threshold for balance or tension.** Anyone quoting one is quoting something else.

- **Convention worth carrying:** a larger proportion of open space reads as minimal, modern and expensive, while a denser frame reads as informative and serious [Satori 00:16:13]. No source derives it or offers a ratio. Treat it as a genre signal, not a setting.
- **Vocabulary, no numbers:** Satori's macro/micro and passive/active space distinction [00:16:13, 00:17:44], meaning space that aids legibility versus space that controls reading order.
- **Caption-level figures:** KOBEL's 90%-then-last-10% sourcing figure. Do not quote it.
- **The largest gap in the mechanics:** all six list the weight inputs; none says how much saturation buys how much area. This is why the blur test rather than a formula is the instrument.

## Canon rules the skill leans on

These come from the studio's design canon, not the six videos.

- **Review order is semantics, then syntax, then understandability, then visual power** [Vignelli, craft design.md rule 5]. Balance is syntax, checked third. A flawlessly weighted frame making the wrong point fails at the first gate, and most review of visual work runs this list backwards.
- **Scale is a relationship, not a dimension** [craft design.md, discount list]. It cannot be briefed numerically, which is the formal reason an agent cannot judge weight.
- **The urge to add tension is information about your ego.** Bierut names the feeling exactly: the resolution's only defect is that nobody will think he was clever [craft design.md rule 10]. Tension is precisely the lever that makes a maker look clever.
- **Write tolerance bands as prohibitions, not aspirations** [Scher, craft art-direction.md rule 11]: no curves, no heavy drop shadow, no two widths adjacent.
- **One dumb thing plus variations, and the recognition-without-the-mark test** [Scher, craft art-direction.md rule 11]. This is the quiet argument pushing a *body* of work toward one fixed asymmetric structure: tension cannot be the house style because tension cannot be repeated without going slack.
- **Loose, taut, snapped** [Encina, craft art-direction.md rule 5]. Built for conceptual distance, transfers cleanly to compositional distance. Explicit that how far is too far is not answerable by rule.
- **Pushification: stop at the edge of your judgment and say that is where you stopped** [Sagmeister, craft art-direction.md rule 16]. The honest stopping point for tension, because nobody in either tier can state a maximum.
- **Identification beats image; the cheap route to timeless is to already look slightly dated** [craft design.md rules 16, 26]. Supports the symmetry call on brand assets from a different direction.
- **Cheap iteration is the medium's structural advantage** [Maeda, craft design.md rule 24].
- **Aptness is the named worst model capability** [craft art-direction.md, needs-a-human]. Why choosing *which* register break cannot be delegated.

## Cross-domain verdict on the interiors source

**It earned a narrow place and was mis-sized.** Judged honestly rather than assumed either way.

**What transfers to a flat canvas:** dominance is zero-sum [00:18:59]; the subtractive route to weight, remove everything sharing the hero's attribute rather than enlarging it [00:10:28], which is the same mechanism as a flat field making one photographic element land [Satori 00:04:35] and is stated more sharply here; drop anchor as a procedure order [00:11:47]; never judge an element alone, because a colour that reads wrong in isolation can be the one making the assembly work [00:13:15, 00:04:23]; the deliberate register break as the tension generator, with too-pretty stated as a failure condition [00:03:08]; and the fine line itself, too balanced is boring and too far feels askew [00:11:47].

**What does not transfer, and this is the larger half:**

- **Weight in a room is partly real mass and real function.** A chair's presence includes that you can sit in it. On a canvas weight is purely optical, so their intuitions about which object dominates rest on physics a canvas does not have.
- **A room has no frame, no fixed viewpoint and no reading order.** Balance there is recomputed from every standing position, and they explicitly compose across lines of sight between rooms [00:17:38]. That is the biggest non-transfer, and it means this source is **structurally silent on edge pressure**, one of the strongest tension devices on a canvas, because a room has no edge.
- **Provenance does not transfer.** "The old dirty that adds soul" [00:24:26] is material history a flat graphic cannot have, and rendering age reads as a style choice rather than age. Their most-repeated tension source is unavailable.
- **Nor does their sourcing method.** Holding a project at 90% and finding the last pieces by antiquing [00:13:59] depends on physical discovery of unrepeatable objects. A graphic has no basement to find a table in.
- **Their core philosophy is the opposite of a publishing cadence:** don't rush, wait for the bespoke piece, spaces are acquired over time [00:06:21].

**The verdict:** not a mis-pick, but a ten-minute source stretched over thirty-four. It is a client-work podcast, roughly three quarters sourcing stories, client anecdote and children's rooms with nothing extractable, and it teaches nothing about the frame, the edge or the reading order, which is most of what balance means on a canvas. Its real value is corroboration: the register-break mechanism arrived at independently in a domain with different economics, different materials and different viewing conditions is the strongest evidence in this set that the mechanism is real rather than a graphic-design convention. **Keep it for that. Do not add a second interiors source.**

## Not covered by these sources

- **Measurement of any kind.** Every claim about what balance *achieves* (satisfaction, stability, stopping a scroll, feeling grounded) is asserted from examples. Nobody tests anything, and no ratio, threshold or step scale appears anywhere in the six.
- **Motion and sequence within a frame.** All six treat a static frame. Balance under animation, and tension created by timing rather than placement, are absent.
- **Accessibility.** Contrast appears throughout as a weight input and never once as a legibility standard. Nothing on contrast ratios, and some of the strongest tension devices here (low-saturation accents, near-contact, type at the frame edge) cut directly against legibility. Check that separately.
- **Right-to-left and non-Latin layout.** Mirroring and diagonal balance carry real directional meaning in RTL composition and no source raises it. Every example is left-to-right.
- **Responsive and multi-breakpoint balance.** A frame balanced at one aspect ratio is a different frame at another, and nothing here addresses recomposing across sizes beyond the thumbnail problem.
- **Platform crop behaviour** for feed thumbnails, which decides whether edge pressure survives delivery.
- **Data visualisation.** Where balance competes with honest axes these principles are actively dangerous; the canon's no-stylised-accuracy rule governs instead.
- **Long-form editorial across many spreads,** where balance is a property of the sequence rather than the page.
- **A model for how the weight inputs trade against each other.** All six list them; none says how much saturation buys how much area.

## Numbers this pass derived, and where each one comes from

All of these are new in `SKILL.md` and none comes from the six sources, which are silent on platform rendering. They are derived from `reference/canvas-assumptions.md` so the arithmetic in the applied section can be followed and corrected in one place.

- **Margin 90px.** 1080 / 12. Renders at 30pt nominal, 22.5pt at the stress test.
- **Live area 900 x 900 and 900 x 1170.** Canvas minus two margins.
- **Type and spacing base 48px, ratio 1.5.** 48 is 12pt x the 4x stress factor, so it is the floor for anything that must survive the stress test. The ratio is coarse because a 1.125 step collapses to two rendered points, per the canvas assumptions.
- **Leading at 1.1x type size.** Convention, inherited rather than derived. Any value between about 1.05 and 1.2 would serve.
- **Void at 59 percent of live height.** 529 / 900, where 529 is what remains after a 371px mass derived from the type stack above.
- **Column split 568 / 48 / 284.** (900 - 48) / 3 = 284, a 2 to 1 split.
- **Character width at roughly half the type size.** Inherited convention for a typical sans, used only to estimate measure. Varies by typeface by a wide margin.
- **Measure comfortable between about 45 and 75 characters.** Standard typographic convention, not from these sources.
- **Ink coverage of a headline at roughly 25 percent.** A construction, chosen for this skill. Marked **(thin)** in the body. It affects only the tie-detection arithmetic, never a final call.
- **Avatar display at about 48pt, giving a 22.5x downscale.** An estimate of the same kind as the 3x feed figure and correctable the same way, by posting one real asset containing a ruler of known widths and measuring it.
- **Optical centre sitting slightly above geometric centre.** Standard convention. No source in the set mentions it.
