## Claims refused, and why

Assertions carried by no evidence or mechanism in any source. Named here so they are not quietly reintroduced.

- **Shape psychology.** Squares read reliable, circles read community, angles read speed, emblems read togetherness "due to the roundness" [l9_BM1opTj8 00:08:28, 00:23:25]. Support offered: famous examples, chosen after the fact. The source defers the evidence question to "loads of websites out there".
- **Simple marks cause emotional rather than cognitive processing, and emotion causes spending.** Presented as insider knowledge [w0M3Z3oTh08 00:01:28]. No evidence, and the transcript is unreliable here.
- **Simplicity produces trust.** Asserted for tech, wellness and finance [g2bSztQ_Q7g 00:05:25]. The calibration move built on it is useful; the causal claim is not supported.
- **Misalignment produces distrust** [_Hj691jSkDI 00:01:18]. The optical demonstrations in that source are excellent; this claim is not one of them.
- **Straight edges for man-made, curves for organic/living** [g2bSztQ_Q7g 00:07:44, l9_BM1opTj8 00:09:57]. Stated as a general rule by one channel twice. Convention, not principle.
- **Asymmetry reads as organic, alive and personal** [g2bSztQ_Q7g 00:01:39]. Assertion.
- **B2B permits more corporate, B2C more stylistic** [Umin1iXhsn0 00:02:35]. Assertion.
- **"99% of the time your second iteration will be better than the first"** [l9_BM1opTj8 00:21:55]. Rhetorical figure, not a measurement. The underlying instruction, do not ship the first iteration, is sound and corroborated.
- **Simplicity causes memorability**, evidenced by Nike, Apple, McDonald's and Microsoft being simple [l9_BM1opTj8 00:26:40]. Circular, and survivorship: famous marks are sampled because they are famous. Replaced in the skill by the 30-minute redraw, which can actually fail.
- **The Nike wordmark is Futura Condensed Extra Black** [l9_BM1opTj8 00:00:00]. Plausible and widely repeated, but caption-level and unverified here. Do not cite publicly without checking.

### The accrual argument, and who makes it

The reframe that opens the skill, that a mark is a container filled by what the brand does afterwards, is Michael Bierut's, in the Vox piece [`RBTiTcHm_ac`]. Thirteen of the fourteen sources optimise the artifact; this one says the artifact is close to beside the point, and it is the only source in the corpus whose position rests on a documented case rather than on assertion. The body now states the position without the attribution.

- Marks are "empty vessels in a way and then you pour the meaning into them" [RBTiTcHm_ac 00:03:19].
- The swoosh case: commissioned from a design student, the founders did not much like it, and it "was nothing the day it was drawn." It came to mean something because the shoes were good and the marketing spent years associating the product with athletic achievement.
- The general claim: meaning is not "inherent in these shapes" but is "about what those shapes have come to represent in the minds of the people who are looking at them" [RBTiTcHm_ac 00:01:21-00:02:00].
- The diving-versus-swimming test, which the skill's "The ultimate test" section now carries unattributed: arguments about new logos "think they're judging a diving competition but actually all these organizations are in swimming competitions. It's not what kind of splash you make when you hit the water, it's how long you keep your head above that water" [RBTiTcHm_ac 00:03:56].
- The deployment-has-changed argument, now in Applied: "none of that's as important as an email signature or your Twitter avatar or the little thing that sits next to your url," and those "can be changed at the drop of a hat" [RBTiTcHm_ac 00:02:38].
- The logo-system category: "a graphical framework that can have endless permutations," with the Clinton H built so its forms could hold any colours rather than two [RBTiTcHm_ac 00:02:00-00:03:19]. Reached independently in substance by Collect & Contrast, designing for variability so a mark "can also kind of function like a design asset rather than just a logo mark that you have to slap on everything" [tFasmqbn3pE 00:02:32-00:03:04].

The body's old heading for this material, "The sources disagree about what a mark is for", was build narrative. It is now "Choosing what the mark has to do", and the disagreement is presented as a decision the reader makes between two jobs with a stated trade-off.

## Known weaknesses

- **Two voices supply six of fourteen sources.** The optical, letterform and canon material comes from outside that concentration, but the process material leans on Satori and Paterson.
- **No source is a type foundry or a lettering specialist.** The best letterform critique in the corpus comes from a practitioner who twice states he is not an expert at the typeface style he is correcting [mJ_NPQYD8bw 00:03:50, 00:10:21]. That honesty is why it is trusted, but the material is not authoritative on lettering. A dedicated lettering source would strengthen this skill more than anything else.
- **The canon tier is one 5-minute news segment.** It carries the judgment layer and sets the ultimate test, which is a lot of weight for one short source. Pentagram, Chermayeff & Geismar and Vignelli material exists at length and should be added.
- **No source demonstrates a mark failing in the wild.** Every case study ends at client approval. Duration is the skill's stated merit criterion and nothing in the corpus tests it.
- **The avatar case is reasoned, not sourced.** One source names app icon and favicon as constraints [Umin1iXhsn0 00:03:50]; Bierut names the avatar as the modern primary placement [RBTiTcHm_ac 00:02:38]. The specific 32-48px circular guidance is inference from those two plus the reduction ladder, not instruction from any source.
- **Not run on live client work.**

## Changed by the cross-skill numeric reconciliation

The shared floor is two figures, not one: 36px at the nominal 3x downscale and 48px at the 4x stress case, both delivering about 12pt rendered. A floor quoted without its case is incomplete. The framing is established in `reference/canvas-assumptions.md`.

- **The worked monogram, thickness unit.** The sentence calling 36px "this plugin's working-file floor" named no case. It now says that 36 is the floor at the nominal downscale, that the stress-case floor is 48, and why t is deliberately not set at 48: a stroke has to be seen rather than read, and the ladder check holds it instead. That check already computed t at the 4x reduction as 36 / 4 = 9px and is unchanged, as are s = 54, the gap series 54 / 108 / 162, H = 360 and the overshoot range of 5.4 to 7.2px.
