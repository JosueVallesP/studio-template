# dsn-poster: provenance

Where everything in `SKILL.md` came from, how well corroborated it is, which claims are historical positions rather than tested findings, where the sources disagree, and what none of them reach.

## Corroboration summary

Five sources across four independent channels. Gareth David Studio supplies two, so **the real channel count is four, not five**, and anything taught only by those two videos is one channel's house style. Two of the remaining three are scholarly lectures: an 88-minute art-historical lecture on the New Typography and a 48-minute curator's talk on the Swiss grid. That is more scholarly weight than any other skill in the plugin carries. `SKILL.md` does not say so, and should not: corroboration counts belong here.

The sourcing has an unusual shape, and it matters when deciding how hard to lean:

- **The two long sources are historical and critical, not instructional.** They describe what a movement argued and why. They contain almost no procedure. Everything procedural in `SKILL.md` is either derived from their reasoning or imported from the three short practitioner sources.
- **The three short sources are instructional and shallow.** Together they give roughly 35 minutes of material, two of it from one channel.
- So the skill's depth comes from the lectures and its mechanics come from the tutorials, and neither group can check the other. Where they happen to agree, the agreement is worth more than usual because the two groups share no lineage.

What more than one independent channel teaches, and is therefore safe:

| Claim | Channels |
|---|---|
| A poster's permitted complexity is set by where it will be seen | Gareth David (both), Swiss grid lecture, Tschichold lecture |
| One primary element, then a secondary, then supporting elements | Gareth David (both), Swiss grid lecture |
| Clarity of communication is the thing being optimised | all four channels |
| Type alone can carry a whole composition | Swiss grid lecture, Noel Leu |
| Starting from the format's own existing examples produces category-shaped work | Noel Leu, plus `dsn-composition`'s sources |
| A poster is a one-shot medium read at a glance | all four channels |

What rests on a single channel, and is flagged inline in `SKILL.md`:

- The four-scale hold, character to word to line to layout (Noel Leu only)
- The four sculpture failure modes (Noel Leu only)
- The register pair, Swiss against Japanese roadworks signage (Noel Leu only)
- The subject-blind failure, "could be Spanish as well" (Noel Leu only)
- Footer elements legitimately living in the margin and read outward or downward (Gareth David only)
- Lowercase building mass in an all-type poster (Swiss grid lecture only)
- Reserving a blank zone for overprinting, the tip-on economy (Swiss grid lecture only). This is the ancestor of the variable zone in `SKILL.md`; the hard-cap rule attached to it, that a zone allowed to run to a second line invalidates the gap ladder below it, is derived from this skill's own spacing arithmetic and is not in the source
- The four decidable criteria now attached to the four-scale hold in `SKILL.md`, namely counters staying open at 4x, visible letter gaps within a fifth of the widest gap, an intended edge out by no more than half a character width, and the blur silhouette, are **constructed here**. The source names four failure modes (tips over, no harmony, wrong proportions, wrong dynamic) and gives no criterion for any of them, so they were unusable as written: a reader could not decide a verdict from them

Nothing in `SKILL.md` is derived from view counts. For the record: the two Gareth David videos have 190k and 380k views, the Swiss grid lecture 26k, the Tschichold lecture 7.6k, and the Noel Leu lecture 2.6k. **The ranking by views is the exact inverse of the ranking by depth.**

## The sources

**St Bride Library, "Jan Tschichold and the New Typography with Paul Stirton"** (`81_mCjDU5IE`, 88 min, 7.6k views, 2021-11-19). **The canonical and most authoritative source in the set**, and the only one connected to the scholarly literature. A survey of the movement that produced modern asymmetric typographic layout, drawn from Tschichold's own study collection. Its value here is the reasoning behind the movement's claims and what its advocates were reacting against, not its history.

Uniquely supplies: the ten statements of *elementare typographie*, of which five are discussed, including "the new typography is purposeful", so it could never be casual or passive, and that the purpose is communication, where "the message must appear in its shortest, simplest, and most urgent form" [00:21:25]; the elemental means being letters, numbers, signs and rules from the type case plus the precise image, with sans-serif as the elemental letterform and later "there is no excuse for any other forms" [00:22:31]; vertical and diagonal directions used simultaneously to increase urgency, and reading order abandoned where the message suits it [00:23:27]; "elemental design excludes the use of any ornament", with *any* in bold and no exception permitted [00:23:27]; and that elemental design is never definitive, so typographic design will constantly change, which the lecturer reads as a doctrine of permanent revolution [00:23:27].

Also uniquely supplies the movement's internal disagreement about what ranks first. Lissitzky's manifesto held that the visual characteristics of the page matter more than the legibility of the text, and asked for "economy of expression, optics rather than phonetics" [00:17:16], while Moholy-Nagy opened from the opposite end with typography as an instrument of communication that must be as clear and effective as possible [00:05:36]. Tschichold's own 1928 book is a practical handbook subtitled for printers, full of do-this-not-that instruction [00:28:38].

**The single most load-bearing passage for this skill is the author's own scope limit.** Tschichold later concluded the New Typography was "best suited to advertisements where labels, logos, catchphrases, and the juxtaposition of short dramatic text and imagery" apply [01:26:24], never totally abandoned it but believed "it was perhaps only suitable for certain types of work" [01:20:54], and returned to classical typography for books. That is a boundary drawn by the method's own author around exactly the problem this skill addresses, and it is the licence for the whole transfer.

Two production details worth keeping. The Phoebus Palast cinema posters were sometimes produced in 24 hours, with only one or two publicity photographs available, and all the lettering was hand drawn rather than set [00:24:32, 01:08:16]. The method survived extreme constraint, which is the practical argument for it. And Futura arrived in 1927 and the Leica in 1925 [00:31:23], so the movement's formal possibilities were partly an equipment story.

**Poster House / SoDA Baltimore, "The Swiss Grid", Angelina Lippert** (`e3rVZIA0Ab0`, 48 min, 26k views, 2021-04-03). A museum curator on the Swiss grid tradition. The grid mechanics belong to `dsn-grid`; what this skill takes is the poster reasoning around them.

Uniquely supplies: the attention mechanism stated plainly, that pre-lithographic broadsides required literacy, were drab, lacked dynamism, and "unless you were right up on them and a particular word caught your eye they were pretty easy to ignore" [00:06:44]; the constraint that produced the method, where four official languages meant the same poster re-set repeatedly and translated phrases occupy different amounts of space, so a system that solved placement once was worth building [00:09:32, 00:12:03]; designers reframed as "communication engineers rather than just artists" [00:10:22]; planning first, with two questions, what problem must be solved and what message must be presented, before any field count [00:21:53]; and the standardised welt format, which even the later rebels stayed inside [00:25:31].

**The load-bearing passage for type as image** is Vivarelli's advice to Müller-Brockmann that even pure geometry was too subjective and he should turn the text itself into the image and nothing more [00:20:25], with the all-type series that followed: lowercase so that line spacing and letterforms condense into a weightier block, and red reserved for the series title and the headline performers rather than the content generally [00:21:10].

Also supplies the tip-on economy: a reserved blank rectangle, printed once in quantity, overprinted or pasted with each week's dates [00:27:54]. A declared variable zone inside a fixed frame, which is template thinking and transfers directly.

Two cautions this source volunteers against itself. The style's dominance was institutional: the designers winning the national competitions were the professors teaching the style at the two schools and the people the museums hired to advertise their own shows, which she calls a cycle of perpetuating one harmonious style [00:16:29]. And the modern claim that the grid "remains a go-to tool for corporate and governmental communication" [00:10:22] is an adoption fact, not evidence that it communicates better.

**Strelka Institute, "Noel Leu, The Power of Typography: How Text Controls Our Impressions"** (`UGRkCRkgS-I`, 61 min, 2.6k views, 2017-11-14). A Swiss type designer, co-founder of a type foundry. Loosely structured and heavily autobiographical, so most of its runtime is not usable, but four passages are among the sharpest material in the plugin.

Uniquely supplies: the floor argument for type-led work, that Swiss modernism "tried to have maximal impact with minimal design" and "if you do graphic design the minimum you can do is typography" [00:20:27]; the letterform as a two-dimensional sculpture, where the surrounding space is part of the sculpture rather than the gap after it, so typography is "a sculpture in sequence" that must work as single character, word, sentence, text and layout [00:21:22, 00:22:22, 00:23:25]; the four sculpture failures, statically incorrect so it tips over, no harmony, wrong proportions, wrong dynamic [00:21:22]; the subject-blind failure, diagnosing a Russian language textbook as having no discernible page structure or hierarchy and nothing Russian in it, so "this could be Spanish as well" and "it's just a very generic layout that is not visualizing culture" [00:43:31, 00:44:18]; the register pair, a Swiss roadworks sign against its Japanese equivalent, same information, one aggressive and one apologetic [00:52:19 to 00:55:02]; and the self-reference trap, where every previous year's poster for a poster festival shared one attitude and language despite different authors, each fitting itself into the succession, and his method for escaping it was to source the register from a different visual culture entirely [00:48:06, 00:49:05].

His claim that a triangle is more aggressive than a rectangle because it is pointy [00:52:19] is **assertion, not finding**, and `SKILL.md` marks it. His framing that typography both reflects and determines a culture [00:10:58], supported by a chain of comparisons between European and Japanese swords, helmets, roofs and shrines, is a suggestive argument and not a demonstration. The graphology reference at [00:04:22], treated as a science, is discredited and nothing in `SKILL.md` rests on it.

**Gareth David Studio, "What Makes A Good Poster Design?"** (`Urac4u6GngM`, 10 min, 380k views, 2022-04-04). Five factors: hierarchy, placement, dynamic use of type and image, balance, and a specific impression.

Uniquely supplies the placement argument, which is the only claim all four channels reach independently: a poster is not one size fits all, and where it hangs determines its permitted complexity, so an outdoor or billboard placement seen fleetingly needs larger elements, higher contrast and more space, while a gallery, office, school or station placement where someone can spend time permits more information [00:04:58]. This is the direct ancestor of the dwell budget in `SKILL.md`, though **the seconds figures are not his**; he gives no numbers and they are constructed here.

Also supplies the three-level ladder as a poster-specific minimum, primary hook, secondary hook and supporting elements, with the primary defined as the element with the most clarity, legibility and contrast [00:02:09], and the competing-voices analogy for a flat field [00:01:24]. The ladder itself is `dsn-composition`'s territory.

**Gareth David Studio, "Anatomy of Poster Design"** (`mQaKXbLgWyg`, 14 min, 190k views, 2024-02-23). A taxonomy in three parts: foundation (surface area, margin, grids), visual elements (colour, shape, image, typography, surface texture, iconography), and composition (hierarchy, primary hook, secondary hook, supporting elements, space, form, call to action, footer elements).

It is a checklist rather than a method, and the author says so, noting that not every part needs to be included and a minimal approach may use typography alone [00:11:56]. Uniquely supplies two things `SKILL.md` uses: **footer elements legitimately placed in the margin space or on the fringe of the poster, read outward or downward and comprehended last** [00:11:06], which is the demote-to-fringe move in the cut ladder; and the observation that a poster is seen as a whole before its individual pieces are digested, because of the viewing distance [00:10:11].

Its "form" category, meaning how the elements read as one arrangement, is the only place in the set that names the whole-before-parts effect, and it does not develop it.

## Where the sources disagree, and the decision rule

**1. Does legibility or visual effect rank first?**
Lissitzky ranks the visual character of the page above the legibility of its text [`81_mCjDU5IE` 00:17:16]. Moholy-Nagy's essay and Tschichold's handbook both put communication first [00:05:36, 00:28:38]. **Rule: Tschichold.** On a frame rendered at a third of its built size, legibility is the binding constraint and visual effect is the variable. Lissitzky was arguing about a page held in the hand at full size, so the condition that made his position arguable does not exist here.

**2. Strict grid adherence or deliberate play?**
Müller-Brockmann in Zurich taught "a strict almost religiously regimented method", and Zurich work adheres "pretty much at all costs". Hoffmann in Basel felt a designer should keep freedom, encouraging students to play with grids and break them a little to reach a more harmonious result [`e3rVZIA0Ab0` 00:18:51, 00:21:53]. **Rule: `dsn-composition`'s irregularity budget settles it, one deliberate break total.** Both camps produced work that holds up, which means the disagreement is about temperament rather than about correctness. What neither camp permits is unplanned breaks.

**3. How much can a single frame carry?**
Gareth David requires at least three levels of hierarchy in a poster [`Urac4u6GngM` 00:02:09]. The Swiss all-type tradition and the New Typography both reduce further, sometimes to one block of text [`e3rVZIA0Ab0` 00:20:25]. **Rule: the placement argument, which Gareth David himself supplies, decides it.** His three levels assume a poster-sized surface someone can approach. At feed size the third level is not read, so the floor drops to two. This is not the sources disagreeing so much as one of them failing to apply its own rule to a surface it never considered.

**4. Is the movement's formal programme a finding or a position?**
The Tschichold lecture is explicit that sans-serif, lowercase, asymmetry and the ornament ban were argued for on theoretical and political grounds. Lowercase in particular was driven by the view that capitals were aristocratic lettering that should be "levelled out" the way the French Revolution levelled society, with the supporting argument being "I do not speak in capital letters" [00:33:29, 00:34:27]. **Rule: every item in that programme is a position until something else supports it.** The audit the ornament ban forces is usable; the ban is not. The mass effect of lowercase in an all-type block is a real typographic consequence reported independently by the Swiss source; the political reason given for lowercase is not evidence of anything.

## Corrections and discards from the legibility pass

Material stripped from `SKILL.md` that is not recorded elsewhere in this file.

**A wrong corroboration count, now corrected.** The previous body said of the placement principle that "its companion video is the same channel, and neither lecture states it at all, so the corroboration is one lineage, not five sources." That contradicts the corroboration table above, which records the claim as reaching Gareth David (both videos), the Swiss grid lecture and the Tschichold lecture, so three independent channels. The table is the record and the body was wrong. **The placement principle is well corroborated; only the seconds attached to it are thin.** `SKILL.md` now marks the figures thin and leaves the principle unmarked.

**Five stages compressed to four.** The letterform-as-sculpture claim states five stages, single character, word, sentence, text and layout [`UGRkCRkgS-I` 00:23:25]. `SKILL.md` checks four, merging sentence and text into "the line", because on a frame that cannot carry a paragraph the sentence and text stages are the same object. The compression is an editorial decision made here, not in the source.

**The best-sourced claim, removed from the body.** The previous opening told the reader this was the best-sourced skill in the plugin after `dsn-grid` and that its reasoning "can be leaned on harder than usual." That is a statement about the build, it is recorded in the corroboration summary above, and a reader executing the skill cannot act on it.

**Claims the body made about its own gaps.** "None of the five measures whether any of this communicates better" and "no source counts elements or seconds" are both already in the numbers table and the not-covered list below. `SKILL.md` now carries the substance as a confidence line and a short scope note with no counts in either.

## Not covered by these sources

- **Any measurement against an outcome.** None of the five tests whether asymmetric, grid-based, sans-serif layout communicates better than what it replaced. Both lectures are explicit that the styles they describe won through institutional and political channels. The mechanisms are well reasoned; the superiority claims are unproven.
- **Platform rendering, cropping and chrome.** Total silence, as in every other skill in this plugin. The applied section is extrapolation from `reference/canvas-assumptions.md`.
- **Sequence.** Nothing on carousels, slide decks or any multi-frame form. The one-shot frame is the whole subject, which is why `dsn-composition` and `dsn-grid` carry the carousel material.
- **Motion and animation.** Absent. Noel Leu mentions animated versions of his own poster [`UGRkCRkgS-I` 00:57:58] and says nothing about how motion changes the decisions.
- **Print production.** Stone lithography, letterpress lockups and hand-cut linoleum appear as history, not as practice. Nothing on modern print specification.
- **Non-Latin setting.** The Swiss grid lecture's four-language constraint is four Latin-script languages. Noel Leu works with Cyrillic and discusses Japanese calligraphy, but as cultural comparison rather than as setting instruction. Nothing on right-to-left, vertical setting, or mixed-script composition.
- **Accessibility.** No contrast ratios, no minimum sizes, no colour-blindness consideration. Absent from the lectures too.
- **Anything after roughly 2003.** The Swiss grid lecture's most recent work is from 2003 [`e3rVZIA0Ab0` 00:30:29]. Nothing in the set addresses design for screens, let alone for feeds.
- **How to make a quiet frame rather than a loud one.** The Swiss lecture names the distinction precisely, mid-century posters being quiet against the later generation's "visual exclamation points" [00:26:22], and then says nothing about how either register is built.

- **The margin.** Now says the 90px margin is derived here from the crop and chrome hazard rather than being a shared constant, that the canvas file declines to fix any crop-safe margin, and that the 900px live width, the 4 columns and the 198px column all rest on this 90 and the 36px gutter, while `dsn-grid` closes a different grid on 72.
- **Values unchanged.** (900 - 3 x 36) / 4 = 198 still closes, as do the 432 / 276 / 198 / 120 column series, the 498px content block and the 50 and 25 character counts.
- **The supersede note is no longer stranded.** This file recorded that its computed sizes supersede imported values of 96, 48 and 38 and that `dsn-typeset` wins any conflict over type settings, and that file said nothing about it. The matching statement is now in the body there, and it does not contradict this one: it agrees that the three sizes here govern a single frame and that the ladder there wins when a project needs both.
