# Audit: dsn-poster

Independent check of `SKILL.md` against the five transcripts, not against `references/provenance.md`. The provenance file was written by the same agent that wrote the skill, so it is treated here as a claim to test.

Sources checked: `poster__81_mCjDU5IE` (New Typography lecture), `poster__e3rVZIA0Ab0` (Swiss grid lecture), `poster__UGRkCRkgS-I` (type designer lecture), `poster__Urac4u6GngM` and `poster__mQaKXbLgWyg` (both the same practitioner channel).

**Counts.** 2 mislabelled, 3 unsourced numbers, 2 derived and unlabelled, 0 unsupported, 6 of 6 inline citations resolve exactly. The quotations in this skill are the most accurate of the three audited; the problems are in the corroboration arithmetic and in numbers presented as instruction.

**On the two scholarly lectures.** The skill extracts principle and does not retell history. There is no chronology, no biography and no movement narrative in the instructional sections; the history is confined to one marked section, "Register, and what the movement argued", where each item is labelled position, political position, dogma or assertion before it is discussed. The two strongest uses of the lectures are both principle rather than period: the author's own scope limit [81_mCjDU5IE 01:26:24] and the type-as-image instruction [e3rVZIA0Ab0 00:20:25]. The caution at the end of that section, "These are arguments that won, not experiments that passed", is the right frame and is in the transcript, which volunteers its own institutional critique.

**On copyrighted artworks.** No extended description of any specific poster appears. The longest passage touching a particular work is one clause on the all-type series, that red marks "the series title and the headline performers and nothing else", which is a rule being extracted rather than an artwork being reproduced in prose. Named works elsewhere are mentioned only as the location of a principle. Nothing here needs trimming.

## Mislabelled

### 1. Placement corroboration claimed across all five sources, where it is one channel

`SKILL.md` wrote: "**4. Cut depth is set by the viewing condition, not by taste.** Same content, two placements, two different pieces. All five sources agree that placement governs complexity, and it is the only thing they all agree on."

Both halves fail.

The claim is stated once, by one channel: "a poster design is not necessarily a one-size-fits-all solution ... if a poster is placed outdoors, on a wall, on the street, or on a billboard, then it may be seen fleetingly, and thus require a design to be much more impactful ... The elements may be larger and have higher contrast" [Urac4u6GngM 00:04:58], with the contemplative half in the same breath, "gallery, office, or school space, or at a train session, when one may be able to spend more time observing" [Urac4u6GngM 00:04:58]. The companion video from the same channel supplies only the whole-before-parts effect: "posters are seen from a distance and at first glance are seen as a whole before digesting the individual pieces" [mQaKXbLgWyg 00:10:11]. Same channel, so one lineage.

The two lectures do not state it at all. Searched across both for every way the idea could be phrased, neither contains "billboard", "viewing distance", "where it hangs", "at a glance" or "from a distance" in any form. What the Swiss grid lecture has is format standardisation and display regulation, "welt format or world format poster and rules were introduced establishing where posters could be officially displayed in public" [e3rVZIA0Ab0 00:11:15], which is about where posters may legally go, not about how much a placement permits a design to carry. The New Typography lecture has nothing on the subject.

This matters out of proportion to its size. Principle 4, the dwell budget, the cut ladder's step 4 and the entire applied section rest on this claim, and the skill tells a reader it is the set's single consensus finding. It is one practitioner channel's argument, stated once, in a ten-minute video. The reasoning is good and the rule should stand; its corroboration was overstated.

Fixed: corrected to one channel, with the two-video relationship named and the lectures' silence stated.

### 2. Four scales, where the source names five stages

`SKILL.md` wrote: "**the frame has to hold at four scales at once: the single character, the word, the line, and the layout.**"

The source names five: "it can consist of a word it can consist of a sentence it can consist of text and eventually it can also consist of a layout and in order to do a good typographic design or typeface design all of those stages from single character to layout the typeface needs to work" [UGRkCRkgS-I 00:23:25]. Character, word, sentence, text, layout. The skill collapses sentence and text into "line", which is a different object: a line is a typesetting unit, a sentence and a block of text are not. The test built on it ("Four-scale hold ... Inspect character, word, line and layout separately") inherits the error.

Fixed: the source's stages restored, with the skill's four-step version marked as its own compression.

## Unsourced numbers

The provenance file states that none of the five sources gives a single number for anything in `SKILL.md`. That is correct. Three numbers are nonetheless presented in the skill as instruction without being marked.

### 3. The dwell budget in seconds

"**Write the dwell budget in seconds.** One to two for a feed or a passing billboard, ten to thirty for a wall someone stands in front of. This number sets the element count before you know what the elements are."

The source supplies the fleeting-against-contemplative distinction with no figures whatever [Urac4u6GngM 00:04:58]. One to two and ten to thirty are constructed here. They then propagate: the test table fails a frame at "Over four at under two seconds", and the applied section sets "Dwell budget: one to two seconds".

Fixed: marked as constructed at the point of use and in the test table.

### 4. Two to four elements

"**Dwell budget: one to two seconds,** so the element count is two to four total, counting the handle. Not two to four groups. Two to four elements." Derived from the constructed dwell budget plus a sibling skill. No source counts elements.

Fixed: marked as derived.

### 5. "In almost every real test"

"One display line at 96px against the whole frame beats an illustration plus a 48px headline in almost every real test."

No test exists. None of the five sources measures whether anything communicates better than anything else, which the skill's own gap list states at the end of the file ("none of the five measures whether any of this communicates better"). The phrase "in almost every real test" claims evidence of exactly the kind the set does not contain, inside the sentence the skill calls "the single most useful transfer in the file".

Fixed: the claim of testing removed and the basis named.

## Derived: reasonable, not stated by any source, not labelled as derived

### 6. "Purest form"

"The Swiss tradition reached its purest form when Vivarelli told Müller-Brockmann ... he should 'turn the text itself into the image and nothing more' [e3rVZIA0Ab0 00:20:25]." The quotation is exact and the attribution is right: "a colleague of brockman's vivarelli told him that even the use of pure geometry was too subjective and you should turn the text itself into the image and nothing more" [e3rVZIA0Ab0 00:20:25]. The source presents it as one point in one designer's evolution, with three posters showing the range. "Purest form" is the skill's ranking. Minor and left as is.

### 7. "Upgrades it from house style to a finding"

Of the self-reference anti-pattern: "**Two independent sources now**, with `dsn-composition`'s version, which upgrades it from house style to a finding." The observation is sourced, and vividly: asked to design a poster for a poster festival, "it's a design that references itself", and every previous year by a different designer shared one attitude [UGRkCRkgS-I 00:48:06]. Two independent observations of the same pattern is corroboration, not a finding, and nothing in either source tests it. Left as is; the word is doing less work than it looks.

## Positions correctly marked as positions

Checked item by item against the transcripts. Every entry in the "what the movement argued" list is both accurate and correctly labelled.

- Sans-serif as elemental, with "there is no excuse for any other forms" [81_mCjDU5IE 00:22:31], marked a position with an ideological motive and noted as abandoned later. Correct.
- Lowercase as political, capitals as "aristocratic lettering" to be "leveled out ... the same way that the French Revolution had leveled out society" [81_mCjDU5IE 00:33:29], with "I do not speak in capital letters" at 00:34:27. Marked political, argued by analogy, no perceptual claim. Correct, and the skill is careful to keep the real mass effect separate from the bad reason given for it.
- All directions at once, reading order abandoned [81_mCjDU5IE 00:23:27]. Marked a position. Correct.
- The ornament ban. Marked dogma, audit kept, ban dropped. Correct.
- The triangle explanation, marked "an assertion rather than a finding". Correct.
- The institutional caution. Present in the source, which volunteers it against itself.

One residual tension worth stating. The skill opens by claiming it "can be leaned on harder than usual" because of the two lectures. The lectures are the reason to lean *less* hard on anything procedural, since they contain almost no procedure, and the skill's own closing gap list concedes none of the five sources measures an outcome. The claim is defensible as a statement about depth of reasoning, and the countervailing caution is present in the file, so it is left alone. A reader should take it as a claim about the quality of the argument, not about evidence.
