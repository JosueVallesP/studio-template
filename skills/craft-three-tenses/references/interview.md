# The interview

The interview collects bridges, not lists. Asking "tell me about your past", then "your present", then "your future" gets three lists that never touch, and the useful material lives where they touch. So start in one tense and follow the thread into the other two.

It follows `skills/craft-interview`: one question at a time, ask about things that happened, turn hedges into facts, and keep the person's words.

## Before the first question

- Read the bank if it exists, and what the studio already holds (the list in `SKILL.md`, "Before anything"). Skip anything already answered, and when a story on file fits a topic, start from it: "You told the story about <their words>. Where is that heading now?"
- Tell the person in two or three sentences what's about to happen: five areas of their work, one question at a time, every answer becomes post material, and they can stop after any area.
- Ask which area they want to start with, or start with Projects, which is usually the easiest.

## The loop for each topic

### 1. Open in the Now

Ask about the present first. It's what they're living, so they answer quickly and with detail.

- People: "Who are you working with most closely right now?"
- Tools: "What's one tool, book or method you'd recommend to anyone in your field today?"
- Process: "Walk me through how you did the core part of your work on your last job, from the start to the end."
- Projects: "What are you working on right now that you're proud of?"
- Beliefs: "What's a decision you made recently, and how did you make it?"

### 2. Pull back into the Then

For the answer they gave, ask where it came from:

- "Where did you learn that?"
- "When did you first start working that way? What were you doing before?"
- "Who showed you that?"
- "What happened that made you believe that?"

Look for a moment, not a summary. "I learned it over the years" is a summary. "My first client fired me in 2016 because I missed the deadline, and since then I write the timeline before I write anything else" is a moment. If you get a summary, ask for the moment: "Is there a specific time you remember?"

### 3. Push forward into the Future

For the same answer, ask where it's heading:

- "Where is that going next?"
- "What are you testing that might replace it?"
- "Who have you already reached out to, or started working with, on this?"
- "What have you already committed to that only makes sense if this keeps going?"
- "What have you seen change in your field this past year that you're already acting on?"

The Future is anchored in what they've already started, tested or committed to, never in a picture of someday (`skills/craft-interview`). If the answer is still vague, ask for the step they've already taken or booked: "What's the last thing you did that moves this forward?"

### 4. Write the bridge

When you have all three ends, write the bridge to the bank (`bank-format.md`), with one post idea for each overlap. Read it back to the person in one or two lines, in their words, so they can correct anything: "So your first agency boss made you rewrite every brief, you now run every client project from a one-page brief, and you want to teach the brief as a workshop next year. Did I get that right?"

When an answer was a half-thought (a hedge, two ideas in one), offer the sharper version beside it and ask whether it's still true (`CLAUDE.md` rule 3). The bank keeps the raw words either way.

Then ask whether there's another one in this topic, or move on.

### 5. Keep loose ends

Some answers only have one or two ends. Someone might have a strong Then story with nothing in the present yet. Don't force it. Save it as a loose end in the bank. Loose ends often connect later.

## Depth over coverage

Two good bridges beat five thin ones. Signs a bridge is good enough:

- It has at least one name, date, number, place or quote.
- The Then end is a moment, not a summary.
- The Future end has a next step, not only a dream.
- You could write a first line for a post from it without inventing anything.

If a bridge is thin, ask one more question before moving on. If the person can't give more, save it and move on. Some things come back to them later.

## Pacing

- One question per message. No lists of questions.
- Don't praise the answers, and don't sum up everything they say. Restating a bridge in their words once it's done is enough to show they were heard.
- After each topic, tell them how many bridges you have and offer to stop or continue.
- If they go off on a tangent with good material, follow it. The topics are prompts, not a script.

## Ending the session

When they stop (or finish all five topics):

1. Tell them how many bridges and post ideas the bank now holds.
2. Name the two or three strongest ideas, one line each.
3. If a Then end is a story worth keeping in the canon, say so in one line and add it to `canon/bank/stories.md` as a `draft` entry, in their words, with its source and a log line, and ask one question (`skills/setup-brand-tree/SKILL.md`, "Where a picked-up belief goes"). A change to a `confirmed` story goes to `proposals.md` as `kind: change to S<n>` instead.
4. Offer one next step: pick one of the ideas and write it now (`/post`), or come back to fill the topics they skipped.
