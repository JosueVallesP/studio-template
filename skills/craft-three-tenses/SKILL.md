# Three tenses

Method by Josué Valles, the studio's author.

People follow people who are going somewhere. A job title or a positioning statement says where someone stands; a person's content shows them moving through time. So it covers three tenses:

- **Then:** who they were, what happened, what shaped them.
- **Now:** who they are, how they work, what they're doing this week.
- **Future:** who they're becoming, what they're testing, where they think things are going.

A person who writes in only one tense gets stuck in one of three ways:

- **Only Then** reads like a resume. Credible, but there's nothing to follow.
- **Only Now** reads like a tips feed. Useful, but there's no story.
- **Only Future** reads like a vision board. Exciting, but hard to believe.

The best material sits where two tenses touch:

- **Then/Now builds authority.** Something from years ago explains how they work today, so the reader understands why they're good at it.
- **Now/Future builds connection.** What they're doing today and where it's heading. People like to join something that's moving, and they like being there early.

A follow is a bet on what happens next. The goal is to give readers enough Then to trust the person, enough Now to find them useful, and enough Future to want to stick around.

The tenses are a lens, not a rule. A post doesn't fail because it lives in one tense.

## The five topics

The same topics show up in every tense, which is what makes this easy:

| Topic | Then | Now | Future |
|---|---|---|---|
| People | Mentors who shaped them | People they work with | People they'd like to work with |
| Tools | Books and tools that changed how they work | Tools they use and recommend | Tools and methods they're trying |
| Process | How they learned what they know | How they work, step by step | Experiments they're running |
| Projects | Early projects, behind the scenes | Current work, behind the scenes | Plans for the next few years |
| Beliefs | Stories that shaped how they see the world | How they make decisions | Predictions about their field |

These are prompts, not boxes. Anything that matters to the person can become a sixth row.

## The other uses of the tenses

This file is the one place the tenses are defined. Two skills use them on something other than the person's life:

- **On a cause** (`skills/job-manifesto`): Then is how things were, a good that was lost or an earlier attempt that failed; Now is what's broken, with evidence; Future is what should be, and what the person is building toward. The tenses are set against the cause, not the person's life story, and the future is the tense that most needs filling.
- **On an idea** (`skills/job-think`, as a perspective move): was this always true, is it true now, will it be? An idea that only holds in one tense has found its condition.

Neither use writes to the tense bank. Both follow the same rule as everything here: every tense is filled from the person's files or what they said, never with a generality.

## Where it sits

- **The interviews** (`skills/setup-brand-tree`) find the beliefs and confirm the stories in `canon/bank/stories.md`. The tense bank is lighter and faster: post material in the person's words, kept as a working bank beside the idea bank. When a Then end is a story worth keeping in the canon, it goes into `canon/bank/stories.md` as a `draft` entry with the person's words as its `quote`, the tense bank as its source and a log line, for their yes (`skills/setup-brand-tree/SKILL.md`, "Where a picked-up belief goes"). Only a change to a `confirmed` story goes to `proposals.md`, as `kind: change to S<n>`. Nothing from here is ever confirmed without the person (`CLAUDE.md` rule 6).
- **The idea bank** (`bank/ideas.md`) is what `/post` and `/essay` draw from. When the person picks an idea from the tense bank, it becomes an idea bank entry (see Mode 3), so the writer can cite it.
- **The writing** is `/post` and `/essay`. This skill finds the material; it doesn't carry post templates or draft posts.

## Before anything

Look for `bank/tenses.md`. If it exists, read it. If it doesn't, start with Mode 1 (create it from `references/bank-format.md`). Also read what the studio already holds so you don't ask for what the person answered somewhere else: `canon/core-summary.md`, `canon/bank/stories.md`, the confirmed entries in `canon/person/`, `material/` and the last few shipped pieces (`pieces/*/final.md`).

The format and rules for the bank are in `references/bank-format.md`. Read it before writing to the bank.

## Pick the mode

Work out what the person wants from what they say:

| They say | Mode |
|---|---|
| "Build my tense bank", "set this up", first time, no bank exists | 1. Build the bank |
| They paste a draft or name a piece, "check this", "which tense is this" | 2. Check a draft |
| "What should I post", "give me ideas", "I'm stuck" | 3. Suggest ideas |
| "Update my bank", or the bank has stale Future ends | 1, in refresh form |

If a draft check or an idea request comes in and no bank exists, do the job with what's in the draft, `material/` and the canon, then offer to build the bank in one line.

### Mode 1: Build the bank

An interview, one question at a time. The full method is in `references/interview.md`; read it before the first question. It follows `skills/craft-interview`, and it asks about things that happened. The short version:

1. Start in the present, because it's what the person is living and they answer it fastest and in the most detail.
2. For every present answer, pull the thread both ways: "Where did that come from?" and "Where is that heading?"
3. Push for the concrete thing: the name, the year, the client, the number, the moment. A bridge with no specifics produces generic posts.
4. Write each finished bridge to the bank as you go, so nothing is lost if the session stops.

Before the first question, say in two or three sentences what's about to happen, why, what they'll have at the end and how long it takes (`CLAUDE.md` rule 7): five areas of their work, one question at a time, every answer becomes post material, about ten minutes an area, and they can stop after any area.

A full bank is two or three bridges per topic. That's plenty to start; the bank grows over time. Offer to stop after any topic, and don't push the person through all five in one sitting.

**Refresh.** Future ends go stale fastest. When you open the bank, check their dates. For any older than about three months, ask what happened. A plan that came true is a new Then/Now story. A plan that died is often a better post than the plan was. Update the entry either way, keeping the old Future end with its date.

### Mode 2: Check a draft

The person pastes something they wrote, or names a piece in `pieces/`. Read `references/draft-check.md`, then:

1. Name the tense the draft sits in, and point to the lines that put it there.
2. Say which overlap it's missing, if any. Many good posts sit in one tense on purpose; say so when that's the case and leave it alone.
3. If it would get stronger by reaching back or forward, offer one or two sentences that do it, built only from the draft, the bank, the person's files and what they said today. Match the draft's voice and style.
4. If nothing on file fits, ask one question to get the missing piece.

Never rewrite the whole post. The draft is theirs; you're suggesting a reach, not a new version. On a piece in `pieces/`, add one line to its `log.md` with the tense and the reach offered. Whether the reach goes in is the person's call.

### Mode 3: Suggest ideas

1. Sort the person's recent posts by tense (the last ten shipped pieces, or the posts in `material/`) and tell them their pattern in one or two sentences: "Seven of your last ten posts are Now. You barely mention where you came from."
2. Pull three to five ideas from the bank, weighted toward the overlaps they've neglected. Most should come from Then/Now and Now/Future, with the odd pure-tense idea for balance.
3. For each idea give the bridge it comes from, the overlap it sits in, the point the post would make, and a first line written plainly.
4. When the person picks one, copy it into `bank/ideas.md` as a new entry: `raw` is the bridge's words word for word, `source: bank/tenses.md, <bridge name>`, `type: story` (or `method` for a Process bridge), `status: ready`. Mark the idea `picked <date>, I<n>` in the tense bank so it doesn't come back next week. Then offer `/post I<n>` or `/essay I<n>`.

## How to write

Every question, suggestion and line follows `skills/craft-house-rules`, with the person's voice guide on top. For this method in particular:

- Name the concrete thing. "My first boss at the agency in 2014" beats "a mentor".
- Only use what the person told you or what's in their files (`CLAUDE.md` rule 1). Never invent a memory, client, number or plan for them. If a suggestion needs a detail you don't have, ask for it.
- Keep questions short. One question per message, with a small example of the kind of answer you mean when it helps.

## Never

- Never fill a missing tense with a generality, in a bank, a draft check, a manifesto or an idea.
- Never write to `canon/` except a `draft` story entry the person can confirm, and never change a `confirmed` entry; a change to one goes to `proposals.md`.
- Never rewrite a draft.
- Never put facts about a person in this file.
