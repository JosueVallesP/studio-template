# Offer library

The reference lists for `/offer-designer`, from Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers* (2021). Every item here is a possibility to choose from with the person, never a default to fill in. Each one still passes the truth tests in `skills/craft-sales-copy`.

## Objections and the bonuses that answer them

| The objection | Bonuses that can answer it |
| --- | --- |
| "I don't have time" | A fast-start guide, done-for-you templates, a short daily plan |
| "I'm not good with the tech" | A setup call, video walkthroughs, click-by-click guides |
| "Will this work for my situation?" | Case studies from the buyer's own field (real ones, from the proof file), templates for their niche, a one-to-one planning session |
| "How long until I see results?" | A quick-win module, a first-week plan, a first-result sprint |
| "What if I get stuck?" | A community, weekly Q&A calls, direct message support |
| "I've been burned before" | A longer guarantee, a trial period, a small first commitment |

Use the objection the person has heard, in the buyer's words, not the row's wording.

## Guarantee types

From most to least aggressive:

1. **Unconditional.** "Full refund within X days, no questions asked." Fits low-ticket offers, digital products and first-time buyers. Highest risk, though refund rates usually turn out lower than people fear.
2. **Conditional.** "Do X, Y and Z, and if you don't get the result, full refund." Fits high-ticket programs, coaching and courses. The conditions filter out buyers who won't do the work, and when they're well chosen, the conditions are the path to the result.
3. **Performance.** "If you don't get this measurable result, we refund, keep working for free, or pay you." Fits services where the person controls the outcome: agencies, done-for-you work. Only as strong as the person's record.
4. **Implied.** "We work with you until you get the result." Fits services, consulting and coaching. Costs time, not cash.
5. **Anti-guarantee.** "All sales final." Works only when demand already exceeds supply and reputation does the selling. Lowest risk, and it can lower sales.
6. **Stacked.** Two or more together, for example a 30-day unconditional refund plus a conditional one at 90 days.

Every offer has some way of taking the risk off the buyer. Name it plainly so people remember it. If it's conditional, the conditions are reasonable and written out. Track refunds; the person's own rate is the number that matters.

## Container words

What kind of thing the offer is. Pick by energy, format and how long the buyer commits.

- **Short and intense:** challenge, sprint, intensive, bootcamp, workshop
- **Structured learning over weeks:** academy, masterclass, certification, institute
- **A method:** system, blueprint, method, framework, toolkit, playbook
- **Ongoing, with others:** mastermind, circle, collective
- **Premium and small:** accelerator, retreat, private, one to one

Hormozi's own examples show how the MAGIC parts combine: "The 90-Day Book Launch Accelerator" (time frame, goal, container) and "The $10K Freelancer Blueprint" (goal, container). The person's version uses their own buyers' words, and a time frame only when most buyers can hit it.

## How the method bends

**Services** (agencies, consulting, coaching): lean toward done for you and done with you. A performance guarantee is possible because the person controls the work. Scarcity is already real: their hours are limited.

**Digital products** (courses, templates, software): lean toward do it yourself and done with you. Use a conditional guarantee. Scarcity comes from cohorts, enrollment windows or bonuses tied to a real start date.

**Physical products:** add digital bonuses, which raise the value a lot for little cost. An unconditional guarantee is easy. Scarcity comes from real limited runs or seasons.

**High-ticket** (a starting line: about $5,000 and up): more one to one and done for you, guarantees that can stack, and a longer, consultative sale where the offer document becomes a proposal (`/diagnosis-call`).

**Low-ticket** (a starting line: under about $500): a simpler structure with fewer bonuses, an unconditional guarantee to remove friction, and a name that leads with speed to the result. A very low-ticket front-end product is its own method: `/pamphlet-power`.
