## Essay

We are living through a strange moment in history. A moment where machines can imitate minds. Where algorithms can talk like us, write like us, even think like us or so it seems.

> These machines don’t understand us. They predict us.

Under the hood of every modern AI, from chatbots to recommender systems, is the same core idea: compression. The model ingests vast amounts of data, looks for patterns, and tries to summarise it. Reduce it. Flatten it into something smaller and faster to work with. Like a zip file for human behavior.

This is what a stochastic compressor is. A fancy way of saying: a pattern-spotting machine that reduces complexity into probability.

And here’s the unsettling part: if you are compressible , the machine can predict you. It can generate your tweets. Your emails. Your startup pitch. Your jokes. Even your beliefs.

That’s the hidden cost of fitting in. If your thoughts are average, they can be compressed. If your ideas are derivative, they can be predicted. If your personality is shaped by trends, you can be simulated  -  and eventually replaced.

The more you become like everyone else, the more you disappear.

This is not just a technological shift. It’s a philosophical one.

We used to fear being misunderstood. Now the real danger is being completely understood , because it means you're no longer surprising. No longer original. No longer human, in the deepest sense.

> Compressibility is the byproduct of conformity.

Scroll through your feed. The same recycled opinions. The same motivational platitudes. The same aesthetics, language, outrage cycles. Even rebellion has become predictable. If you’re just echoing what the machine has already seen, you're just another node in the dataset.

The world trains you to be compressible. To be “relatable.” To be efficient. To be safe. To fit into a résumé. To follow a playbook.

But the algorithm can only compress what it already knows.

To be incompressible is to break the pattern. To do something that hasn’t been done. To think what hasn’t been thought. To speak in a voice that’s unmistakably yours.

So what does it mean to be incompressible?

It means being weird , in the truest sense of the word. Not performatively weird, not “quirky” for social approval, but fundamentally unpatterned. It means holding unpopular opinions. Going deep into strange interests. Saying no when everyone says yes.

It means cultivating taste that isn’t algorithmic. It means being boring to the AI and fascinating to the human .

An incompressible person can’t be summarised in a bio. Their path doesn’t follow the template. Their beliefs don’t always map cleanly to a tribe. Their output, whether it’s code, art, or conversation, makes people pause, not scroll.

Most of all, incompressibility requires solitude. Silence. Reflection. Space from the noise so you can remember who you are when no one is watching.

The goal is not to outsmart the machine. The goal is to be the thing the machine can’t model.

In a world where everything is being turned into data, your uniqueness is your last form of leverage.

The market will reward originality again. The future will crave what the algorithm cannot produce. And the only way to avoid being automated is to be authentically human .

So in an age of stochastic compressors…

Be incompressible.
