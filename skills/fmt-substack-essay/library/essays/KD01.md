## Essay

“My book is gone.”

It’s January 2026. I’ve just finished a horrible phone call with Apple after being robbed in Buenos Aires. My bank accounts were emptied, my identity was stolen, and yet the news I just delivered to my girlfriend hit much harder:

I lost my book, Magnetic Writing .

I had been writing it every morning for a year - 800 hours of work, and it was inches away from being completed. But the robbers wiped my Apple devices. And Mr Genius over here only saved the book offline in an attempt to avoid distraction.

The news devastated me.

That week, I woke every morning with a dark cloud over my head.

I had never invested so much time in something only to have it stripped away. And I’d said no to many business opportunities to take this bigger swing. I had a decision to make: start again, or move on.

But the thought of giving up felt worse than starting again from zero.

I realised I had to write this book. It meant so much to me, and I knew it had great potential. But if I were going to commit again, I needed to write the book I would be most in love with. Not just a book on how to write, but how to use writing to build self-awareness, have deeper ideas, and think more clearly. And use these qualities to attract the audience, business, and life you want.

My problem with version 1 was that I had been using Claude to edit, and it had started to feel a little sterile. It took out my humour, the philosophy stuff, and my stories. It told me to focus, but it read like a business 101 book. The world does not need another one of those.

So I decided to write it again, but entirely on my terms. Since January, I’ve set aside 3-4 hours every morning to bring it back from the deadline.

I’m grateful to say I have just finished editing version 2 by hand, and I am much, much happier.

The rush I felt when I realised there were no more words to type was surreal.

Excited to get this out to you,

Kieran

P.S.

The crazy thing about the book is that I already consider it a success.

I’ve never worked on something that has had such a profound effect on me. 16 months on one project has taught me so much about prioritisation, persistence, and pursuing a purpose.

I don’t know what will happen in the future. But all I know is that I continue to fall more and more in love with writing. I already have my next book ideas in mind, and I can’t wait to dive deeper to see if there’s something there.
