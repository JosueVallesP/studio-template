## Essay

It’s September 1, 2021, and I’m standing in an alleyway next to the dental practice, trying to suck air into lungs that feel like they have collapsed.

A small part of me chuckles at the irony.

Usually, it’s my patients who have a panic attack, but today, it’s my turn. Why? Because I was about to quit my career, and my new boss has no idea. I glance at my watch. He was expecting me 10 minutes ago. Tomorrow was my first day at work. Today, he planned to show me around the clinic.

This is going to be the worst conversation of my life.

Ok. I can breathe again. Kinda. Enough to do this. I nod my head in resolution, stand tall, and march around the corner. I knock on the door before I change my mind. I’m going to be firm and direct.

The door swings open.

I’m met by my boss’s beaming face and a team of nurses smiling behind him.

My resolution melts.

“ Oh god, I can’t do this ”, I realise, reaching out to shake his extended hand on autopilot. He pulls me into the clinic.”

We’re so excited to have you.” He said.

“Me too,” I reply, close to throwing up in my mouth.

What followed was an incredibly awkward 20 minutes. I met nurses I’d never work with, picked materials I’d never use, and discussed patients I would never see.

“Maybe I can fake being kidnapped instead.” I wondered. Plan B sounds easier than Plan A.

After what felt like an eternity, my boss invites me upstairs to discuss the details.

We enter his office. He points at the chair and invites me to sit.

This is it. Just be smooth, Kieran.

“So what do you think?” He says, sitting back with a confident smile.

“I QUIT”, I shout, 10 decibels louder than intended.

His face drops.

“What?”

Yep. Smooth. Well, you’re in it now.

“I can’t take this job. I’m really sorry.” Guilt gnaws at the pit of my stomach. This was his brand new clinic, and we’d been talking for 6 months for me to be his lead dentist.

On paper, it was the dream job.

But it was no longer my dream.

“But you’re only 5 years in. You can’t give up now.” He said.

I laugh.

“Precisely. If not now, then when?”

He tries to persuade me otherwise, but once he realised I was serious, he shifted his focus.

“So what’s the plan?”

Oh god, here we go. The dreaded question.

## “I am going to be a writer.”

Sympathy paints his face. This is not the first time I’ve seen a reaction like this.

“That’s great.” He lies. “So... are you writing a book or something?”

“Uhh, not exactly.”

I begin fumbling through my reply. I explain that I had started writing online in my spare time a year before as an attempt to plug the gaping hole in my heart that was my lack of fulfilment. But what started as a side hobby quickly developed into a full-blown love affair. I wasn’t very good, but I couldn’t wait to wake up every morning. I had never felt so alive as when I was sitting behind the computer, sipping tea, tapping away at the keyboard. The more I wrote, the more I wanted to write.

Every morning I drove to work felt like I was making a massive mistake.

I explained:

“All I know is that if I enjoy something this much, I am going to regret not giving it a go.”

We said our goodbyes. I left the practice, stepping onto the cobbled street. I took a deep breath and smiled as the morning sun warmed my face. A woman walking past smiled back at me. It was the first time I had looked at teeth without thinking of work.

It was time to make the writing dream happen.

## What writing will do for you

I wish I could tell you that the next day, the words poured out of my fingertips and money into my wallet. But I had stepped into the abyss, and damn it was darker than expected.

I didn’t anticipate the raging imposter syndrome as I tried to pitch myself. I didn’t expect the deep-seated fear of my ideas being rejected. I didn’t imagine how much self-doubt would rear its ugly head with simple decisions like deciding my niche.

But I never regretted my decision.

Because even in the toughest time, I knew I was right.

I wanted to make an impact with my ideas. I wanted to have a career where I was rewarded for thinking. I wanted to earn with my mind and not my time. I wanted to feel like the things I was learning weren’t going to waste. I wanted to spend my days creating things I was proud of, while being free to work on my terms, from anywhere in the world.

Writing makes these things possible.

You can reach the world without needing anyone’s permission. You can share what makes you come alive and attract an audience who feels the same. You can build offers that solve problems you are passionate about, with people you love to work with, and be rewarded for making real change.

Now, you might not consider yourself a writer in classic terms.

But the label is not important: the action is.

Or the result of the action.

Writing gives you authority. It gives you trust. It gives you options. An entrepreneurial writer lays fertile ground for opportunities to bloom.

Be it clients, connections, or just a sense of purpose.

Writing is a magnet for the life and business that you want,
