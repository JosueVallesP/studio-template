## Essay

Jean-Louis-Ernest Meissonier, A Poet

In some ways, I believe the age of the “content creator” is over.

That whole get-a-bunch-of-followers-and-sell-them-your-online-course business model is way harder than it used to be, and I think we’ll look back on it as a peculiar moment in time rather than the birth of an industry.

But on the flip side I believe the age of the “content thinker” might be just beginning.

The content thinker is something I just made up this very second as I type, but I think it’s a good way to delineate a new behaviour that will be immensely powerful in the coming years.

The short definition is this:

The content thinker is someone who produces content with the goal of developing real-world mastery of their field, with the gathering of followers being only an afterthought.

The content thinker is not a new phenomenon. Steve Jobs was a content thinker. Jeff Bezos is a content thinker. Raval Navikant is a content thinker. And as you can see, crucially, the content thinker is not someone whose main job is getting followers online.

They can be a CEO. They can be an investor. They can be a corporate leader. They can be a craftsman.

Indeed their main job should be anything but getting followers online - because they are using content as the means to a very different end. They are using content as a means of out-thinking, out-manoeuvring, and out-performing everyone else in their space.

Content thinkers are what we may have also in the past called “thought leaders”. But the crucial difference now is that “thought leaders” are not people like me, or authors, or academics, who basically sell their thoughts for a living.

Instead they are people with real jobs who want to use thought leadership for competitive advantage.

As I wrote last week , this is crucial for the new era of competition. Due to over-supply in almost all markets, winning in the next decade is not going to be merely a question of being a good operator (as it has been in the past), but instead it will be a question of who can redefine the rules of their industry.

The only way you can do this is to be a thoughtful and innovative student of your industry, which is a habit that the vast majority of executives simply don’t possess.

But if you follow my instructions here?

Then you will.

And you will steal a huge march on every competitor around you. Trust me.

So without further ado, let me introduce you to…

## The IRL Beast Content System

This is the exact system I use, every week.

It’s called “IRL Beast” because its purpose is exactly that - to turn you into an in-real-life beast in whatever industry it is that you occupy.

Everyone else out there is currently doing one of two things:

- They aren’t creating / thinking at all

- Or they are creating in a way that’s optimised purely for reach and followers

Here we will be doing neither of these things. Instaed, we will be creating and thinking to develop ourselves, and our businesses.

How?

## Step 1 - Record literally every conversation you have with Granola

I strongly believe that speech is the “innovation factory” of thought. When you’re talking to another person, you are alive, and energised, and intermingling your ideas with theirs - a dynamic that’s far more powerful than sitting at your computer and “thinking”.

When you speak to other people - colleagues, clients, whatever - you are constantly going to be throwing out the germs of truly interesting and insightful ideas.

The problem of course is that if you’re doing it right, you’re “in the moment”, and you’re not capturing these ideas for future use. Luckily however, there’s now technology to fix that.

Granola is literally the only new product of the AI generation that impresses me. The rest of them can go to hell. Basically it’s just a note-taker app (on your phone or on your computer), that can transcribe all your meetings - whether online or IRL.

Your job is basically to have it be recording every discussion you have.

It takes notes and all of that usual stuff, but we’re not interested in that. We’re interested in the raw transcripts only. That’s where the gold is.

## Step 2 - Set up Claude or whatever to mine your transcripts once-per-week for ideas

When you’ve got Granola piling up all these transcripts of you talking, you can use your next bit of tech to sift through them.

I get Claude to provide me a report once per week which is basically a long bullet-point list of anything that I’ve said which sounds like a fresh new idea, or which might be the germ of an interesting essay or post.

Crucial points:

- It is only taking the things that you’ve said, not ripping off other people you speak to

- It is not embellishing or building out the ideas in any way; it’s giving them back to you as close to verbatim as possible

It is a secretary, and nothing more. This is very important.

It will miss a lot of interesting stuff that you said. And it will surface a lot of stuff that isn’t very interesting. But in amongst it, there will for sure be 3-7 interesting lines of thought.

## Step 3 - Create short posts to build out your favourite ideas

This is where you actually create the content.

Choosing a platform that you like (LinkedIn, X, Substack, etc.), just develop 3-7 of the ideas from the preceding week into circa 300 word posts.

Even if you have no need or desire to build an audience, that doesn’t matter. You are doing this in public due to the psychic pressure it puts on you to make these ideas robust and thought-through. (It will also massively improve your communication skills as a bonus)

This is where the magic happens.

This act of turning these half-baked thoughts into coherent posts is where you integrate them with your IRL skillset. The mental mastication at play here is the entire point of the process. These are the reps in the gym. This is where you prepare to walk into any room, or onto any stage, with the ability to drop these bombs at will.

For this reason - and I cannot stress this enough - do not use AI here.

Not even a tiny bit. None of this “it’s my thought partner” BS. None of this “it challenges my thinking” crap. AI is fundamentally more articulate and more prolific than you are; so if you give it an inch here, it will dominate you.

You are authoring these ideas purely from your own personal experience and subconscious. They are not meant to be “the best possible version of that idea”. They are simply meant to be yours , and integrated with your psyche.

A sketchy idea developed like this, is 10x more valuable than a flawless thesis that AI wrote for you.

## Step 4 - (Optional) Use Instagram edits app to record reel versions

If you want to integrate even deeper, you can turn these thoughts into reels using the Instagram Edits app.

You don’t have to be using Instagram for this, it’s just a video editing app that they’ve developed, and it is absolutely awesome. It has an in-built teleprompter so you can paste your post as a little script and just belt it out.

This is yet more reps, since speaking the thing is even better than writing it - and it’ll give you more performance courage, which is great for leading teams, selling, getting investment, whatever.

## Step 5 - Blend the ideas every couple of weeks into a 1,000 word essay

Given that you’re going to be talking about your own field, you should have a pretty decent feeling for which ideas are important and consequential, and which are a bit “throwaway”.

Furthermore what you’re going to start to see is the way the ideas connect and nest within one another. E.g. Tuesday and Thursday’s posts may actually just be subsets of Friday’s post.

Using these skills you want to blend your ideas into a deeper, more thoughtful, 1,000+ word essay, which not only embeds them further but also rapid develops your pattern-matching skill (which is essentially the same as your strategic thinking skill).

Once again, no AI allowed. Every leader of history you’ve ever admired managed to do this without that crutch, so why the hell do you need it?

Of course if you want to use it as a proof-reader, or perhaps even for some mild grammatical corrections, that’s OK. But if you’re just gonna dump a bunch of half-baked thoughts into it and say “work these up into an essay”, then truly you have no business doing what we’re talking about here. That’s an entirely different game.

___

Like I said at the outset, if your job is “content creator”, and you’re trying to get followers, then I don’t think there’s anything special here. There are better ways of achieving that.

But you’re not a content creator.

You’re something else.

And whatever that thing is, assuming it involves making decisions and leading a company, this process is going to exponentially enhance your abilities.

It also represents what I believe is a truly healthy and thoughtful integration of technology. The turgid crap that would have made this habit unworkable for most busy people has been removed, but the bit that should be hard remains hard.

As the world continues to abandon its last vestiges of independent and critical thought, the rewards for those who refuse to do so will only become greater.

I’d like to think that readers of this newsletter will be the first among them.
