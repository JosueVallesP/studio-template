# Essay moves: inventory, the reader's questions, structure, title, opening, examples

This stage reads the essay library and decides what kind of essay the material can carry. A structure the material can carry means one whose required material lines are all present in the inventory (the gate list in step 2). It does not write the essay. It ends with a format plan the draft stage can follow.

Library files (relative to this stage file):
- `../library/hook-moves.md`: 16 title moves, 9 subtitle jobs, 15 opening moves.
- `../library/body-moves.md`: 13 structures, 19 long-form inside moves, the closes, the furniture, and the pairings that hold.
- `../../fmt-linkedin-post/library/body-moves.md`: the cards for inside moves M1 to M9 and closes C1 to C6, which the essay library shares.
- `../library/essays-sorted.csv`: the index of 57 essays with every tag and relative engagement.
- `../library/essays/<ID>.md`: the full text of each essay. Read it for every example you pull.

Read the cards for the moves you are considering. Do not work from memory of them.

## Why this order

The order is material, the idea, the reader's questions, structure, then title. The library shows the title doesn't predict the body on Substack. Two things decide the essay: what the reader needs to get the idea, and what the material can carry across 1,500 words or more (a real progression, a method, a lived story, a named idea, a case). Work those out first. Then pick the title move, subtitle job and opening move that sell what the structure will deliver; the words of the title and opening get written after the body (the draft stage), unless the person already picked them (the plan's OPENING line). If you pick the title first, you'll either bend the material to fit it or ship a title the body doesn't keep.

## Step 1. Inventory what the person has

Read before you ask. In this order:

1. The brief, if one exists (`brief.md` in the piece's folder): its parent ID (usually a branch), source IDs, material and outcome.
2. `canon/core-summary.md`, then the canon entries the brief points to: the POV or branch the essay carries (`canon/person/povs.md`, `canon/person/branches.md`), the stories (`canon/bank/stories.md`), the proof (`canon/bank/proof.md`).
3. The idea bank entries the brief names, or the ones linked to the same branch or POV (`bank/ideas.md`), and the credibility entries that back them (`bank/credibility.md`).
4. The Substack channel file (`canon/strategy/channels/substack.md`, or `newsletter.md`), if it exists: the channel's job, its mix for the quarter, cadence, whether posts can be paid, what's off the table, and the selling rule. A move or a plug that works against the channel's job drops out in step 3 the same way one that works against PURPOSE does.

Fill each slot below from what you read, with the ID next to it (`STORY S4`, `POV P2`). Which entries count: `CLAUDE.md` rule 2 (a POV of forming or exploring confidence is the IDEA only with `thinks_out_loud: yes`). Then ask about the slots that are still empty, aimed at the slots most likely to be full. Three questions at most in this step; one question is one thing the person has to decide. When no person is present (a crew agent), questions go to the brief's Open questions and the piece is `briefed` or `blocked` as the strategist's rule says. When no canon exists, ask first for the slots the strongest structure needs.

Be literal. If something is not there, leave it empty.

```
IDEA        the one idea the essay carries, in one plain sentence: from a confirmed POV or branch; on a direct piece with no canon behind it, the point the person agreed to in `/essay` stage 1, or a line quoted from `material/`, `inbox/` or `said today`
RESULT      a number or outcome they can state, with a time frame
METHOD      a repeatable process or framework they actually use, with steps they can name
MODEL       a progression with levels or stages, each with signs a reader can check
STORY       a specific moment they lived, with exact details and a turn
CASE        a real piece of work (theirs or a client's) they can take apart stage by stage, with the result
BORROWED    a true story or case about a recognizable person, company or period, verifiable
SOURCE      a known text (a book, a thread, a quote, a speech) they can annotate, quoted exactly
TERM        a name they use for a pattern, with a one-sentence definition
CHANGED     a belief they held and acted on, and the evidence that changed it
OPINION     a belief some of their readers would push back on
PATTERN     something they've seen repeat across a real sample
DATA        a survey, study or analysis they ran or can cite exactly
NEWS        something recent most readers saw, with a source
ADMISSION   something true about them most people in their position wouldn't say
OBJECTIONS  the real objections or fears that stop their readers, in the readers' words
OCCASION    a reason for this essay now: readers who asked (a count helps), a reply to the last issue, an anniversary
```

Also record:

```
PURPOSE     what this essay is for: trust with buyers, leads, reach, being known for a point of view, relationships, converting readers to paid
AUDIENCE    who it's for, as a segment ID and one line
LENGTH      a guide from the channel file and the material, never a target (short essay under 1,500 words, standard 1,500 to 3,000, long guide over 3,000); the sections decide the real length
ACCESS      free, or paid with a free preview (only if the channel file allows paid posts)
```

Take PURPOSE from the brief's outcome, or from the layer mix in `canon/strategy/content-plan.md` and the channel's job. Take AUDIENCE from the brief's segment or `canon/strategy/brand-strategy.md`.

If IDEA is empty, stop. An essay without one idea is a collection. Ask for it, or offer the two or three POVs or branches the canon holds that the material fits (with no canon, the two or three ideas the material states best, each with its quoted line). On a direct piece, a point the person agreed to in stage 1 of `/essay`, or a line quoted from their sources, is enough; it doesn't need a confirmed entry.

## Step 1a. The reader's questions

Before any structure, write down what the reader needs to get the idea:

- **READER NOW.** One or two lines: what the reader (AUDIENCE) believes or does about this today, and whether they'd push back.
- **QUESTIONS.** The questions the reader would ask, in order, between the title and getting the idea, in their plain words, numbered. The first is the one the opening raises; each next one is the one the last answer raises ("Why would that be true?" "What about the obvious objection?" "How do I do it?"). Stop when the reader has the idea and can use it. An essay usually has four to eight; each one becomes a section or part of one.

These are the reader's questions, never lines of the essay: they decide what the essay covers and in what order, and the reader doesn't see them as questions. When a question needs a fact about the person the inventory doesn't hold, ask for it (inside step 1's budget) or note it; never fill it with an invented one.

## Step 2. Choose the structure the material can carry

The questions point at the structure (a run of "why would that be true?" and "what about the objection?" is an argument; "how do I do it?" in order is a protocol; "what happened, then what?" is a story essay). Each structure also has a gate: the material it needs. Read the cards in `../library/body-moves.md` for the ones whose gate opens and that answer the questions.

| Structure | Needs (from inventory) | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| E1. The argument | IDEA as a claim + two or three reasons, each with a real example + the real objection | point of view |
| E2. The diagnosis | a problem the AUDIENCE has now + a cause you can show (DATA, BORROWED, PATTERN) + a prescription that follows from the cause | trust, reach inside a defined audience |
| E3. The named idea as a lens | TERM (or a borrowed named idea, credited) + three or more places it shows up + a way to apply it to the reader | being remembered for an idea |
| E4. The ladder | MODEL with real levels, signs for each, proof at each rung | trust, saves, being known for a way of thinking |
| E5. The story essay | STORY with a turn + the IDEA it proves + ideally a second case | relationships, being seen as a person |
| E6. Changing my mind | CHANGED: a belief really held, evidence in the order it came | trust, point of view |
| E7. The worked case | CASE or RESULT + METHOD, in enough detail to pull a rule from each stage | trust, leads, saves (the best performing structure in the library) |
| E8. The protocol | METHOD with followable steps + a reason the usual approach fails | saves, leads |
| E9. The explained list | items that each earn a section + a way to turn them into use | saves |
| E10. The annotated source | SOURCE + notes that add something the source doesn't say | reach through the source |
| E11. The resolved paradox | two things the reader sees as opposed + a resolution beyond "balance" | reach, reposts |
| E12. The objections answered | OBJECTIONS + an answer for each that's a reason, not encouragement | trust |
| E13. The book | a complete body of thought, LENGTH long guide | saves, a canonical piece |

Procedure:

1. Mark every structure whose gate is open. Usually two to four.
2. Keep the ones that answer the reader's questions in roughly their order. A structure that leaves one of the questions unanswered, or adds sections no question asks for, is the wrong one for this idea.
3. Filter by PURPOSE and the channel's job.
4. Rank by the strength of the material. A model with levels the reader can check beats a list of tips. A case with numbers beats a method described in the abstract.
5. Pick one, and say in one line what in the inventory makes it available. Name the runner-up.

If no gate opens, say what would open one: "If you can name the three levels and how you'd know you're on each, the ladder becomes available."

## Step 3. Choose the title move

Read the title cards in `../library/hook-moves.md` for the moves you consider.

| Title move | Needs | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| T1. Proof, then payoff | RESULT with exact numbers + a payoff from that result | opens, saves, leads |
| T2. The how-to with the catch removed | METHOD that works under a condition the reader doubts (time, luck, expertise) | opens, saves |
| T3. Think like the person you want to be | a real model of how an admired kind of person thinks + proof you crossed the gap | reach, saves |
| T4. The hidden reason | a problem the AUDIENCE feels + a cause they wouldn't look for | reach |
| T5. The plain command | a behavior the reader avoids + the reason they avoid it + a story or proof in the body | relationships, loyalty |
| T6. The paradox | two things that seem to cancel + a real resolution | reach, reposts |
| T7. The coined term | TERM + a subtitle that translates it | being remembered for an idea |
| T8. The arguable claim | OPINION specific enough to be wrong | point of view |
| T9. The reader, named, with stakes | a precise AUDIENCE in their own words + a real consequence | reach inside an audience |
| T10. The shift | a change you can show + what replaces the old thing | point of view |
| T11. The saying, explained | a saying the reader knows + the mechanism behind it | reach |
| T12. The reader's own question | the question readers actually ask (from replies or calls) | trust (thin card) |
| T13. What happened to me | an event the readers would care about + an existing relationship | relationships (thin card) |
| T14. What you're missing | a specific capability or benefit + a method to get it | opens from existing readers |
| T15. The whole thing | a complete work given away | saves (thin card) |
| T16. The numbered promise | a real list + a credential | saves |

Procedure:

1. Mark the title moves whose gates open and that fit what the structure delivers. A ladder can carry T3, T9 or T12; a worked case can carry T1 or T2; a named idea as a lens carries T4 or T7. The pairings that hold in the library are in `../library/body-moves.md` ("The title doesn't pick the structure"); use them where they apply and the gate where they don't.
2. Filter by PURPOSE. Rank by material. Offer two or three, each with one line on what makes it available, and say which you'd pick.
3. Check each against its "how it fails" list now.

## Step 4. Choose the subtitle job and the opening

**Subtitle.** Ask what the title leaves the reader unsure about, and pick the job from the table in `../library/hook-moves.md` part 2. The rules from the library: a coined term, a riddle or a bare quote needs U1 (explains) or U5 (tells the story); a big or abstract promise needs U2 (makes the payoff concrete); a title that could be for anyone needs U3 (names the reader). U0 (none) only when the title is complete alone.

**Opening.** Mark the opening moves whose material is there (`../library/hook-moves.md` part 3). If the voice guide sets a default opening, start there. Otherwise prefer the one that gets to the reader's own problem fastest with what the person has. Then write down the promise the opening will end on: what the reader will have by the end of the essay, in one sentence.

## Step 5. Plan the sections, the inside moves, the close and the furniture

1. **Sections.** From the reader's questions, list the sections in order, each with the question it answers, its job in a few words and the material ID it uses. Every section serves the IDEA. Then hold the list against the structure card's parts as a check: a part the card names that no section covers means a question was missed or the idea doesn't need that part; say which in one line.
2. **Inside moves.** From the structure card and `../library/body-moves.md` layer 2, list the ones this structure needs. For a standard or long essay add the moves that hold a long piece together: a map up front (N2) when there are more than three sections, a bridge at the end of each section (N4), the coined term (N1) if there is one, and the one idea coming back in every section.
3. **Close.** Pick from the closes in `../library/body-moves.md` layer 3 and the LinkedIn closes C1 to C6. The restated principle is the default in the library and performs at the median; prefer a close that hands the reader something to do or be, unless the material gives a reason.
4. **Furniture.** If ACCESS is paid: the paywall goes after the free reader has received something whole, and before the extension (the full method, the templates, the worked examples). Never at the turn. Plugs and asks follow the channel's selling rule; none inside the essay unless the channel file allows it.

## Step 6. Pull examples

1. Filter `../library/essays-sorted.csv` for essays with the chosen structure, then the chosen title move. Sort by relative engagement, leaving out the rows marked as a flat set or a cross-post artifact for performance purposes (they're still fine examples of craft). Take two or three.
2. Read each in full from `../library/essays/<ID>.md`, with its notes. Notice what each section does, not how it's worded.

## Output: the format plan

Write the brief into the piece's `brief.md`, made from `templates/piece/brief.md`. If `/post` or `/essay` already started it, fill what's missing and leave the rest.

The shared sections as the template asks (the one idea, reader, outcome, not saying, call to action, open questions, and the parent line). **Purpose** from step 1. **Material**: the filled slots, each as the template's Material section says.

The format plan, which this skill owns:

```
IDEA            the one idea, one sentence, with its POV or branch ID, or on a direct piece the stage 1 point or the quoted line
READER NOW      what the reader believes or does about this today, and whether they'd push back (1a)
QUESTIONS       the reader's questions in order, numbered (1a)
STRUCTURE       code and name, why it's available, the runner-up
TITLE MOVE      code and name, why it's available and what it buys
SUBTITLE JOB    code and name, what the title leaves unsure
OPENING MOVE    code and name; the promise the opening ends on
OPENING         the title and opening lines the person picked, as they saw them or rewrote them (direct path, `/essay` stage 2); otherwise none
SECTIONS        numbered, each with the question it answers, its job and the material ID it uses
INSIDE MOVES    the ones the structure needs, plus the long-form holding moves
CLOSE           which one, and why
LENGTH          in plain words, from the sections; a guide, never a target
ACCESS          free, or paid with the paywall's place named
EXAMPLES        IDs of the pulled essays, with each title as the CSV holds it
WATCH FOR       the "how it fails" items for the structure, title and opening that this material is closest to tripping
```

If no piece folder exists, give the person the same plan in the reply.

Hand it to the draft stage. The examples show the job done well; the draft does the same job with this person's material. It does not copy an example's shape or wording.
