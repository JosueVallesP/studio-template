# calibration 2E: the studio's draft and the person's version

## The studio wrote

Rick Rubin was asked on a podcast if he uses AI for music. He said no, and his reason has stuck with me: he likes the artists he likes because of how they see the world, not how they sound. AI can make something sound like anything, but it doesn't have a point of view to show you.
I think the same is true for writing.
If your AI drafts sound generic, tweaking the tone won't fix them. The problem is that there's nothing underneath, because the AI was never told what you actually believe.
So before you rewrite the prompt again, write down what you think about your field and why. That's the part no tool can give you.

## The person wrote

Someone asked Rick Rubin whether he uses AI for music. He said no.
I'm sure I don't need to tell you how relevant this is for your writing too.
The vast majority of people spend a lot of time fixing "their voice" or prompting AI so it sounds "more like them," when the solution starts much deeper and much closer to yourself.
What do you believe that others don't? How do you see the world? What's your POV?
When your answer to those questions is clear, you'll start sounding like you.
