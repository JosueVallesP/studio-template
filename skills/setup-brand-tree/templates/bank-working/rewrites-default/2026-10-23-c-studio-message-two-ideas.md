# calibration 1C: the studio's draft and the person's version

## The studio wrote

I read Tuesday's call with Dana and found two ideas that can stand on their own.
The first is your point that clients don't need more content, they need fewer pieces they can stand behind. It's clear, and it pushes against how content is usually sold. I've kept it as an idea for a post, waiting for your yes.
The second is the story about the onboarding call that went sideways. It's a good story, but right now it only makes sense if you know that client. Who else runs into the same problem? Your answer is what will let it work for readers outside that one project.
The rest of the call was either too tied to that project or something your readers already know, so I left it out.

## The person wrote

I read Tuesday's call with Dana and found two ideas worth exploring:
1. Why clients don't need more content: the idea is bold enough and different enough to ____.
2. The onboarding call that went sideways story: it's a good story, but right now it only make sense to you and that client. If we could find a way to make the idea a bit more "transferable," though, that'd give it a strong appeal. Do you know anyone who experienced something similar or why____?
The rest of the call was just basic housekeeping, so that's it for now.

## What changed

- "I've kept it as an idea for a post, waiting for your yes" cut: the studio narrating its own filing · rule: S5
- two ideas became a numbered list, each with a one-line title · rule: S2
- "Your answer is what will let it work" → "If we could find a way to make the idea a bit more transferable": the studio speaks as a collaborator · rule: S9
- "too tied to that project or something your readers already know" → "basic housekeeping": less said about what was left out · rule: S5
