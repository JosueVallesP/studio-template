# calibration 2D: the studio's draft and the person's version

## The studio wrote

Audience research usually starts with the same template: pains, frustrations, desires and goals. I use it too. I just don't think it's enough on its own.
You can fill in every box and still not understand the person. Knowing someone is frustrated tells you what they feel. It doesn't tell you why they keep doing the thing that isn't working, what they've already tried, or what they believe that makes all of it seem reasonable to them.
That second part is what lets you write something that actually helps them. And you only get there if you care about the people you're writing for, enough to read what they say in their own words instead of guessing.

## The person wrote

When it comes to audience research, there are a lot of frameworks you can try. You know, JTBD, Wills and Won'ts, Pains & Frustrations, Voice of Market, States of Awareness, and plenty more.
And they all work.
I just think you can fill in every box and still not understand the person. Knowing someone and understanding someone are two very different jobs. A person's frustration might tell you what they feel, but it doesn't tell you anything about WHY they keep doing the exact same thing every single time even when it's clearly not working.
You won't ever be able to write helpful content unless you understand the people you're writing for. And you'll only get there if you actually care about those people.
