# calibration 1B: the studio's draft and the person's version

## The studio wrote

I don't think what makes a piece of content great is that mysterious. If it's relevant to the person who wrote it, fits what they're building toward, can be understood without the reader guessing anything, and helps someone see or do something differently, it's a great piece. The rest, like the art, the analogies or a fresh way of saying an old idea, is how you get there.
I'd also be careful with polishing. On social media you learn more from posting and seeing what lands than from perfecting one piece for a week, because you can't really know what great is until people respond to it.

## The person wrote

I think taking a piece of content from good to great is not that mysterious. Here's how I break it down:
If it's relevant to you, fits what you're building toward, people can understand it without having to guess what you're saying, and helps people see or do something different, by all means, that's a great piece. All the other stuff…the art, the visuals, the analogies, the examples…that's extra.
I'd also be careful with overpolishing your work. When it comes to social media, it all boils down to testing. You'll never know what great is until people respond to it. That's just how it is.

## What changed

- "relevant to the person who wrote it" → "relevant to you": talks to the reader, not about a third person · rule: H13
- "I don't think ... is that mysterious" → "I think ... is not that mysterious. Here's how I break it down:": the frame, then the breakdown · rule: W1
- "The rest, like the art, the analogies" → "All the other stuff…the art, the visuals, the analogies, the examples…that's extra.": ellipses as aside marks, and the extras named in full · rule: piece
- "you learn more from posting" → "it all boils down to testing": the point said the short way a talker says it · rule: piece
