# calibration 3G: the studio's draft and the person's version

## The studio wrote

I just think most people don't know what they think. I don't mean that as an insult. You don't really find out what you believe about something until you try to put it into words.
That's why journaling feels so liberating. When you know nobody's going to read it or judge it, you can write the half-baked version of an idea and see where it goes.
The catch is that free writing is hard. Give someone a blank page and they'll either freeze or write three pages about nothing.
What helps is a better question. "What do you believe about leadership?" is almost impossible to answer well. "What happened at work this week that you'd tell a friend about?" gets you a story, and the belief is usually sitting right there inside it.

## The person wrote

Most people don't really know what they think, and I don't say that as an insult. I just think you can't really find out what you believe about anything until you sit down and try to put it into words on a piece of paper.
This is why journaling is so liberating. The bare idea of writing knowing that no one will judge you allows you to actually say what you want to say, and find where you don't have anything to say.
But it's hard.
Give me a blank page and I'll either freeze or write 3 pages about nothing.
A little trick I've been trying recently makes it a lot easier.
It boils down to shifting the question a bit so it forces you to find the specifics.
"What do you believe about leadership?" for example, it is very abstract. There are so many things you believe about that, so you can't find any.
Compare it to "What happened at work this week that you'd tell a friend about?"
Much easier.
That question gets you a story, a belief, and so many details.
