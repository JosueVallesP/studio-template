# Routing: where to start and how to plan sessions

Everyone ends up with the same canon. People just arrive with different parts of it already clear. Start where the person has the most clarity, then work outward. Don't walk the layers in order because the model is drawn that way.

## Reading the intake

Look at three things from the intake:

1. **How much material they shared.** A book, years of posts, or more than about twenty pieces counts as a lot (a starting default; revisit after a few real runs). Up to twenty posts, a few talks or a sales page counts as some. Nothing in hand counts as none, even if they own plenty; what they'll bring goes into the next session's plan.
2. **How they think.** Out loud or in writing.
3. **What feels clearest.** Beliefs, the business, the big idea, or none.

## Where to start

There's no fixed order through the layers, only dependencies (`../SKILL.md`, "No fixed order, only dependencies"): values, behaviors and POVs in any order; the philosophy after three confirmed POVs; the thesis after the philosophy and the goal. Start where the person has the most to tell.

### 1. Lots of material

Start with the map (`skills/job-map`). Draft candidates for every layer the material supports, then use the sessions to confirm, kill and sharpen them. Spend the saved time on what material can't fill: what each value beats and cost, each POV's ground and condition, what would prove the thesis wrong, and the stop list.

Watch for this: published material shows who the person wants to appear to be. Test extracted values against real decisions before confirming them.

### 2. Strong opinions, little material

Start with POVs. These people have stories of things working or failing for a reason they can name; ask for those, and leave who else missed it to the follow-up. The stories usually carry costly choices too, which lead to values. Their answers are the main source for voice, so encourage voice dictation.

### 3. A working business, no clear big idea

Start with the goal and the vehicle. Then ask for client stories: the result a client got that others in their field don't get, a thing a client asked for that they refused, a client they had to un-teach. Those stories feed values, behaviors and POVs, and the thesis comes once the philosophy is in.

### 4. A clear big idea already

Take the big idea as the thesis's first draft. It can't be confirmed until there's a philosophy and a goal under it, so run POVs next, from stories, and check each one against the big idea as it lands: does it support it, branch from it, or pull against it? People with a clear thesis sometimes hold it so tightly they skip the reasons. The ground of each POV is where the reasons show up.

## When two rows are true

Material in hand and a clear answer to "what feels clearest" (b or c) often both apply. Rule: **material first, then route the interview on their answer.** With a lot of material, reading it back is the whole first session (the person confirms, kills and sharpens candidates and answers no fresh questions); the interview starts the session after, from the entry point their answer gives. With some material, extraction takes the first part of session 1 and the entry-point layer the rest. Say which it will be at the start of the session so the person knows what the session is for.

## Thinking style

- **Out loud:** keep questions short and conversational. Encourage voice dictation. Ask for stories often.
- **In writing:** you can ask slightly bigger questions and give them room. They may prefer to answer a few questions in one go between sessions. That's fine; follow up on an answer when the follow-up rule in `SKILL.md` says to.

## Planning sessions

Each session covers one layer, about 20 to 30 minutes. The map shortens everything after it. A person who starts from nothing usually needs eleven or twelve sessions to reach the minimum canon (a starting default): two or three on values and behaviors, two on POVs, one on the philosophy, one on the goal, one on the thesis, and four shorter ones for the stop list, a branch, the audience and the voice. They don't have to finish before they make anything: the first post and the job commands work from material, and each confirmed layer makes the next piece better.

Suggest the next session from two things, in this order:

1. **What the person needs now.** A piece waiting on a POV, a strategy waiting on the thesis, a manifesto waiting on the philosophy.
2. **What they have the most material for.** A journal full of turning points points to values; a run of client calls points to POVs.

Say the reason in a sentence. Write the plan under "Next session" in `progress.md` and update it at the end of every session.

**Material they have but didn't bring.** Route on what's in hand, write what they'll bring under "Next session" in `progress.md`, and plan extraction for that session. If they offer a story instead, take the story.
