# The philosophy session

One session, about 20 minutes. It ends with one thread handed back for a yes. Read `../SKILL.md` ("The method" and "The shape of every session") first; this file holds only what's particular to the philosophy.

## What a philosophy is

- **Is:** the model underneath that explains why their POVs belong together: the one picture of how their world works that those beliefs come from.
- **Isn't:** a list of POVs, a mission statement, or a set of values.
- **The evidence it leaves:** a premise several confirmed POVs share without ever saying it.

So this session asks the person nothing directly. The studio does the seeing; the person decides whether it's the thread.

## It needs three confirmed POVs

Don't run this session with fewer than three confirmed POVs. If there are fewer, say so in a sentence and suggest the POV interview instead: "The philosophy is what your opinions have in common, so it needs at least three of them confirmed first. Want to do the POV interview?"

## Finding the thread

Lay the confirmed POVs side by side, with their ground and conditions. For each pair, ask what would have to be true for both to hold. The premise that sits under the most of them is the candidate thread.

Write it two ways:

1. **One plain sentence** a reader could repeat: "Trust is built by what you refuse to do, not by what you say."
2. **A short paragraph** (half a page at most) that shows how each POV follows from it, citing the POVs by ID in brackets, using the person's words where they had them.

Three outcomes are normal:

- **One thread explains them all.** Hand it back.
- **Two threads.** Hand back both and ask which one is central, or whether they're really two sides of one thing. Don't merge them yourself.
- **A POV that fits nothing.** Say which one, and ask: does it need a condition, is it a different kind of thing (a value or a stop-list item), or does it point to a second thread? The person decides.

## The handback

> **The thread under your POVs**
> Trust comes from what you're willing to give up, and everything you believe about content follows from that.
> - Fewer pieces you stand behind beat more content [P3], because each piece costs you something to put your name on.
> - Telling clients the truth wins the long game [P1], because the truth costs you the client sometimes.
> - Selling through relationships beats reach [P5], because a relationship costs time a stranger can't fake.
>
> Is this the thread, or is it something close to this?

## The four lenses, as checks

Once they say yes, check the thread through the four lenses. These are tests to look at, not boxes to fill, and a gap is a flag, not a failure.

| Lens | The check | If there's a gap, ask about an event |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What's true (ontology) | What does the thread say is true about their world? | Usually answered by the thread itself |
| How we know (epistemology) | What counts as proof for them? | "Tell me about the moment you first saw this was true." |
| How to read it (hermeneutics) | How do they think people should read what happens in their field? | "Tell me about a success story in your field everyone gets wrong. What did they miss?" |
| What it's for (teleology) | What's the work for? | "Tell me about a piece of work you're proudest of. What did it change for someone?" |

## The gate

The philosophy is confirmed when it explains at least three confirmed POVs and the person says it's the thread. The layer is done when, on top of that, no confirmed POV contradicts it and the lenses are checked (`../references/tests.md`). Record which POVs it explains in the `explains` line of `canon/person/philosophy.md`.

## What it makes possible

Essays and frameworks: pieces that explain how the person's world works rather than one opinion about it. The philosophy is also the founding claim of the manifesto, and the thesis session needs it.
