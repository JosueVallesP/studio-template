# The behaviors interview

One session, about 20 to 30 minutes. It ends with one to three behaviors handed back for a yes. Read `../SKILL.md` ("The method" and "The shape of every session") first; this file holds only what's particular to behaviors.

## What a behavior is

- **Is:** a repeated act or refusal that follows from a value, specific enough that someone could film it.
- **Isn't:** a trait ("I'm curious", "I'm thorough"), which nobody can film, or a one-off event, which is a story.
- **The evidence it leaves:** "every time"; rules they keep that nobody asked them to; habits a new colleague would find odd. Refusals count and often say more than actions.

So the questions point at routines, rules and habits, asked through the last time they happened.

## Before you start

Read `canon/person/behaviors.md`, the confirmed values, and the stories on file. Refusals often turn up in the values session ("I never take equity"); start from those when they're there.

## The questions

| Ask | Why it works |
| --- | --- |
| "Walk me through your first week with a new client. What do you do, in order?" | A routine is behavior in sequence, and the odd steps stand out |
| "Is there a rule you keep in your work that nobody asked you to keep?" | Self-imposed rules are behaviors with a value behind them |
| "What's something you do that a new colleague once asked you about, or thought was strange?" | Someone else noticing it is proof it's real and visible |
| "What's something you stopped doing on purpose? When did you stop, and what happened?" | A deliberate stop is a refusal with a date on it |
| "Tell me about the last time a client asked for something you wouldn't do." | Refusals under pressure are the clearest kind |

Then follow up for the gate:

- **It repeats:** "When did you last do that? And the time before?" One instance is a story; two or three is a behavior.
- **It can be filmed:** "If I were in the room, what would I see you do?"
- **Why:** "Why do you do it that way?" The answer usually points to a confirmed value. If it points somewhere new, that's a value candidate for the values session.

## Pushback

| The answer | The pushback |
| --- | --- |
| A trait ("I'm detail-oriented") | "What would I see you do that shows it?" |
| An intention ("I try to always...") | "When did you last actually do it?" |
| One event | "Has it happened again since?" If not, it's a story; file it as one. |
| Polished | "What's the version you'd say to a friend?" |

## Finding the behavior

Write it as something a person could watch: subject, verb, object, the condition. "Reads every document a client sends before the first call, even when it's a hundred pages." Mark it action or refusal. Link it to the value it comes from by ID. If no value is confirmed yet, leave `from` empty and write the candidate value in the log; the behavior stays a draft until that value is confirmed. Never ask "which value is that from?" of someone with no values written down.

## The handback

Numbered, a one-line title each, the behavior as something you could film, the value it ties to, and their lines under it.

> 2. **Reads everything before the first call**
> Before the first call with a new client, you read every document they send, even a hundred pages. It comes from telling clients the truth (V1): you won't give an opinion on what you haven't read.
> From session 3: "I've never once walked into a kickoff without reading the whole folder. Clients notice."

Then: tell me if I misread something or if there's a part I'm missing.

## The gate

A behavior is confirmed when it's observable, it repeats (at least two instances, with one dated), it ties to a confirmed value, every field under Behaviors in `../references/fields.md` is filled, and the person says it's theirs. Ask whether each story behind it can be public, public without names, or private.

## What it makes possible

With values, behaviors make the voice and the stop list possible, and they're where the person's vow in the manifesto comes from later: a behavior stated so someone could check it. They're also the commonest source of story posts.
