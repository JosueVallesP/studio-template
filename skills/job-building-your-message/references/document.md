# The Messaging Framework document

Fill it only with what the session, the canon and the person's Personal Monopoly document hold. Saved to `canon/strategy/messaging-framework.md` as a draft.

**Length:** 1,000 to 1,500 words. If a section's material is thin, keep it short and say it's thin; padding with generic advice makes them unable to tell which parts came from them.

**Voice:** second person, to them, in their words. `skills/craft-house-rules` applies to every line. Every claim about them carries its source in brackets at the end of its paragraph (a canon ID, `material/<file>`, or `said today`). The About section comes from the judged piece in `pieces/<date>-about-section/`.

---

```markdown

# Your Messaging Framework
*<date>*

> <the expanded thesis agreed in stage 5>

This is the line every piece answers to. A piece that serves it gets made; one that doesn't, doesn't.

<Each moment as a short paragraph in their voice, with all four parts: situation, action, result, lesson. Two or three, at least one a failure.>

**How to use these:** <One or two sentences. They're the proof, and they belong in the About section, in talks and at the start of posts. The failure goes first more often than feels comfortable, because it's the one people trust and retell.>

## 2. Your enemy

**You're fighting:** <the enemy as a belief or practice, in one sentence>

**What it costs the people you serve:** <the concrete cost from stage 2, with the case they gave>

**Why they can't escape it alone:** <from stage 2>

**Your position, in public:** <Two or three sentences the way they'd say them: the claim against the enemy and the better way, defensible with their own work. Often the recurring theme of their content.>

**Parked:** <other candidate enemies from stage 2, one line each, for later>

## 3. Why you

<Their stakes and investment proof as a short story. Only events that happened. If the honest version is modest, write the modest version and say why it's enough.>

## 4. What you're building

<The vision as a condition, in one or two sentences. "Together we're building..." if that fits their voice.>

---

## Decisions

<Five to seven, each in this shape:>

### <n>. <The decision, as a sentence they could say out loud>

**Why:** <What they said that this comes from, named, with its source.>
**First move:** <One action, small enough to do this week.>
**You'll know it worked when:** <A signal someone could observe, not a feeling.>

---

## The next 30 days

<Three to five items in order, concrete, the first doable in under an hour.>

<An honest list: authority moments without numbers, an enemy they haven't yet opposed in public, stakes that need a real event behind them. If nothing is thin, say so and name what would strengthen it.>

<One line per canon file touched, with the IDs, all as drafts. Then: the interviews confirm these, so you won't be asked them again.>

<Three or four lines: the expanded thesis filters content, the enemy is the recurring theme, the authority moments are the proof to rotate through, and the framework gets revisited in 90 days.>
```

## Which decisions to include

Five to seven, from what the session found. Every one traces to something they said here or in their positioning.

- **The About section.** The text itself, 100 to 150 words, built from the thesis, one authority moment and the enemy: the judged version from the piece folder. It's the decision they'll use first.
- **The content pillars.** Three or four recurring themes that fall out of the framework: the enemy and its cost, the lessons inside each authority moment, the method, the vision. For each, one post they could write this month, drawn from their own material, with the material named. Each pillar is a branch (`canon/person/branches.md`); if a strategy exists, say which of this quarter's branches each one is.
- **The content filter.** The question they run every idea through, built from their thesis: "does this prove I'm the one person who <their monopoly>, and does it fight <their enemy>?", in their words.
- **Which story leads.** The authority moment that should open their About section and their next talk, and why that one.
- **The first public shot at the enemy.** The first post where they take their position in public, with the angle and the proof they'd use. If they've never opposed the enemy publicly, this is usually the most important decision. The post itself is `/post`'s job.
- **What to stop saying.** Phrases, claims or kinds of content in their current material that contradict the framework or sit at the first or second level. Name them, quoted, if they shared a bio or posts.
- **Proof to gather.** If an authority moment lacks its number, or the stakes lack their event, go and get it: ask the old client for the figure, write down the date and the amount.

## Rules for the decisions

- Trace every decision to its source or cut it.
- Make the first move small enough for this week.
- Make the signal observable.
- If two decisions depend on each other, order them and say so.
- Draft the words wherever words are the deliverable: "rewrite your About section" is advice; a drafted, judged About section is a decision they can act on.
