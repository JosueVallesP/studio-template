# Stage 1: Find the mechanism

The mechanism lives where the person's best client outcomes overlap. The studio finds it in what happened with real clients, never by asking the person to describe their method in the abstract.

## 1. The client success audit

The person picks their ten most successful clients, or five if they don't have ten, from memory or from `canon/bank/proof.md` and `stories.md`. For each client, ask four questions, one at a time, about what happened:

**Q1. "What did this client have to understand before things changed for them?"** The insight everything else rested on.

**Q2. "What did you have to correct in how this client saw things?"** The wrong belief they came in with, and where it showed up in the work.

**Q3. "What did you find yourself explaining to them more than once?"** The framework or process that came up again and again on the calls.

**Q4. "What was the moment they got unstuck? What were you talking about when it happened?"** The single realization that shifted things.

Write each answer in the person's words, with the client (as they'd like to name them) and the source. Where the four answers overlap across clients is the mechanism.

The case behind the method: when The Wealthy Consultant ran this audit across ten clients, the answer to all four questions kept coming back to one idea they came to call the Revolving Price, stacking recurring revenue on top of an upfront fee. It was the thing clients had to understand first, the belief to correct, the framework taught again and again, and the moment of release.

If the answers don't overlap, say so. The person may have two methods, or none yet that a pamphlet can carry. Either is a true result; don't force one.

## 2. Current state, desired state, gap

For the segment, from the research in `canon/strategy/audience.md` and the client stories:

- **Current state:** where they are now, in their words. What hurts, what keeps them up at night (only what the research or the person's clients show).
- **Desired state:** where they want to be, specific and concrete: a desire from the audience file, or what a client said they wanted.
- **The gap:** what's missing between the two. This is where the mechanism lives.

The case: for the Revolving Price, the current state was spending too much on ads, client acquisition costs too high and a feast-and-famine cycle; the desired state was predictable revenue and profitable growth; the gap was a way to spread the cost of finding a client over a longer relationship, which stacking recurring revenue does.

The pamphlet teaches the mechanism at the level of the idea and is honest about what putting it fully into practice takes.

## 3. The five things a working mechanism has

Walk through each with the person. A mechanism missing one isn't ready for a whole pamphlet.

**1. A name of its own.** A name only the person uses, so it can be remembered, searched for and owned. The cases: "Revolving Price" instead of "recurring revenue". The name follows the naming gate in `skills/job-shape`: the pattern it names already shows up in the person's material at least three times. Otherwise it's a label on a one-off. See section 8.

**2. A problem many people share.** Widespread enough that much of the segment feels it, which the audience file's counts show. A problem three people have can't carry a pamphlet.

**3. An insight that goes against what the field believes.** The usual advice rests on a premise that fails, and the mechanism is what the person does instead. Section 4.

**4. Simple to grasp, real work to do well.** The reader can understand it in one reading. Doing it fully takes judgment, practice, and pitfalls the person can name from real clients. This is what makes the person's paid help worth having; it must be true of the method as it is, never engineered.

**5. A change that lasts.** Not a one-time fix or a tactic. It changes how the reader runs their work from then on.

## 4. The contrarian angle

The shape:

> "The usual advice is to <X>, but that leads to <the outcome the research shows> because <the premise that fails>. What I do instead is <the person's approach>, which gets <the result their clients got>."

In the studio, each blank has a source:

- **The usual advice** comes from the peer landscape in `audience.md` (`/understand`), marked as said everywhere or by a few. Not from a hunch about what "most people" say.
- **The premise that fails** is tested with `skills/craft-copylosophy`. A contrarian angle with a premise that holds up is reasoned; one without is a pose.
- **What the person does instead** is a confirmed POV or a branch, or the person's words today.
- **The result** is a proof entry or the person's words, with the client's permission.

A good angle challenges what the reader believes, feels uncomfortable but true once explained, shows the usual approach as incomplete rather than stupid, and makes the mechanism feel like the obvious correction.

The case: "Everyone teaches coaches to just charge more per client. But that keeps you in the feast-and-famine cycle, because you're always one bad month away from disaster. The answer is a different pricing structure, not more clients or higher prices."

## 5. IMPACT

Score the mechanism 1 to 10 on each letter, the person first, then the studio's read with its reason:

- **I, interest.** Do people in the segment ask about this when it comes up? Evidence: the questions in `bank/questions.md`, comments, the counts in `audience.md`. When Taylor Welch mentioned the Revolving Price in a Facebook group, the comments filled up; that's the kind of evidence to look for in the person's own channels.
- **M, market demand.** Are people actively trying to solve this, and spending money on things that aren't working (`tried`)?
- **P, personal authority.** Has the person lived the problem, and do they have a process for it, a thing they can actually sell?
- **A, audience sophistication.** Will the segment understand it as written? `audience.md`'s `sophistication` and awareness stage answer this: a mechanism pitched far above how the reader thinks needs different words, more teaching, or a different reader.
- **C, competitive difference.** Is it different from what everyone else in the peer landscape says? An angle nobody else takes is room to work in.
- **T, transformation.** Does it change how the reader works for good, not once?

## 6. The four tests

**The dinner-table test.** Can the person explain the problem and the mechanism in 60 seconds or less? If not, it's too broad or too intricate. The bones are simple; the depth is in doing it.

**The doing-it-alone test.** If a reader tried this with only the pamphlet, where would they get stuck? The person names the real pitfalls from real clients. If there are none, the pamphlet may be the whole product, which is fine, and the path up is a different offer.

**The authority test.** When the person explains it, do people hear it as theirs, or as "I heard someone say that" or "I could have worked that out"? Ask the person what happened the last time they explained it to someone.

**The premium test.** Is there enough depth that, at a higher level, someone would pay for a guided version, access to the person or their other systems? The original sets this at $5,000 to $20,000; the person's own higher-priced offer is the real benchmark.

## 7. The person scores it

The person rates themselves 1 to 10 on four things, before the studio offers its read:

1. **Clarity:** can they explain it clearly?
2. **Difference:** is it unlike what others in the field say?
3. **Simple to grasp, real work to do:** both true?
4. **It shows what they know:** does explaining it make that plain?

A score below 6 on any is the next thing to work on, before any writing.

## 8. Naming it

The words a person owns are part of what makes the method theirs, and every name passes two gates: it names a pattern already in their material (the naming gate in `skills/job-shape`), and they'd say it out loud to a client without wincing. Ways to get there, as possibilities:

1. **Name each stage of the process** they already walk clients through.
2. **An acronym,** only when the letters spell something the person would say (IMPACT works because it's a word).
3. **A metaphor from everyday life** that maps how the parts relate, not how they look (`skills/job-shape`, the transfer move): "a business as a car", where the parts and their jobs line up.
4. **Rename a common idea** for the person's specific version of it: "Revolving Price" for one kind of recurring revenue.
5. **Borrow a term from another industry,** the way the Revolving Price borrowed the insurance industry's "float".
6. **Coin a word** only when nothing borrowed fits.

Weak and strong positioning, as the shape to aim for (these are made-up shapes, not real cases):

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| "I help people with time management" | "I built <named method> after seeing why most productivity systems make founders less effective" |
| "I teach marketing" | "I made <named system> when I saw why most marketing advice eats the margin" |

The person's own version comes from their client audit. Every coined term the person takes goes on the "Terms they coined" line of `canon/voice/voice-guide.md` marked `draft`, with its plain definition and source.

## 9. The mechanism brief

Save it as `pieces/<yyyy-mm-dd>-pamphlet-<slug>/mechanism.md`, every field with its source:

1. **Name**
2. **Definition**, in one or two sentences
3. **Contrarian angle**, the full sentence, with the source of each blank
4. **Current state, desired state, gap**
5. **The five things**, each confirmed or named as missing
6. **IMPACT scores**, the person's and the studio's
7. **The four tests**, pass or fail with the reason
8. **The person's scores**
9. **The words**: every name, acronym and metaphor, each with where it shows up in their material
10. **The analogy** that makes the mechanism easy to grasp

Then the drafts, along the route in `skills/setup-brand-tree/SKILL.md`, "Where a picked-up belief goes": the mechanism as a `draft` branch entry in `canon/person/branches.md` (a change to a confirmed branch, or a connection across confirmed entries, goes to `proposals.md` as `change to B<n>` or `seen`), the terms as `draft` vocabulary in the voice guide, each with its evidence and one question for the person.
