# Stage 4: The emails and the path up

Contents:
1. Test with people who already know the person
2. Ads, if they use them
3. The emails after purchase
4. The path to the higher-priced work
5. What to read after the first sales

Every product, price, result and testimonial in this stage comes from the person and passes the truth tests in `skills/craft-sales-copy`. Nothing here is a benchmark. Where a number appears, it's a starting default the person can change, and the stage says so.

---

## 1. Test with people who already know the person

The first buyers are the people who already read, follow or work with the person: their email list, their followers, past clients, a community they're part of. They're the cheapest test there is, and they answer the question that matters before any money goes into ads: do people who know this person want this pamphlet at this price?

Ask, from the files first and then the person:

- Where do people who already know them gather? A list, a channel, a group, past clients.
- How do they usually tell those people about something new? Use that way, in their voice.
- What would they count as a good first result? The person picks it; the studio doesn't set one.

The announcement is a piece through `skills/craft-sales-copy` (copy kind `nurture email` for a list, `invitation` for a post or a direct message). It says plainly what the pamphlet is, who it's for, the price, and that the person has higher-priced work (`skills/craft-sales-copy`, truth test 6).

Write down what happened in `pieces/<...>/funnel.md`: how many people heard about it, how many bought, what they said. Questions and replies from buyers are first-party research; offer `/understand` with them as its source.

## 2. Ads, if they use them

Only after the warm test shows people buy. If the person doesn't run ads, skip this section; the pamphlet works as a first product without them.

When they do:

- The ad's only job is to bring the right reader to the sales page. Its words come from the same places as the page (`skills/craft-sales-copy`, "Where the words come from"), the reader's own words first.
- The point of the funnel's checkout add-on and after-purchase offers (`references/sales-funnel.md`) is to pay back what it cost to find the buyer. Whether it does is read from the person's own numbers once real buyers come through, never from someone else's results.
- Change one thing at a time (the headline, the audience, the offer after purchase) so the person can tell what made the difference.

Each ad line is a piece through `skills/craft-sales-copy` (copy kind `ad line`).

## 3. The emails after purchase

A buyer has paid, so they've already said yes once. The emails that follow keep the pamphlet's promise and open the door to the next step, in that order.

A starting sequence, as a default the person can change:

1. **Delivery.** Right after purchase: the pamphlet, how to read it, and one thing to do first. Nothing to sell.
2. **The part people miss.** A few days later: the idea in the pamphlet readers most often skip or get wrong, from the person's own experience with clients. If they don't have one yet, this email waits until they do.
3. **A story.** A real client who used the method, from `canon/bank/stories.md` or `proof.md`, with permission noted. One story, told plainly.
4. **A question.** Ask what the reader is stuck on. Replies are research and often the start of a conversation.
5. **The next step.** What working with the person looks like, who it's for, how to reach them, with the call to action word for word from the vehicle's `C` entries. Said once, plainly, with an easy way to say no.

Each email is its own piece through `skills/craft-sales-copy` (copy kind `nurture email`): its own folder, three drafts, the judge in a fresh context. `pieces/<...>/emails.md` lists the sequence in order, with the day each one goes out and a link to its piece. Every email is worth reading for someone who never buys anything else (the worth-it test in `/turning-prospects-into-collaborators`).

The days between emails and the number of emails are the person's choice. A sequence that runs longer than the person has real things to say turns into pressure; stop when the material runs out.

## 4. The path to the higher-priced work

The pamphlet teaches the idea; the higher-priced work helps people put it into practice. The path between them has to be honest about both.

- **Name the next step.** One offer from `canon/vehicle/vehicle.md` that fits a pamphlet reader best. A `draft` offer is used only once the person confirms it in the session. When there's no fitting offer yet, `/offer-designer` shapes one, and the funnel says nothing about it until it exists.
- **Say who it's for and who it isn't.** The pamphlet's self-selection (two or three kinds of reader) carries into the offer: say which of them it helps most.
- **Make the first conversation easy.** A reply to an email, a short call, a form. When it's a call, `/diagnosis-call` preps it.
- **Never make the method look harder than it is** to sell help with it (`SKILL.md`, principle 2).

Write the path as a short section of `funnel.md`: the pamphlet, the offers after purchase, the email that names the next step, the offer it leads to and how a reader reaches it.

## 5. What to read after the first sales

Once real buyers come through, read with the person, from their own numbers:

- Did people who knew them buy? If not, the pamphlet's promise or its price is the first thing to talk through, before more traffic.
- Which emails got replies, and what did the replies say?
- Did anyone take the next step? If they didn't, ask a few buyers why, in their words.

What they learn goes back into the files: buyer words to `/understand`, a gap in an offer to `/offer-designer`, a price question to `/pricing-tests`. Nothing here changes the canon directly; new findings go in as `draft` entries and changes to confirmed ones as proposals (`skills/setup-brand-tree/SKILL.md`, "Where a picked-up belief goes").
