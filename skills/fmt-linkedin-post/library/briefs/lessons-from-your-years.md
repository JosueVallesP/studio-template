# What your years taught you

Part of [[idea-briefs|Idea briefs]]. Start here when: you have years of practice. The reader leaves with: something to use. It also gives: proof you can do the work.

## The idea

You've done one thing for a long time or at a large scale. List what it taught you.

## The post in one sentence

After [how long, or how much of what], here are the [number] things I'd tell [who].

This says what you hand the reader and why it comes from you. If you can't fill it in yet, gather more material before you write.

## Why it works

The number at the top (years, clients, dollars) tells the reader the list was paid for. Each lesson works when it corrects something the reader does today.

## What you need

- A real stretch of practice you can put a number on.
- Lessons that came from that practice.
- One claim the lessons add up to.

## Questions to answer

- What have you done long enough to see the same problem a hundred times?
- What do people starting out get wrong that you no longer do?
- If the list had to make one point, what is it?
- What number shows how much of this you've done?
- Which lesson would your reader push back on?

## How it fails

- The lessons are common sense.
- The number at the top has nothing to do with the lessons.
- The list has no single claim under it.

## Variations

The lessons can come from one person who taught you (L070), from money you spent (L061), or from a set of beliefs you hold (S036).
