## Post

When Alan Mulally became Ford's CEO in 2006, he made one simple rule: everyone had to attend his weekly meetings. 

The company's executives had built careers avoiding accountability.

But Mulally believed this culture was killing Ford.

So he created mandatory weekly Business Plan Reviews where every leader had to personally report their progress.

When a prominent executive skipped the first meeting, Mulally confronted him immediately.

The executive said he was too busy to attend every week.

Mulally smiled and said: "That's okay. We can still be friends. But you can't work here."

Here's what happened:

1. Ford rebounded from a $12.7 billion loss in 2006 to a $6.3 billion profit in 2014
2. Stock price rose an astonishing 1,640% from its crisis low
3. Ford was the only Detroit automaker that didn't need a government bailout

Mulally's weekly meetings created what he called "One Team" culture.

He forced executives to stop playing politics and start solving problems together.

So he built accountability through radical transparency and relentless implementation.

Most CEOs avoid confrontation with their senior leaders.

Mulally made it the cornerstone of his turnaround.
