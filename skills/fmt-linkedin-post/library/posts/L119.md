## Post

Bad CEO: "Why isn't the team delivering?" 
Good CEO: "Have I been clear about what I expect?"

Bad CEO: "I need better people." 
Good CEO: "I need to manage differently."

Bad CEO: "They should know this." 
Good CEO: "I haven't taught them yet."

Bad CEO: "I hired a VP so I wouldn't have to deal with this."
Good CEO: "I hired a VP so we could deal with this together."

Bad CEO: "My door is always open." 
Good CEO: "I go to them. I don't wait for them to come to me."

Bad CEO: "I don't have time for 1:1s." 
Good CEO: "1:1s are the highest-leverage 30 minutes in my week."

Bad CEO: "The culture will sort itself out." 
Good CEO: "Culture is what I permit and what I promote. Every day."

Bad CEO: "I gave feedback once and nothing changed." 
Good CEO: "I gave feedback once. Then I followed up. Then I followed up again. Then I realised feedback is a process, not an event."

Bad CEO: "My team doesn't take ownership." 
Good CEO: "My team doesn't know what ownership looks like because I've never defined it."

Bad CEO: "I'm a visionary, not a manager." 
Good CEO: "I'm a CEO. My job is both...and the management part is where most of us fail."

TAKEAWAY

The gap between a bad CEO and a good CEO isn't talent, intelligence, or strategy.

It's self-awareness... and the willingness to look at what's not working and say: "That might be me."
