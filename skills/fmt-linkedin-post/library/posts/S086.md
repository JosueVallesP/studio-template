## Post

If your business model only needs 19 clients to hit your annual income goal, what the hell are you doing chasing a million strangers?

I just think we've gone a little mad.

The internet made us believe that because we can reach a million people, we must reach a million people to be successful.

For some people, that’s the right model. If you are selling a cheap ebook, launching a mass-market software product, or selling a commodity like a bar of soap, you definitely need a massive audience.

To make a comfortable living selling your expertise, though, you don’t need huge numbers. You don’t need 100,000 followers. You don’t need hundreds of clients. Certainly, you don’t need a complex 12-step funnel selling $27 courses to thousands of strangers. 

To make a living as a coach, consultant, designer, real estate agent, fractional executive, copywriter, architect, content producer, or brand strategist, you only need a handful of people who trust you, who think your work is genius, and who would take your call any other day.

A client of mine makes $1.4M a year selling leadership development. She got there with about 23 clients and fewer than 2,000 followers on LinkedIn. Her website sucks, she doesn’t advertise, she doesn’t have a funnel, and she really sucks at content creation. By modern internet standards, she’s doing everything wrong, except she’s doing everything right. She’s just playing an entirely different game.

And to be clear, I’m not saying audiences are useless. I have 150,000 followers across LinkedIn and Substack. There is absolutely a place for building an audience…it’s incredible for creating awareness, testing concepts, and spreading ideas at scale. 

But it’s just not the foundation of an expertise-based business. 

It’s just the cherry on top.
