## Post

We're living through an era of unprecedented fear in the business world.

And you know, a lot of it is with good reason.

We have geopolitical instability.
We have rapidly changing technology.
We have a huge demographic transition.

Although they wouldn't admit it, most big companies would be delighted if you told them that in 3 years time they'd simply maintained what they have now.

After all, many of them won't.

But in this atmosphere, the way decisions are made begins to change. Companies move from an offensive stance - of value creation, innovation, and investment - to a defensive stance - of cost cutting, optimisation, and trend chasing.

The main way we are seeing this is the response to AI.

The technology is not being used, as Musk suggested back in the day, to dramatically increase productivity of existing resources (offensive mindset). Instead it's being used to try and maintain productivity with less resources (defensive mindset).

I spoke to one pal working for a huge corporate, and he said their internal mandate was to cut operating costs but FIFTY PERCENT - essentially by shifting American jobs to India and plugging the gaps with AI.

Perhaps this will prove sensible.

But it will also create massive gaps in the market - gaps created by the erosion of quality, and failure to exploit opportunities, that's inevitable with a defensive stance.

Just like how you should buy in a bear market, you should also be offensive in a defensive market.
