## Post

Cancelling a meeting with someone on your team is one of the most disrespectful things a leader can do.

I know your calendar is full. I know things come up. 

But when you cancel a meeting with someone on your team, they don't see what's happening behind the scenes. They just see that you cancelled. And the message they take from that, whether you mean it that way or not, is that they're not important enough to keep on your calendar.

Think about what that meeting actually was for them. 

They prepared for it. They were planning to raise something they've been worrying about for weeks. Maybe they were going to ask for feedback, or help with a problem, or a promotion. 

Now they have to wait. 

And when "next week" turns into the week after, what they learn is that there's no point in preparing because you're going to cancel again anyway.

So my rule is simple:

Put the meeting in your diary and don't cancel it. Keep it light if you want to. You don't need an agenda or a deck. Just show up.

If you genuinely have to move it, move it inside the same week. Not "I'll come back to you with a new time." 

And the person you cancelled on should be the first person you reschedule, not the last.

Your team isn't going to read your strategy memo to figure out if they matter to you. 

They're going to look at your calendar.
