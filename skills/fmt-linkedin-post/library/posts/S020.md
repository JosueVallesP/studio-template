## Post

There’s something I say often during meetings with the team: “Don’t ruin a good story with the facts.” I usually say it when we’re brainstorming a new idea and someone starts becoming overly analytical too early in the process—identifying all the reasons an idea can’t work before they’ve given it enough time to breathe.
 
Because some of the best ideas don’t feel possible in the moment. But if you indulge them for long enough, you might just figure out a way to bring them to life.
 
At the beginning of this year, we had a meeting to brainstorm speakers for our annual event. Maren, on my team, went first: “I think we should have Waldorf and Statler from The Muppets open it up!”
 
It was such a fun idea—I LOVE The Muppets, and have always felt a connection to those two given that they are named after NYC hotels. But logistically, no chance we could make it happen. I jumped in right away: That’s not possible, let’s keep thinking.” 
 
But Maren looked at me with a smile and said: “Don’t ruin a good story with the facts.”
 
It was one of the first times someone on my team had used that line against me. And she was right. Because we gave the idea time to breathe. And through a serendipitous series of events, it happened—resulting in quite possibly the coolest opening I could have ever dreamed of.
 
So this is just a reminder: Don’t be the person in the room who figures out why an idea can’t happen before you’ve given it enough time to become possible. Some ideas need a little room before reality catches up with them. And if you shut them down too quickly, you might miss out on the once-in-a-lifetime opportunity of being heckled by a couple of icons on a balcony at your own event.
