## Post

Sam Altman once said, "80% of your work as a startup founder is building a product so good people tell their friends about it. If you've done that, you've done 80% of the job."

If you haven't, nothing else matters.

Most founders hear this and nod. Then they raise money and immediately start running performance marketing.

At Gamma, we had an explicit internal rule: The lion's share of our growth has to come from word of mouth. We won’t fool ourselves into thinking we can pay our way into infinite growth.

We meant it. And we forced it into reality with over 70,000,000 users.

Here's how to think about the relationship between product and marketing:

1. Let organic lead, paid follow

From $0 to $10M ARR, nearly 100% of our growth came from word of mouth and organic content. From $10M to $50M, still more than half. We treated paid acquisition as amplification, never the engine. The diagnostic is simple: is the product doing the propagation, or are you?

2. Build for inherent virality

At Gamma, the output of the product is something people naturally share: decks, docs, presentations. Using the product is inherently social - if you like it, you're using it to share with other people. Treat that loop like your growth engine, not a lucky accident.

3. Prioritize the loop over the funnel

We track active creators (people who create and share content) and the number of new users who heard about us through a friend. Over 50% of our growth still comes from word of mouth. If that number drops, we know something's wrong with the product - not the marketing.

4. Stay lean until the loop is real

Leanness forces honesty. It forces the product to do the work. We grew headcount painfully slowly. Our team was 12 people when we first hit PMF. If your growth model requires headcount and spend to function, you do not have a growth model.

When ads or marketing are the only spark, every win is temporary. Campaigns decay. CPMs rise. Audiences get numb. Competitors copy.

When the product is the initial spark, marketing becomes a multiplier.

And multipliers are the only thing that scale cleanly.
