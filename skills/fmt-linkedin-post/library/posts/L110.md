## Post

Last week I hosted a dinner with 12 strangers.

We played games with magnet tiles and gold coins.

And we accidentally proved why your manager's feedback isn't working.

How it worked:

We split people into pairs for a tower-building game. One group could only say "yes" to guide their partner. The other could only say "no."

The yes-only group finished faster. (Hat tip Sheel Mohnot, Tali Leff, Sejal Lohiya, and Nandana Yadla).

The no-only group was slower. And also visibly annoyed with each other!

Why? "No" tells you what's wrong but not what's right. The group was left guessing, getting told no, guessing again.

It's why you don't train a dog by yelling "don't pee in the kitchen." The dog doesn't learn where to pee. It just moves to peeing in the living room.

What’s the lesson for managers? The main intuition for coaching or managing is to focus on areas of improvement - what someone did wrong. This theory of dog training and magnet tile building says it could be faster to reward / acknowledge people when they are doing something right vs. coach their flaws. 

Examples:

- Someone who normally is too verbose with written comms sends a concise memo outlining tradeoffs for a big decision. Be over the top to praise this memo and style 
- Someone who is known to be negative on AI finally attempts to do a new workflow. Encourage the behavior of trying vs nagging them when they don’t 

That was just lesson one. In another experiment, we built the perfect incentive system and it completely failed.
