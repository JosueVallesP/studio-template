## Post

I recently had the writer David Sedaris on my podcast, How To Fail. When he walked into the recording studio, he was utterly aghast that the whole thing would be filmed.
For several minutes, he expanded with his customary wit on the egregiousness of all podcasts now being visualised. It wasn’t quite a rant, but he did end up staring down the barrel of the camera and stating boldly: ‘If you’re watching this podcast, you’re a loser.'

I wouldn't go quite that far. I'm grateful for every person who listens and watches. But I understood what he meant. 

Because I miss audio-only podcasting. There was a confessional intimacy to it. Neither the guest nor I were thinking about what we were wearing, whether our hair had gone frizzy, whether the studio lights were melting off makeup over the course of the 2-hour conversation. These days, I have to think about all of it. What colour drains on camera. Whether it's going to rain on the way to the tube and turn me into a bedraggled mole-rat by the time I arrive. What angle is best for me to sit at so that we leave enough space above my head for social media clip furniture. And so on. 
When I started How To Fail in 2018 my face did not feature on any of the branding, now, my face and body is part of the package. It happened without my ever really questioning whether I had a right to say no because, the fact is, the podcast had to become fully visualised in order to keep up with everyone else in the market.

But this pressure isn't landing on everyone equally. Women who don't ‘make the effort’ routinely get told how they've failed to meet arbitrary beauty standards in the comments.
It's tiring. Being faced with one's own face this often is not a natural state for a person to live in.

On good days, I don’t mind it. On bad days, I can start feeling down about how I look before I give myself a swift talking to and remind myself what a privilege it is to be in this situation. I remind myself, too, that it actually doesn’t matter how I look. It matters what kind of interview I can deliver. It matters if it reaches the people who need it; if it makes them feel less alone.

Still, I can’t help but feel, a bit like Sedaris, that it would occasionally be nice to switch off the cameras and concentrate on what I’m hearing and saying rather than on what I might be looking like.
