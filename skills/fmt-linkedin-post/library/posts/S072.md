## Post

I learned a difficult lesson after eight years at a national newspaper. I'd said yes to almost everything my bosses asked of me.

I'd been the most amenable and uncomplaining employee I could be. I'd worked long, unpredictable hours and taken on the assignments nobody else wanted.

When I eventually asked to go on contract so I could have more freedom to pursue my own writing, the request was refused.
I was surprised. And then I was  furious.
But it taught me something: most big employers are never going to feel sentimental about you.

You might believe you have established loyal and reciprocal personal connections with your bosses and maybe you have, but the chances are that when push comes to shove, they are never going to place your interests on an equal footing to those of the organisation you work for. Of course they’re not. They wouldn’t be doing their job if they did.

Once I understood that, I started asking myself a different question - what do I want? That question changed the trajectory of my career. Loyalty is valuable. But so is remembering that your career belongs to you.
