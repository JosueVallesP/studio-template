## Post

I've onboarded 18 ghostwriting clients in the past 1.5 years. Before I write a single piece of content, I run a 7-step research process to make the engagement as valuable, seamless, and efficient as possible:
 
1. Inhale the onboarding doc

Every new client at Compound Content Studio fills out an onboarding form that gathers context on their target audience, content goals, messaging, positioning, tone, voice, etc. This is the baseline for our engagement, so I try to internalize it all. 

2. Run AI-assisted deep research

Next, I move into an AI-assisted deep research phase. I like ChatGPT for this (it's the most thorough in my experience), though Perplexity is another strong option. 

I use a custom prompt tailored to our context at Compound, the aspects I care about most with my accounts, as well as any particularities that I uncovered in the onboarding doc. 

I'll typically read the outputted brief once, and then plug into Notebook LM and generate a “podcast” so I can consume the info multiple times in multiple ways.

3. Scour the internet for existing content

From there, I like to dig into any existing content the client has shared. This may include:

→ Short-form (LinkedIn, X, etc.)
→ Long-form (newsletters, blog posts, Medium articles, research papers, journal articles, or guest contributions)
→ Interviews (podcast, television, or radio)
→ Any other bits or bobs I can turn up

The more you can uncover here, the better.

4. Conduct a high-level industry analysis

Next, I try to grasp the industry, space, and/or vertical. For an unfamiliar area, I’ll do a bit more up-front work in trying to understand the terminology, major players, historical context, etc. 

5. Zoom in on the client’s market

Once I have a general grasp of the industry, I like to focus on the client's market and especially any direct competitors. At this point, I can start to see how their company and product slot into the market.

6. Touch (almost) every page of the website 

The client's company site is an incredible resource. I’ll usually peel through the site page by page, prioritizing use cases, product features, and case studies. The blog is also often a good source of content inspiration. 

7. Comp Shop

Lastly, I like to “comp shop” (aka competitor shop): deep dive into the content of the 3-5 most comparable voices in our client’s target audience. Often, these will be founders, CEOs, or executives at their biggest competitors. 

This list serves as a great resource on what resonates, and they’re also people against whom you can benchmark growth and performance.

This may sound like a lot, but I’ve found incredible ROI on time invested in the research phase. Nailing the first 90 days of the engagement is critical to client success, and stepping in with all this context puts you in a great position to do just that.
