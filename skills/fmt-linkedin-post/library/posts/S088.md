## Post

In 1997, Harry Beckwith wrote Selling the Invisible. 29 years later, it’s still one of the best books I’ve read for anyone selling their expertise. Here’s 6 ideas that make selling a service a lot less confusing:

1. If you're selling a service, you're selling a relationship

Since you’re selling something intangible, people can’t touch, smell, or try it out beforehand. The way you INTERACT with the client and the relationship you build becomes the actual product. It’s not secondary - it’s literally the main thing.

2. Your #1 job is to remove fear

Paying for something you can't see is scary, so your core job goes from trying to sound like a genius to just making the client feel safe. Instead of saying, "I can fix this," tell them exactly how it's going to go: "First we do this, then we do that, and if things get stuck, here's how we handle it." Clarity is the name of the game.

3. A higher price makes the right people feel safer

Pricing is a proxy for quality. That’s just how it is. When it comes to services, price is one of the first things people use to guess how good you are, simply because they have nothing else concrete to evaluate. This is especially true with bigger clients. Cheap prices repel the exact people you actually want to work with.

4. Clients judge the small things before they judge the big things

Most clients don't actually know if your strategy is brilliant. But they definitely know if you took three days to reply to an email, or if your invoice was confusing. They use the small things they can see to judge the expertise they can't.

5. Your biggest competitor is inaction, not other companies

You almost never lose a deal to another firm. You lose because hiring someone is a headache, and doing nothing is easy. Don’t try to prove why you're better than the competition. Just point out exactly what it costs them to keep living with the problem.

6. Satisfaction is just the gap between what you promise and what you deliver

If you promise the world just to close the deal, even great work will feel like a letdown later. Keep it simple: set a baseline expectation you know you can beat, and then just do a little bit more than you promised.

---

I first came across this book years ago, and it completely changed how I think about client work. 

It’s very short and you can go through it in an evening. 

Highly recommended for anyone who sells expertise for a living.
