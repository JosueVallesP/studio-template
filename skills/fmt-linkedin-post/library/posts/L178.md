## Post

What is happening to Creative Strategy?!

When I first started working as a creative strategist, the role felt like it was built for people with a specific way of thinking.
People who could read culture.

Understand timing.
Synthesize what was happening in society, on the internet, and in real life, then turn that into ideas and content that landed at the right moment and reinforced a brand’s narrative.

Creative strategy required context.
Trend mapping.
Taste.
A deep understanding of the digital landscape and human behavior.
Lately, when I read job descriptions for creative strategist roles, the role feels almost unrecognizable.

Heavy focus on CTRs, LTVs, CPAs, ROAS, and a thousand other paid media metrics that leave little room for actual creative thinking.
I understand why brands want this.
Results matter. Measurement matters. Accountability matters.
But I do worry that something is being lost.

When creative strategy becomes overly technical, we start optimizing for sameness. Brands chase what already worked instead of defining what should exist next. Context, philosophy, timing, and cultural fluency get swapped for dashboards and benchmarks.

And the result is obvious.
So much content looks the same.
Feels the same.
Moves the same.

The best creative strategy has always lived at the intersection of art and science. Not one replacing the other.
Just a thought that’s been sitting with me as I look at where the industry is headed.

Curious how others are feeling about this.
