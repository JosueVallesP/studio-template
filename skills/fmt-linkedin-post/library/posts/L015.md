## Post

Over the weekend, I ran 200 miles around Lake Tahoe, and I didn't learn a damn thing about marketing.

Running ultramarathons like the Tahoe 200 are objectively stupid. It took me 68 hours to run/hike/bushwhack from the starting line near South Lake Tahoe all the way around the lake to end up right where I had started. And I did it all while only sleeping 2 hours, another objectively stupid part of it.

Did I learn about best practices for marketing ? No. Stop trying to suggest that. But I did learn to embrace dumb in a deeply visceral way. There's beauty there.

That's why pursuits like this are so important for us—as individuals and as a species. They take us into the weird, dark places that no one bothers to go. Last year roughly 0.0007% of the world's population completed a race of 100 miles or more. How lucky am I then?

Moreover, as AI continues to ingest and regurgitate back to us, our innate human ability to be delightfully irrational will become even more important than ever. I'm actually hopefully that we'll find simultaneously new and ancient ways to more human.

At Liquid Death we often ask ourselves, "What's the dumbest thing we can do?" We realized long ago that if we're only ever trying to be smart, we'll stick to the well-trodden places everyone else has already gone. But if we choose to dive head-first into the places sensible people know that no one should go, that's where you'll return with things no one has ever seen. (Like specifically, if you try to run 200 miles on almost zero sleep, you'll probably trip balls for the last 9 hours and have every rock in the trail turn into a face staring back at you.)

So go out and learn how to be dumb.

Being smart will only get you so far. Being dumb will get you at least 200 miles further.
