## Post

Marvin Bower led McKinsey from 1950 to 1967, and he had strong feelings about words.

At his McKinsey, the firm had clients, not customers. It had a practice, not a business. Its people were firm members, not employees. And it never negotiated with clients. It "made arrangements."

His explanation: "The terms we use reflect the way we think."

That sounds fussy at first. But think about what each word implies. A customer buys a product. A client relies on your judgment.

The language was a reminder to everyone inside the firm of the standard they were supposed to hold.

He backed it up, too. In 1963, at 60, Bower sold his shares back to the firm at book value, far less than they were worth, so the next generation of partners could afford to own it.

The words you use every day end up shaping how you behave. 

It's worth choosing them on purpose.
