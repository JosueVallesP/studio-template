## Post

There is no shortage of business advice.

Thousands of books.

Millions of posts.

An endless stream of people explaining the "right" way to build a company.

The challenge isn't finding advice.
It's finding advice that's actually useful.

That's why I'm sharing some of the most valuable ideas I've found.
(From books that have shaped how I think about building a business.)

Starting with Good Strategy Bad Strategy by Richard Rumelt.

Three ideas stood out:

1) Strategy is a response to a specific problem.

If you can't clearly define the challenge, you don't have a strategy.

You have assumptions.

2) Diagnosis comes before action.

Most organisations rush to solutions.

The best leaders spend more time understanding what's really happening.

A good diagnosis often makes the right action obvious.

3) Goals are not strategy.

"Grow revenue."
"Increase market share."
"Improve customer experience."

Those are objectives.

Strategy is deciding what matters most and what you're willing to ignore.

Simple ideas.

But far less common in practice than they should be.
