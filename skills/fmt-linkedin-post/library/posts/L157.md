## Post

After 25 years of building and scaling revenue teams across >$500M in ARR, the #1 reason I see sellers, managers, and CROs fail isn’t effort, strategy, or hustle.  It’s a lack of basic financial acumen (and I know Gal Aga agrees with me).

If I were going to design a course to fix that problem for revenue leaders, here’s exactly how I’d structure it. 👇🏻

Module 1: Understanding the P&L and the core financial statements

This module would cover financial literacy, not accounting theory.  The goal wouldn't be to go deep on the different kinds of depreciation or why R&D should be capitalized.  

It would be to quickly help revenue leaders get up to speed.  

Specific elements should include:

- How the P&L actually works: revenue, gross margin, operating expenses, EBITDA
- How the P&L connects to the balance sheet and cash flow statement
- Where revenue decisions quietly create downstream cost, margin, and cash consequences
- What “healthy” vs “fragile” P&Ls look like at different stages of growth

Key Takeaways:

Leaders would be able to scan a P&L in 60 seconds and understand:
- How the business is really performing
- Where the obvious gaps and inefficiencies are
- Whether growth is creating leverage or just hiding problems

Module 2: Unit economics and the true cost of growth

This is where most revenue leaders get dangerous with partial knowledge.

We would cover:
- GRR, NRR, churn, expansion, and why revenue quality matters more than topline growth
- LTV:CAC and why averages lie
- Cohort and channel-based unit economics
- How sales efficiency, payback periods, and growth rates interact

Key Takeaways:

You'd know whether growth is creating value or destroying it — and which segments, channels, or motions are responsible.

Module 3: Valuation, benchmarking, and building durable value

Benchmarks without context are how bad decisions get justified.  Too many people have no clue why revenue multiples exist, what they imply, and why they change with the market.

Benchmarks are the crutch of the half-informed.  This module would change that.

We'd focus on:

- How investors actually think about valuation
- Why different business models earn different multiples
- The real drivers of revenue and EBITDA multiples
- A valuation-first mindset for revenue strategy and investment decisions

Key Takeaways:

You’ll stop optimizing for vanity metrics and start making decisions that compound long-term enterprise value.

Revenue leadership without financial fluency is guesswork with confidence.

This course would be designed to change that.

Given its importance, I'm hoping someone is building this course and target it at CROs, CMOs, and revenue leadership.  

And given its urgency, I would hope that someone would launch this course on January 27th, ideally to the best and most enduring revenue community in the world.

Because this course would be an absolute banger.

Fingers crossed.
