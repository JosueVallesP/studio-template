## Post

The best book I've read on strategy is 143 pages long and could probably be explained over a beer.

It's called No Bullshit Strategy by Alex M H Smith I finally got around to reading it after it had been sitting on my bookshelf for too long. 

It's no-nonsense, first principles stuff. How Dave Trott would write about strategy. 

My favourite 10 ideas to give you a flavour. But go and read it for yourself. It's the book I wish i'd written.

1. Strategy is the unique value your business provides to the market. Not a slide deck full of frameworks nobody acts on.

2. Value is the benefit customers receive, not the product you make. Products are just mechanisms. If you confuse the two, you'll struggle to differentiate or evolve.

3. Aim to be the only, not the best. "Best" is subjective and forgettable. "Only" opens white space.

4. The best strategy is usually already inside your business. You just haven't noticed it yet (I am a HUGE believer in this).

5. The market tells you what your business is. Pay attention to the signals rather than forcing an identity the market doesn't recognise.

6. Strategy must provoke action. If people can't act on it, it's worthless regardless of how clever it sounds.

7. Write it like you'd explain it in a pub. If you can't say it simply, it probably isn't clear enough. Ditch the deck.

8. Make your strategy externally obvious. If customers can't see it without you explaining it, it doesn't exist yet.

9. Execute with a Minimum Viable Strategy. Start with the smallest moves that make the strategy visible, then build from there.

10. Commitment makes any position charismatic. There's no inherently sexy market position. Bold, unapologetic execution is what makes brands compelling.
