## Post

The night of January 15, 2009, I was lying in a hospital bed in New Jersey. I had just survived a plane crash into the Hudson River. My blood pressure was through the roof and my body was still in shock.

And I had not been able to reach my family.

Between the media, the medical team, and everything else happening around me, I had no way to call my wife, who was hundreds of miles away in Charlotte with our four kids, watching the news and not knowing if I was alive.

Then a woman named Doreen showed up.

US Airways (now American Airlines) had flown her in from Pittsburgh in the middle of the night. She had one job. Take care of me. Not the paperwork. Not the liability. Me.

The first thing she did was hand me a phone so I could call my wife. Around midnight, I heard my family's voices for the first time since the crash. I cannot fully describe what that meant. Doreen made it happen within minutes of walking in the door, because she understood that in that moment, nothing mattered more.

She stayed with me. She managed the chaos. She thought about what I needed before I knew to ask for it.

I have flown that airline almost exclusively ever since. Not because of the planes. Not because of the price. Because of how they showed up on the worst night of my life.

That is the difference between a vendor and a partner.

A vendor delivers what was agreed to. A partner pays attention to what was not.
Every professional I work with wants to know how to stop being a commodity. 

How to become the person a client will never leave. The answer is almost never a better product or a lower price. It is whether the people you serve believe you actually have their best interests at heart.

You cannot say that into existence. You prove it in the moments no one is measuring. The fast response. The follow-through nobody asked for. The phone handed over at midnight because you understood what the person in front of you needed most.

Take care of the people you serve when it is inconvenient and unmeasured.
That is what they remember for the rest of their lives.
