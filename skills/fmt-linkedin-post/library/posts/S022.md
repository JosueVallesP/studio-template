## Post

I recently blew past 3 million subscribers on YouTube. My biggest realization along the way: YouTube is not a good business.

I went really hard on YouTube at its peak—the MrBeast and Casey Neistat era three or four years ago. It felt like it was going to be the next television, with creators as the new celebrities, but without having to get slimed in front of a bunch of screaming 8th graders. 

Then I dove deep into it and the realities set in.

Monetization on YouTube, or any social platform for that matter, is rough. The platform captures so much value that it's incredibly difficult for creators to scale economically without monetizing off-platform.

And the power users of YouTube are, by and large, teenagers and 20-year-olds. They're engaged and dedicated, but you can't sell much to people paying for YouTube Premium with their mom's Visa. 

There's a strange prestige that comes from getting millions of YouTube views. It's shocking to people outside of YouTube, and even to people inside the industry. 

But then you look at the revenue numbers and you’re like:

"Oh.”

You can see this shaking out in real time based on where YouTube is drifting. It's becoming a podcast platform, simply because the economics around podcasts are so much better. That's where the incentives are, so that's where the creators are driving things.

I'm still bullish on YouTube long-term. I still love the platform and I still love making videos. I just have a much more sober relationship with it these days.
