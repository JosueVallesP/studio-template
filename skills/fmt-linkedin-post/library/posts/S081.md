## Post

A common piece of advice in personal branding is to pick a topic and own it. Become "the hooks guy" or "the pricing lady." And it works, kind of...

I just think one of the worst things a personal brand can do is lock you in.

That usually happens when you build your brand around a topic instead of a person. 3 years later you've outgrown it, but that's still what everyone expects from you. You want to talk about something else, and it feels like starting over.

I'd rather think about a personal brand in 3 layers that change at different speeds:

1. What you believe. This is your way of seeing the world, the things you'd probably still argue for in 20 years (though you never know). It's the slowest layer and should barely move.

2. Who you help. This is the kind of person you love working with and the situation they're usually in. It changes slowly, over years.

3. What you talk about. These are your topics, formats and offers. This is the fast layer, and it should change as you learn and grow.

The brands that last stand on the first two layers. 

The topics can change on top of that, and the brand still holds together.

I've had to do this myself.

For a while, a lot of what I wrote was about growing on LinkedIn. Now I spend most of my time on bodies of work, relationships and building personal brands with real depth. That could have felt like starting over. But underneath, the belief is the exact same: the people with real depth should be the ones everyone knows, and reputation comes from relationships and real work more than reach. The people I want to help stayed the same too. Only the top layer moved.

That's a big part of why I spend so much time on introspection before we touch anything public. If I only looked at what someone does today, I'd build them a box. When I look at who they are and where they're going, they have room to grow.

If you want to check whether your brand is locking you in, look at your headline and your About section. Count how much of it is about the top layer, meaning topics, services and tactics, and how much is about the first two.

If it's almost all topic, try rewriting it so it leads with what you believe and who you help. 

Keep the topic, but move it lower.

Then, the next time you want to write about something new, connect it back to the bottom two layers.
