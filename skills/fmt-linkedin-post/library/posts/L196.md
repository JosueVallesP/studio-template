## Post

I asked 200+ HR leaders about the ONE book that transformed how they hire. Here’s 9 books that came up again and again (and why they'll change how you think about talent):

1. "Who: The A Method for Hiring" by Geoff Smart and Randy Street

Forget "tell me about yourself." This book introduces scorecard hiring - defining outcomes before you interview. Multiple CHROs told me it was their biggest game-changer.

2. "Radical Candor" by Kim Scott

The book that fixes why good people leave. It's not about being nice OR harsh - it's about caring personally while challenging directly. Essential for anyone who manages people.

3. "Quiet" by Susan Cain

Your hiring process is probably designed for extroverts. This book shows why you're missing incredible talent and how to fix it. Changed how many leaders structure interviews entirely.

4. "The 5 Dysfunctions of a Team" by Patrick Lencioni

Stop hiring brilliant jerks. This shows which behaviors actually predict team success. HR leaders use it to design behavioral interview questions that matter.

5. "Reimagining Inclusion" by Mita Mallick

Debunks 13 myths about workplace inclusion with real examples. Not theory - actual playbooks from someone who's done it. The most recommended book for 2025.

6. "Delivering Happiness" by Tony Hsieh

Zappos built a billion-dollar company by hiring for values first. This book shows how to make culture measurable in your hiring process.

7. "Leaders, Decisions and Biases" by Harjeet Khanduja

Your "gut feel" about candidates is probably wrong. This book reveals the science of better hiring decisions through structured approaches.

8. "Designing Your Life" by Dave Evans

Not a traditional HR book, but HR leaders love it. They use it to help candidates figure out what they actually want = better fit and longer tenure.

9. "The New HR Leader's First 100 Days" by Alan Collins

Every new HR leader mentioned this. But they're also giving it to hiring managers because great hiring happens when everyone thinks like a talent leader.

What struck me most:

ZERO traditional HR textbooks made the list.

Every book challenges conventional hiring:

• Resumes don't predict performance
• Culture fit often means "hire people like me"
• Most interviews test interview skills, not job skills
• The best people aren't actively looking

The HR leaders getting results aren't following the old playbook.

They're reading psychology, entrepreneurship, and behavioral science.

They're questioning what they learned in certifications.

They're building hiring processes that actually work.

TAKEAWAY

Great HR isn't about better systems or AI screening.

It's about understanding humans deeply enough to spot potential others miss.
