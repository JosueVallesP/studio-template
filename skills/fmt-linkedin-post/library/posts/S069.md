## Post

During one of the most difficult times of my life, a friend gave me a phrase I've never stopped using: holding the shopping.

It is the idea that, when someone you love is going through something hard, you don't have to do their emotional processing for them. You just have to signal that you're there. That you're holding a bag full of emotional resources (and maybe their favourite biscuits) ready for whenever they need them.

I didn't understand how far that stretched until a close friend's father died. I was one of the first people she called. She said: please come over. So I did. I went to sit with her. I didn't fix anything. I remember worrying afterwards that just being there wasn't enough, that I wasn't the kind of capable person who turns up with a homemade lasagne (although I did turn up with some chocolate fingers).

My therapist later said to me: maybe she called you because she didn’t need the lasagne. Maybe she needed something else. And that was what you could offer.

You don't have to cure anything. You don't have to solve anything. Grief can’t be fixed. Sometimes the most helpful job is just being present, sitting with a friend in need and metaphorically holding the shopping.
