## Post

We run the same first post for every new client at Compound Content Studio, and we’ve nearly turned it into a science. Here’s exactly how we create S-tier origin story posts. 

Every great origin story post (and all great content, really) starts with great source material. We typically gather this from 3 places: 

1. The onboarding form 
2. The kickoff call 
3. External research 

By combining these sources, we're able to amass the details needed to anchor the narrative and the personal anecdotes needed to provide depth and color to the story. 

Once we have gathered the source material, the real work begins. 

At their core, the best origin story posts are "career-cradle-to-current" narratives: they follow from the inciting action of one's career arc (career cradle) and trace the journey from there to the present day (current). 

Now, as no two career paths are the same, the specifics of that inciting incident will vary. For some people, it may be a moment in their childhood when they realized or discovered what they wanted to do with their lives. For others, it might be a high school teacher, a college professor, or an early job that set them on their course. Regardless, this moment grounds us in a “before” and sets the stage for the rest of the story. 

From there, we’ll plot out the essential “points of interest.” These are the moments of significance, for better or worse, that are fundamental to understanding how our client got from A to B. These typically provide the narrative tension that compels a reader to keep reading. 

The final step is to succinctly capture the present. We need to land the plane in the current reality of their business, and do so in a way that’s engaging to both a general and ICP audience - both of whom we aim to appeal to with this type of post. 

In short, the essential elements are:
 
→ A compelling, story-driven hook that establishes the “who,” “what,” and (occasionally) “why.” 
→ An opening that sets the stage and pushes us into the meat of the narrative 
→ A succinct-but-vivid body with strong pacing and chronological structure 
→ An exciting climax that pushes the story into the present 
→ A closing that explains what they’re working on today 
→ Optional: A CTA that encourages readers to “follow for more” 

If you’ve made it this far, it likely comes as no surprise that origin story posts are typically the longest posts we run for clients. These posts regularly push LI’s 3k character limit. 

But they’re also one of our highest-performing post types. So the juice is well worth the squeeze.
