## Post

One of my favorite underdog stories, and almost no one in tech knows her name:

At 12, Thai Lee's father sent her to America alone. She spoke little English. She had no friends. Today she's a self-made billionaire running a $14 billion company that serves Boeing, AT&T, and Johnson & Johnson.

Born in Bangkok to Korean parents. Spent early childhood in South Korea. Her father, an economist, believed in education so deeply he sent her to live with a host family in Massachusetts at 12.

Remember, Thai was a child: Twelve years old. Family thousands of miles away. The only Asian student in her class. One language she barely spoke. Most adults couldn't handle that.

She chose biology and economics as majors specifically to minimize class presentations - she was self-conscious about her accent. After college, she worked at Procter & Gamble and American Express. Not to climb the ladder. To learn how businesses actually operate.

1989: Bought a failing software reseller in New Jersey for under $1 million. Five employees. A handful of customers. Her thesis was very contrarian: in enterprise tech, service and relationships could matter more than hype.

1990s: While dot-com startups burned VC cash chasing eyeballs, she reinvested every dollar and grew customer by customer. No venture capital. No press releases. Just profits.

2000: The bubble burst. SHI was too busy making money to notice.

Today: $16 billion in revenue. Still private. Still profitable. Never took outside investment.

Here's the through-line:

She avoided the spotlight. So she built substance.

She had no VC. So she kept control.

She couldn't rely on hype. So she made customers everything.

Every constraint became her edge.

Look at how she built the moat:

Ran HR herself for the first 10 years. Personally interviewed the first 1,000 employees. Her very first hire is still at the company 30+ years later.

99% customer retention. Fortune 500 clients including AT&T, Boeing, and Johnson & Johnson. Relationships built over decades, not quarters.
