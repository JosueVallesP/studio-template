## Post

"Gen Z is lazy." 
"Boomers can't use technology."
"Gen X doesn't get modern cultural references."

After 14 years hiring across four generations, I can tell you: age has nothing to do with whether someone makes an exceptional employee.

What actually separates top performers from *waves hand around vaguely at everyone else*:

1. They research before they ask. They aren't outsourcing their "figuring it out" muscle to someone else. 

2. They aren't afraid to pick up the phone. 

3. They understand WHY the work matters beyond their to-do list.

4. They balance listening and learning with their own sharp POV, bringing new yet informed ideas to the table. 

5. They have boundaries. They don't burn themselves out to prove loyalty to a company that would replace them in a week.

6. They care about people. They want to help. They're invested in outcomes, not just paychecks.

7. They don't wait to be told what to do - they figure out what needs doing and do it.

8. They understand their value (no I don't mean how much they should be paid) but what they bring to the company's success. 

9. They treat client relationships as sacred. 

10. They learn from everyone, younger and older, and are constantly curious.

My take: Generational stereotypes are just lazy management disguised as cultural commentary.

If your team isn't performing, look at your leadership. Not their birth year.
