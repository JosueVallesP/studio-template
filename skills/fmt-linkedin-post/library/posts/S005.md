## Post

In 6 years, I went from AE->CRO. I was a student of sales. I thought once I became CEO, I’d miss it, but you never stop selling. Here are 4 things being a CEO taught me about sales that I never fully understood before:

1. You’ll never get a big budget without being tied to board-level OKRs

Whenever we discuss big purchases, there’s a lot of internal friction and risk: 

- We need to shift budgets which is a pain in the as*
- We need to allocate a lot of resources to the project (= real cost)
- We need to think about the alternative value of that budget and resources
- We need to see if we need or should present the project to our board

So if the vendor’s solution is not deeply tied into the most critical problems and business priorities we’re focused on, there’s zero chance we’ll even consider it.

If I were an AE - I would try to understand if C-level approval is required and not work an unbudgeted deal not tied to board-level priorities. It’s a waste of time.


2. Developing business acumen is the biggest sales discovery shortcut

Naturally, I never mastered business at the level that I have now. How boards operate, the P&L, fundraising, navigating long-term risks, etc.

When I go on discovery calls now with a VP Sales (our ICP), I can read between the lines and understand what their execs and board are concerned about.

Think about the level of business case you can help your champions build when you understand more than they do what their CFO and CEO are thinking.

If I were an AE - I would spend hours learning about this stuff and building my business acumen, as much as sales training.


3. Helping a champion sell internally is the most underrated sales skill

We have made 100s of buying decisions since founding Aligned. Not once was our team properly prepared by the AE that was selling them.

It just blows my mind how big a missed opportunity that is.

Our team spent hours, but:

- Business cases were way too detailed
- Implementation felt too risky
- The solution didn’t fully tie to the root case
- It was too positive without potential risks

If I were an AE - I would focus my energy on becoming a business case expert and never leave internal selling to chance. That's the *real* sale.


4. Driving internal consensus is the second most underrated skill

When my team asks for a budget I always try to understand who else would benefit or would need to be involved from other teams.

Many projects lose momentum since we understand that other departments would need to be involved, but looping them in late often kills it.

If I were an AE - I would make sure to never miss asking who else this project might affect, and/or recommend who to loop in. And do it as early as possible.


TAKEAWAY:

Being a student of sales is a must.

Become a student of buying and you unlock the next level.

Don’t wait until you actually become a buyer.

Go interview your VP, CFO, CEO, etc. on how they buy!

You’ll learn more about sales than any sales crash course.
