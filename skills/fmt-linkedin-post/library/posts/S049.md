## Post

When Indra Nooyi became CEO of PepsiCo in 2006, she started writing handwritten letters to her senior leaders' parents.

"I want to thank you for the gift of your child to our company," she would write.

After an "ah-ha moment" visiting her own mother in India, Nooyi realized parents deserved credit for their children's success. So she made it her mission to acknowledge that.

Her team thought it was unusual, but Nooyi didn't care.

She wrote over 400 of these letters each year to parents of her senior executives.
The letters were part of her broader "Performance with Purpose" philosophy: treating people as complete human beings with families, dreams, and lives outside work.

- She invited families to company events. 
- She sent condolences during bereavements. 
- She made it clear that PepsiCo valued the person, not just their productivity.

The results spoke for themselves:

Under her 12-year leadership, PepsiCo's revenue grew from $35 billion to $63 billion, stock price doubled, and the company maintained strong retention rates and employer brand.
The letters symbolized a leadership approach built on empathy and genuine care.

Most CEOs see employees as resources to be optimized.

Nooyi saw them as whole people deserving recognition - and she made sure their families knew it too.

TAKEAWAY:

The most powerful leadership gestures aren't always grand strategies or bold moves. 

They're also the small, personal acts that show you see people as human beings first.

That's not just good leadership.

That's transformational leadership.
