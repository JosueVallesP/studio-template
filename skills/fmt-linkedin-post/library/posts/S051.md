## Post

There is a particular sport in watching a woman build a company, and then treating every decision she makes as proof that she should have never tried in the first place. We all see male founders praised for “discipline” when they restructure, and “innovation” when they incorporate new technologies. Meanwhile, women are asked to atone for having the nerve to make any decision at all. Throughout my life as a founder, I have received a reliably colorful amount of commentary. When you're visible, people project a version of you and hand it back to you as fact. They assign motives and convictions with remarkable confidence, untethered to actual reality or truth. 

Eighteen years ago, I sent an email from my kitchen in London with a few recipes and some recommendations. I called it goop. I did not have a grand plan to build a company, let alone an entire category. I had a clear point of view, my BlackBerry, and loads of curiosity about wellness, beauty, health and all the things that make daily life feel better. When goop began, many of the conversations we were having about women’s health, food, sleep, stress, sex, pleasure, and the relationship between how we feel and how we live were still treated as peripheral. But I believed they mattered. 

goop launched into the 2008 financial crisis, grew through the reinvention of digital media and commerce, and navigated a global pandemic that pushed so many consumer businesses into survival mode. I have gotten some things right, and other things wrong, but I have always listened, learned, and kept going. We have created products, conversations, communities, and businesses that I am enormously proud of. It has taken humility, gumption, and many late nights. It has not always been perfect, but we have consistently worked to adapt our business to the world as it evolved. AI is one part of today’s changing landscape for entrepreneurs. I approach AI as a tool, useful in the right places, but not a substitute for taste, judgment, imagination, or care. People continue to be the core of everything goop is. My hope is that AI will make room for more of the work only humans can do. 

No founder gets to keep every version of their company forever. As an entrepreneur, my role is to test, learn, and pivot - again and again. I am responsible to customers, partners, employees, investors, and a board. I will not pretend leadership is easy, and I will not perform perfection. As a founder, I am responsible for making the decisions needed to sustain goop and its future. Those decisions will always feel personal because people are personal. Building goop for two decades has only been possible because of our team’s commitment and passion for moving women's health, beauty, and wellbeing out of the margins and into everyday life. I am forever grateful to everyone who pushed us to ask the hard questions, and I’m ready, with that same drive, commitment, and care, to take goop forward.
