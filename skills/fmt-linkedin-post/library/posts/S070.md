## Post

When I was 22, I was working in journalism as a gossip columnist for The Londoner’s Diary on the Evening Standard. My job was essentially to go to glamorous parties that my newspaper had been invited to in order for the event to gain necessary coverage. I would be expected to bowl up to celebrities, introduce myself as being from The Londoner’s Diary and try to engage them in conversation despite being a total unknown. Out of that might come some newsworthy titbit for the next day’s paper that would serve both the celebrity and the deadline. It taught me so much about social confidence.

The only way to appear confident, I quickly learned, was to pretend I wasn’t nervous; that I wasn’t highly anxious or neurotic or terrified. It made me realise that confidence is something you can fake until, eventually, it becomes real. 

When you start going to the gym, you do the exercise in the hope of building a muscle that doesn’t yet exist. It’s the same premise.

Nobody tells you that readiness sometimes comes after you do the thing you're dreading, not before.
