## Post

Sara Blakely became the youngest self-made female billionaire in the world. She started with $5K and turned Spanx into a multi-billion dollar brand.

I spent the last few days reading through her story.

My summary and takeaways:

In the late '90s, Sara was selling fax machines door to door in Florida. 

She hated it, but she was good at it. 

One night, before a party, she had a problem. 

She wanted to wear cream-colored pants, but didn’t have any shapewear that didn’t show lines or feel like medieval armor. So she grabbed scissors and cut the feet off her pantyhose. 

“I saw how good my butt looked,” she said later. 

“And I thought...this is it.”

She didn’t tell anyone for a year. 

Instead, she went to work…literally. 

By day, she was still selling fax machines. 

At night, she was teaching herself patent law at the Georgia Tech library.

She called hosiery mills across the country, pitching her idea. But no one took her seriously. 

Most didn’t even call back.

So she took a week off, mapped a route across North Carolina, and drove from factory to factory, knocking on doors. 

One guy said no…then called her back. 

“I told my daughters about you,” he said. “They think your idea makes sense. I’m in.”

That was her first break.

Next came the name.

She’d heard comedians say hard “K” sounds make people laugh… like Kodak, Coca-Cola, Kinkos. 

She landed on “Spanks,” then tweaked it to “Spanx.” 

Trademarked it for $350.

For packaging, she couldn’t afford a design agency. 

So she sat on the floor with a dozen competitors’ boxes, studied what they all had in common, and worked with a friend to create something that looked nothing like anything else on the shelf …bright red, with cartoon graphics.

Then she cold-called Neiman Marcus.

She got the buyer on the phone and pitched hard. 

The buyer gave her 10 minutes…if she flew to Dallas. 

So she did. 

But halfway through the meeting, Sara could tell she was losing her. So she made a bold move: 

“Come with me to the bathroom,” she said.

There, she did a live demo. 

Changed into Spanx under white pants, walked out, and said, “Look.”

The buyer’s eyes lit up. “I get it now.”

Marcus gave her a trial in seven stores. 

She showed up at stores in person, trained the sales staff, and demoed the product on herself in the aisles.

Then came Oprah…

Sara sent a sample to Oprah’s stylist with a handwritten note. 

One day, she got the call: 

“Congrats…Oprah picked Spanx as one of her Favorite Things.”

Sales exploded.

She went on QVC and sold 8,000 units in six minutes. 

Eventually, she quit her job. 

Within a few years, Spanx was doing 100s of millions in revenue…and the rest is history. 

She didn’t get there by “thinking like a CEO.” 

She got there by being scrappy. Half-broke and all-in. 

And honestly, that’s the kind of founder I’m starting to admire most.
