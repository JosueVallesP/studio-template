## Post

A lot of advice about posting on LinkedIn often comes down to one thing: 

Be authentic.

But what does that even mean?

The most common interpretation is some version of “be raw,” “be vulnerable,” “share your failures” or “don’t overthink it.”

I get the appeal. 

After all, it’s hard to believe someone is performing when they’re showing you their worst moments.

The way I see it, though, is a tiny bit more nuanced. 

For me, authenticity boils down to the gap between your internal motivations and your external ones. In other words, are you posting something because you believe it, or because of how you expect people to react?

That’s the first question. 

The second is HOW you post it: thought through, or on the spot. 

Put the two together and you get four kinds of posts.

1. Careful honesty. A post you believe and thought through. You worked on it until it says exactly what you think. It’s authentic.

2. Raw honesty. A post you believe and wrote on the spot, like a quick comment where you say what you think without planning it. It’s authentic too.

3. Careful performance. A post you thought through for the reaction. You designed it to get likes. It isn’t authentic, even when it looks raw.

4. Raw performance. A post you wrote on the spot for the reaction, like jumping on a trend or agreeing with whatever’s popular without asking yourself what you think. It isn’t authentic either.

The two authentic posts have one thing in common: you believe them. 

So I think the bottom line is that being authentic on LinkedIn means posting what you actually believe, whether you spent two minutes on it or two days.
