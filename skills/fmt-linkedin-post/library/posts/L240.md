## Post

Synthetic respondents are the new fast food of market research.

Quick. Cheap. And you have no idea what's actually in the meat.

I watched a demo of a synthetic users tool recently. Their "persona building" is essentially asking an LLM to roleplay as your ICP.

That's not research. That's fan fiction.

LLMs don't know what your target buyer thinks. They know what has been written about people like your buyer.

When a real VP of Eng tells you "we evaluated 5 vendors and compliance killed 3 of them," that is ground truth. When an LLM says it, it is improv.

Research exists to create certainty. You do it because you are about to commit budget, reputation, roadmap and 3-6 months of team effort.

I talked to a research company founder whose platform offers both. He told me customers try synthetic research, then inevitably come back to human research.

Why? Doubts. They read the outputs and are like "does this really reflect reality?" And the only way to know is to run human research to validate it... and now you are paying twice. So better to cut straight to the high-fidelity intel (straight from the source) and save money.

Synthetic has its place: low-stakes decisions, B2C research where personas are broad ("men 35-50 buying jeans"), early brainstorming.

But for B2B where you target a specific job title, at a specific company size, in a specific industry? There's so much to context to how those people think, and LLM generated respondents are generating bs. 

I get it that speed and cost are seductive. But isn't it decision certainty that you're actually buying.
