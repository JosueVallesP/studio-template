## Post

I never planned to tell this story publicly.

Phoebe Waller-Bridge told it for me, without either of us quite realising, at first, that she had.

Years before Fleabag existed, I told my friend Phoebe about the day I had my first miscarriage. I was at brunch with a friend visiting from Australia. I'd had a scan the day before that showed no heartbeat, but - typical, Type-A people-pleaser that I was - I didn't want to let my friend down, so I went anyway. Halfway through brunch, I went to the loo and that's where I discovered it was happening. Right there and then.
I remember going back to the table where my friend was waiting, sitting down and thinking: I don't want to ruin this. I suppose, looking back, part of me was in denial.
But the pain was increasingly difficult to ignore. I had to say something. So I did. And my friend was extraordinary. She said: “I’m getting us a taxi, and we’re going straight to the hospital.” And that’s what we did.

Years later, Phoebe - by this time a global superstar thanks to her hit show Fleabag - called me. "Elizabeth,” she said. “I think I've done something without meaning to…"
She described the scene. She’d written a version of my miscarriage into the opening episode of Fleabag, season 2.  I told her: that's brilliant. Take it. Do with it what you will.

Watching it back, it was both different from my experience and completely faithful to it. That’s what good art does with pain. It doesn't reproduce it. It finds the shape underneath it that other people recognise as their own.
I’m so grateful to Phoebe for turning this experience into a story so many can understand and relate to.
And for those who’ve been through it? Hopefully that scene is a little bit comforting and healing too.
