## Post

When people tell me they want to build a personal brand, they usually mean they want to start posting. They want to show up on LinkedIn a few times a week, grow their following, maybe start a newsletter.

But personal branding is not just posting on social media.

Let’s use Christopher Nolan (my favorite director) as an example. 

If you study his life, you’ll notice he does almost none of the things we usually call personal branding. He has no social media accounts. He doesn’t use email or a smartphone, and he doesn’t let people use phones on his sets. He rarely talks about himself unless he has a film to promote.

And yet millions of people believe something specific about him. 

That belief comes from his films, how he makes them and what he says no to. He crashed a real Boeing 747 for Tenet, built a rotating hallway for Inception and planted 500 acres of corn for Interstellar.

That’s what a personal brand is: what people believe about you. 

You have one too, on a smaller scale. The people who know you believe something about what you’re good at, what you care about and what you’re like to be around, and you had that long before you posted anything.

Posting can change that belief, but it can also make it worse. 

If everything you write sounds like everyone else, the people reading it will assume you’re like everyone else.

So personal branding is shaping that belief on purpose instead of leaving it to chance. 

And I think a brand is good when the belief is true and specific.

* True means it matches who you are and reflects the true caliber of your work. If people believe less than what’s there, you get smaller opportunities than you deserve. If they believe more, it falls apart the first time they get close.

* Specific means the belief fits you and almost nobody else. “Talented director” fits thousands of people. “Makes huge, original films that play with time, shot on film, with as much as possible built for real” fits one. 

That’s how a brand turns into leverage: people say yes to your next thing before they’ve seen it. 

Universal gave Nolan a $100 million budget for Oppenheimer, a 3-hour, R-rated film about a physicist, before a single scene existed. It made about $975 million and won Best Picture.

So if you want to build a personal brand, I wouldn’t start with a posting schedule. 

I’d start with what people already believe about you. You can find out by asking a few people who know your work how they’d describe you to a friend who needs what you do.

If what they say could describe thousands of other people, like “a great consultant,” or if it sells your work short, posting more won’t fix that. 

You fix it with the work you take on, what you say no to and what you show people. 

Once the way people describe you is true and specific, posting starts to help, because it takes that belief to people at scale.
