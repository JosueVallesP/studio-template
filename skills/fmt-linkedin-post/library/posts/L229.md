## Post

In 2012, I moved from Tokyo to LA with three kids and only $20,000 in my bank account.  I didn't know anyone, so I joined the most expensive public golf course I could afford and started showing up every Saturday at 6 AM.

Here's what I found out: 

Successful American business people (the kind of people I needed to meet) play golf from sunrise on Saturday mornings. They tee off early so they can be home by 10 or 11am for their families. 

Many people who play from that early come as a single player and are paired with 3 other strangers. You get to meet new people every week.

I'm not a great golfer, but that wasn't the point. 

The point was to put myself in a room (or on a fairway) with the right people, for enough time to have a real conversation. A genuine four-hour conversation about life, family, work, and whatever else comes up between holes.

Over six months, I built the core of my LA network on that golf course. 

Investors, advisors, potential clients, future partners (people who would never have taken a cold call from me but were happy to chat on the back nine).

The lesson isn't "go play golf." 

The lesson is this: stop trying to network through channels designed for networking. Conferences, LinkedIn messages, cold emails (those are saturated). 

Instead, find where your target community already gathers for something they enjoy, and join them there. 

If your ideal clients play chess, find where CEOs play chess. 

If they run marathons, train for one in their city.

You'll never build the best relationships in a meeting room. 

You'll build them in the spaces where people let their guard down.
