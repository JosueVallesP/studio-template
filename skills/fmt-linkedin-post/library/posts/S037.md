## Post

The typical Brit understands why a footballer gets paid a lot, but it seems most people have no idea how hard it would be to be a CEO.

The CEO of Tesco makes £10M a year. 

This is the top job for a business that is a supermarket, bank, telco, and insurance provider. This business has over 340,000 employees and generates £70 billion in revenue. It has over 50,000 suppliers.

Every day, this business encounters logistical issues, financial issues, regulatory issues, and a thousand other problems that need solving. To do this, it needs an army of competent executives, and these executives don’t work for someone who’s lucky or went to the right schools, they work for someone who’s highly competent. A CEO has to mobilise this top talent around them.

A CEO is responsible for bringing in institutional capital. The cost of doing this through a consultancy would be 1–2%, so if a CEO can bring with them £500M of institutional money who trusts them to perform, it’s worth £5–10M on that alone.

A CEO has to be a master problem-solver, a leader of A-type execs, a fundraiser, a marketer, and a strategist.

This type of person has options. They could start their own business, they could work at any number of companies globally, they could do M&A deals, or join a PE firm. All of these roles would pay £5M–10M.

A CEO is also independently wealthy by the time they get to this level. Why take on the stress of this job if the money isn’t significant?

Also, at this level, the CEO is paying 45% income tax, so almost half of what they earn goes toward the UK tax office already. In one year, the CEO of Tesco pays £4M of income tax.

All of this to say that anyone who thinks anyone could do the job or that a great CEO would do it for £500K a year is simply naïve to what’s involved. 

Like me saying, “Doesn’t a footballer just kick a ball around? I’d kick a ball around for half the price.”

If the Tesco board could get a CEO for millions cheaper, they would.

Every little helps, after all.
