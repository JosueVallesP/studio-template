## Post

If you want to hire top talent in traditionally lower status roles (content, partnerships, people, etc) — then pay them like traditionally higher status roles (PM, growth, finance, legal)! 

The only reason I considered joining Clay in a content role a few years ago was that I was paid like a PM. Many of my teammates are also doing their jobs for the first time — even though obviously their zone of excellence — because we finally gave them a reason to try:
- Peter Kang came from BizOps; now he runs our social media storytelling 
- Jessica Jin was a PM; now she runs our brand schemes, from our billboard to customer kung-fu fight videos
- Everett Berry was an engineer and founder who turned into our first sales leader

This basically comes down to Varun Anand, who is world-class at seeing someone's real spike and making it financially worth acting on. He knew my Substack, Jessica's viral political campaigns, and Peter's TikTok before any of us joined, and built roles around what we were already proving in our spare time.

Talented people optimize for money, but their actual potential often lives somewhere the market doesn't reward. Companies willing to pay for the person rather than the title end up summoning world-class work, attracting more unique talent, and compounding into a team no one can copy.
