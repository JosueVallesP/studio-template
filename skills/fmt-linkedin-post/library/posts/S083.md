## Post

A lot of people I work with tell me they want an audience on LinkedIn. 100k, 500k, 1M+ followers. You know, BIG numbers. When I ask them why, they almost always say some version of this:

"I want more clients," or "I want people to come to me so I don't have to chase work," or "I want to be known in my industry" or "I want to get invited to speak at the big conferences in my field."

Fair enough.

But if you really think about it, none of that requires a huge audience.

An audience doesn't automatically give you clients or referrals or a spot on a big stage. An audience is a proxy for reputation. People use big followings as a heuristic for how good and how trusted someone is, and that's ok. The problem is that when you focus on "building an audience," you'll almost certainly start playing to strangers. 

I suggest a much simpler approach:

Don't start by posting tips or writing "I help X get Y" in your headline and hoping the right people find you.

Flip the script a bit.

Write down a list of 30, 40, or 50 people you deeply admire, you'd love working with, or who could open a door for you. Then, start creating just for them. They're your audience. I call this "reverse-audience" building because you're building your audience before you publish anything.

When I started publishing on LinkedIn, I admired Justin Welsh and wanted to get to know him. He had 300k followers at the time, so a cold message from me was never going to work.

So I went through every post he'd published over four years, picked the hundred best, turned them into templates, and built a carousel with all hundred. Before publishing it, I sent it to him.

He was surprised that anyone had put in that kind of work. The carousel helped me grow, and it also started a relationship with someone I admired.

This is just an example.

You can break down someone's work, write a case study about their company, or invite them into something you're making, like an interview series.

The idea is that you give them a reason to get to know you, without asking them for anything.

Yes, it looks slower at the beginning. 

But the point was always the opportunities, and opportunities come from people.

Plus, AI is making content abundant, so this type of content becomes more valuable every year. What's still rare is someone who takes the time to study your work and make something just for you.

If one of your goals is to get more clients, be known in your industry, or speak at big conferences over the next few years, start by building relationships with the specific people who can help you get there.

You'll get to know the right people AND eventually build an audience because a relationship with one well-connected person will open so many doors that you'll start meeting their clients, their peers and their followers, and some of them will start following you too.
So, two birds in one shot.

Hope that's helpful.
