## Post

In 1990, I landed in Australia with £70 in my pocket.

4 months later, I flew home with $15,000 from selling my 1st ever business.

Back then, you were supposed to have at least £1,000 in the bank to even get an Australian visa.

So I rocked up to the Embassy with the cash, got approved…
↳ Then spent the whole lot celebrating that week.

(I know… genius financial planning 😂)

By the time I actually flew out, I had £70 left to my name.
Which disappeared within a few drinks the moment I landed 😅

And that’s when I realised I was screwed & needed a job.

So one day, when I walked past this car garage, I asked the guy:
“Any chance of me washing some cars here, mate?”

He looked at me like I was slightly mad… but agreed 😂

I borrowed a bucket & sponge off him, stuck up a big sign that said “Tru Clean Cars”.

And just like that, I apparently had a business 🤷🏼‍♂️

3 or 4 days in, a bloke walks over & asks me to clean 200 cars for his sales team.
Not gonna lie, I thought I’d hit the jackpot.

4 months later, I sold the whole thing for $15,000.
(which was an unbelievable amount of money back then!)

People often say you need a lot of cash to start something.

And in hospitality, that's not wrong at all.

But I started a business in different country with no money.
(Not even my own bloody bucket 😂)

Turns out, a bit of nerve is all it takes to get going.

You can always figure the rest out later 👊🏻
