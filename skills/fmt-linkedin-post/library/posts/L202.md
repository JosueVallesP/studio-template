## Post

Most founders and CEOs I work with complain about "Office Politics." 

But let me get this straight…

In 99% of cases, the toxicity wasn't coming from the employees. It was coming from the CEO’s inability to set boundaries.

Here are the 4 changes you need to make to stop "office politics" dead in its tracks:

1. Model the Directness You Expect

Your team is just a mirror. If you avoid difficult conversations, your team will learn that "being nice" is more important than "being true." The result? Gossip, back-channeling, and passive-aggressiveness. You created the politics by refusing to model directness.

2. Stop Being the "Chief Therapy Officer"

A team member comes to you to complain about someone else. Instead of saying, "Have you spoken to them directly?" you listen and sympathize. Why? Because it makes you feel needed (Validation) or important (Control). But by doing this, you are effectively institutionalizing gossip. You are teaching your team that the way to solve problems is to complain to "Dad" or "Mom" rather than facing the issue.

3. Audit Your Own "Shadow Archetype"

When there is tension, which part of you shows up?

* The Controller: Do you pit people against each other to maintain power?
* The Avoider: Do you ignore toxic behavior because you are scared of conflict?
* The Competitor: Do you need to be the smartest person in the room, making your team fight for your attention? 

Your "politics" are often just your own ego needs playing out on a larger stage.

4. Stop Protecting the "Toxic High Performers"

Every interaction builds your reputation. The late-night email you send while stressed, the way you handle a "star performer" who is a cultural toxic waste dump…it is all communicating what you value. If you permit political maneuvering because the person delivers revenue, you have just promoted politics to a core value.

---

Your business problems are personal problems in disguise.

If you want a transparent culture, you have to be a transparent leader.

That’s just how it works.
