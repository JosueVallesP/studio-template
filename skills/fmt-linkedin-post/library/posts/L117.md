## Post

I don't think people understand just how brutal the job market is right now

*25% of unemployed people have been jobless 6+ months. That's the highest ever outside of the Great Recession and a brief blip during covid. So people are jobless longer now than after the dot-com crash + 9 other recessions dating back to 1950.

*Average unemployment is now 24 weeks. Also the worst dating back to 1950 outside of 2010 and 2020.

*Yes, the official unemployment rate is 4.3%. Not bad. But that only counts people in the "labor force." Add in people who have basically given up finding a job or those working part-time and want FT work, and it's 8%. 

*Now you're talking over 8 million people who have been jobless 6+ months or just given up finding a job. Compare that to the 188k TOTAL jobs added in all of 2025.

*The 6 months unemployed number is key because when you go beyond that, you lose unemployment benefits (if you could get them to begin with).

*Quit rates are down. Hiring rates are down. People are hunkering.

*This is especially hard on young people. Entry-level job openings are down 35% in a year.

*The unemployment rate for recent college grads is worse than for the general population for the first time in history. Despite an average $30k in student debt.

*And all of this doesn't even factor in AI yet, which has only impacted employment by 0.09% so far.

So yeah that's where we are.
