## Post

The coaching industry has a strange relationship with proof.

On one hand, coaches are asked constantly to demonstrate ROI... outcomes, metrics, behavioural change. Sponsors want numbers. L&D teams need to justify budgets. The pressure to measure is real and it's not going away.

On the other hand, the most transformative coaching I've witnessed and personally experienced is the kind that's almost impossible to quantify. A shift in how someone sees themselves. A decision they didn't make. A conversation that unlocked something months later.

I'm not against measurement. Done well, it's essential.

I just think we're sometimes measuring the wrong things. Not because we want to, but because they're the only things we know how to measure. Activity over outcomes. Sessions over shifts.

The question I keep sitting with: what would it look like to measure what coaching actually changes, rather than what it produces?

Curious whether others feel this tension, and how you hold it with clients and sponsors.
