## Post

You’re not Steve Jobs. You’re not Elon Musk. You’re not Travis Kalanick. And honestly? You shouldn't want to be…

Most founders I work with don't have a "Messiah Complex." They just want to be good leaders of healthy companies. So when they're struggling to scale, I don't tell them to read another tech biography.

I tell them to read Marcus Aurelius.

Yes, the Roman Emperor.

Here’s 10 Marcus Aurelius principles that scale-up leaders can actually build on (all from Meditations):

1. “What is not good for the hive is not good for the bee.” Scale isn’t about your heroics, but about the health of the whole. Make choices that strengthen the organisation, not your personal preferences.

2. “If it is not right, do not do it; if it is not true, do not say it.” Drop the drama. Consistency and honesty beat 3 a.m. intensity every time.

3. “You have power over your mind…not outside events.” Anchor yourself in what you can control: priorities, standards, behaviour. Let go of the rest.

4. “The impediment to action advances action. What stands in the way becomes the way.” Treat blockers as the brief. Constraints are the path, not the excuse.

5. “Ask yourself at every moment, ‘Is this necessary?’” Complexity is a tax on scale. Ruthless simplicity is a kindness to your team.

6. “Waste no more time arguing what a good man should be. Be one.” Culture is behaviour. Make the hard calls cleanly and fairly. What you permit, you promote.

7. “If someone can prove me wrong… I shall gladly change.” Coachability is a superpower. Change your mind quickly when the truth demands it.

8. “Don’t be overheard complaining… not even to yourself.” No victimhood. Own the next move and model agency.

9. “At dawn, when you have trouble getting out of bed… remember: I am rising to do the work of a human being.” Leadership is an endurance sport. Protect your stamina or you become a liability.

10. “Objective judgment, now. Unselfish action, now. Willing acceptance, now.” See things clearly. Act for the whole. Accept reality without theatrics.

The Takeaway:

The media loves the story of the tortured genius who changes the world while destroying everyone around them.

But if you want to build a business that lasts (and survive the journey yourself without losing your family or health) stop trying to be a Hero.

Just be a leader.
