## Post

The number one mistake early-stage startups make: hire too quickly.

It's tempting to think that your next hire is going to solve all your problems. But before you hire anyone, start with one question:

“What is their mission?”

When hiring, people often chase shiny resumes. Candidates that have had stints at successful companies. But is their experience even relevant?

To answer this, deconstruct the role you're opening up and ask: what must this person accomplish to truly have an impact?

For first-time founders, this can be especially hard because they've never done the job themselves so they don't even know what the role truly entails.

One solution: try to do the job yourself. The point is not to become an expert; think of it as going on reconnaissance for this role. You're scouting the situation so you know exactly what type of person you need to accomplish the mission.

Before we hired our first growth marketer, I ran growth marketing myself for 6-12 months. By background I'm a finance person. Ex-CFO. I'd never run influencer campaigns, never built a paid funnel, never thought deeply about hooks or creative strategy.

So I learned the hard way. Onboarded our first wave of creators myself, one call at a time. Spent my days in our Gambassadors Slack talking to power users, reviewing content, iterating on how we told our story. Worked directly with agencies, tried and discarded ideas, watched which scripts bombed and which formats quietly printed signups.

That period was uncomfortable. I felt like a beginner again.

But that was the point.

If you haven't done the work (even at small scale) you're hiring blind. You don't know which metrics actually matter, which tradeoffs are real, or what "great" looks like day to day. You'll over-index on resumes and buzzwords because you have nothing else to go on.

So next time you feel pressure to "scale the team," start with one question.

“What's the mission?”
