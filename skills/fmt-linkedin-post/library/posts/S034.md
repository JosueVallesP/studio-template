## Post

5 reasons why I'm so skeptical of the entire AI tracking industry:

1) We know that AIs give different answers every time they're asked the same question. Different brands, different order, different sentiments, different levels of endorsement. You'd have to ask ChatGPT the same question more than 1,000 times to get two answers with just the same list of brands in the same order (and 10s of thousands to get two answers with the same sentiment around each item on that list)

2) We know there's no source of truth about prompt volume. Clickstream panel data is the closest thing, but it can only see the first prompt, can't see inside any app behavior, can't see API-sent prompts, and can't see any prompts inside many signed-in/paid accounts (e.g. within corporate firewalls). And the AI tracking companies aren't even disclosing which vendors they buy from (wouldn't surprise me if some are entirely lying about even using clickstream panels in their volume estimates).

3) We know that every past interaction changes the response an AI gives you. So even those experiments we did showing you could get "brand visibility %" can't account for the massive degree of personalization.

4) Anything/anyone claiming to measure influence through URL citations has to prove that the models are using those citations to power the response and not just as post-hoc rationalization. To my knowledge, no study has confirmed this and plenty have suggested it's somewhere between messy and total baloney.

5) There's no audit trail. No way to prove a particular prompt recommended a brand. No way to prove that, if they did, it resulted in incremental lift. No way to know what impact, if any, a given exchange had on a give sale. Even correlation between things like "how did you hear about us?" surveys or visibility % lift in tool reports vs. sales from "direct" visits are sketchy at best. If these are the metrics you trust, why don't you trust such fuzzy logic for other channels? Why do PR, events, comms, content, organic SEO, et al. have to be so bullet-proof when AI gets to be full loosey-goosey?!

Here's how I'm playing it -- invest in and measure mentions, watch incremental lift, ignore the rest.

p.s. one more -- I've never seen a high quality, controlled study showing brand visibility % lift in an AI tool while a brand turned off other channels to avoid contamination correlated against sales lift. Yet I remember how many thousands of these had to be published in the 2004-2014 era of SEO in order for that channel to earn investment. It's maddening.
