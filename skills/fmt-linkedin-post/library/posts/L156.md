## Post

If you want to become a better writer in 2026, I can’t recommend Ray Bradbury’s program for aspiring writers enough.

I spent several months with it last year, and am so glad I did.

The goal, in Bradbury's words, is to “stuff your head” with as many different ideas as possible. A voracious reader and self-described “collector of metaphors,” he saw this as the prerequisite to good writing.

To stuff one’s head most effectively, Bradbury prescribed a nightly reading routine of:

→ 1 piece of short fiction
→ 1 poem
→ 1 essay

To avoid the burden of selecting the texts myself and to increase the breadth of what I was reading, I turned to anthologies. This included the Norton Anthologies of Short Fiction and Poetry, along with The Art of the Personal Essay.

Along with the reading, I also found it helpful to take notes on anything that stood out to me. This basically became a commonplace book full of my favorite quotes. 

And while I didn’t start churning out perfect prose in my sleep (still hoping this is a delayed benefit), it was a great way to get more familiar with a ton of legendary writers. I was introduced to the likes of Seneca, Plutarch, Dickinson, Hawthorne, Poe, Wharton, Montaigne, and Melville, among others. Melville’s “Bartleby, the Scrivener” and “An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge” by Ambrose Bierce became 2 new favorites.

Yes, writers probably have the most to gain from this practice. But I don’t think it’s a stretch to say that anyone could benefit from reading more like this—more widely, more regularly, and more deeply. Especially as media keeps getting faster, slowing down and methodically exploring work that has stood the test of time was refreshing.

I really ought to pick this back up myself this year, so I welcome any recommendations across the 3 groups!
