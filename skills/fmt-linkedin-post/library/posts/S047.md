## Post

A boyfriend once asked me where I wanted to go for lunch. My immediate response was: "I don't know, where do you want to go?" He said, "No, you tell me what you want."
And the truth, baffling now in hindsight, was that I genuinely didn't know. Not because I was easy-going. Because I was terrified that if I said what I actually wanted, he wouldn't want it too and that would be the thing that made him leave.

I spent most of my 20s trying to be a version of a person I thought was more palatable. Easier, cooler, less needy, constantly trying to contort myself into the shapes I imagined others wanted me to form. It sounds like a nice, generous way to be. But it isn't. If you think about it, people-pleasing to that extreme becomes quite selfish because if you never let someone see who you are, how can they ever really know you, or choose you? It’s dishonest.

Over the years, I’ve come to understand the difference between fitting in and belonging.
Fitting in is changing yourself according to what you think someone else wants from you.
Belonging is being accepted as you truly are. Flaws and all.

I only really learned the difference in my late 30s, after a marriage that ended, after some genuinely humbling years and after finally being honest with myself about what I actually wanted - both for lunch (!) and in life.

If you're still working this one out: you're not behind. Some of us just take the scenic route.
