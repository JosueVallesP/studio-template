## Post

I'm so confident that writing will remain a career-defining skill in the AI era that I'm literally betting my future on it. Here's why.

Until AI advances to a point where it can truly think for itself (at which point we're all probably screwed), thinking clearly will continue to be one of the most important skills in every profession and industry—as it has been for basically all of human history. 

And as someone who sees clear writing as the best way to create clear thought, what better way to hone both skills than by working in a role that forces me to write at scale? I’ve seen huge positive effects of this even in just the ~10 months I’ve been working at Compound. Having to turn thoughts into words day in and day out is an incredible forcing function for building this muscle. 

I also think that building strong writing skills is exactly what sets you up to be most successful with any of the AI tools that exist today (or are most likely to exist in the future). Of course, no one knows for sure how LLMs will evolve. There’s certainly a possibility they advance past the point of needing direct human instruction. But until then, the people who will see the greatest benefits from these tools are those who are exceptionally good at communicating their thoughts. This is, of course, the crux of writing.

So while I have no idea what's going to happen with professional writing in the future, I'm confident that the skill of writing (and all the subskills contained within) will continue to be both extremely important and valuable in all areas of life. 

Which is why I’m not only comfortable but excited to be working in a writing-intensive role right now.
