## Post

For years, employees were told not to post from work.
In some cases, people were even fired for it. 😳

Now, brands are doing something different: they're handing employees the camera directly.

This week, Starbucks announced a pilot program with TikTok at Cannes Lions International Festival of Creativity that expands its employee creator initiative, allowing employees to create content and participate in ad revenue opportunities. 

Starbucks is even helping shape a custom Creator Network *within* TikTok's Content Suite. 

Love this shift. 

Not long ago, employee-generated content was seen as a risk. Today, it's becoming a marketing strategy.

Why? Because consumers often prefer to trust people more than polished brand campaigns.

☕️ A barista showing how they make a drink.
☕️ A flight attendant sharing a day in the life.
☕️ A store associate explaining their favorite product.

These moments often feel more authentic than content produced in a studio. 

The bigger story isn't just in Starbucks.
It's in the evolution of who gets to be the brand voice.

We're moving from brand-generated content to creator-generated content to now, even employee-generated content.

The employees who were once told to stay off social media are increasingly becoming some of a company's most valuable marketers.

What used to be a liability is now an asset. 🤏🏽🤏🏽🤏🏽

And that's a pretty amazing shift in how brands think about authenticity.
