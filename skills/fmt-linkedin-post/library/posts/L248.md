## Post

Most people think strategy is about setting goals.

Wrong.

Goals are just destinations.

Strategy is building an advantage that survives competition.

If anyone can copy what you're doing, you don't have a strategy.

You have a head start.

One of the best frameworks I've come across is The 7 Powers by Hamilton Helmer.

It explains why some companies compound for decades while others stall.

The core ideas:

1. Scale Economies
As you grow, serving each additional customer becomes cheaper.

2. Counter-Positioning
Win with a model incumbents can't adopt without hurting themselves.

3. Switching Costs
The more painful it is to leave, the stronger your position.

4. Branding
Trust reduces decision-making. People choose the familiar.

5. Cornered Resources
Own something others can't easily access - talent, technology, data, or distribution.

6. Process Power
Great systems aren't invented overnight. They evolve through years of iteration.

The question isn't:

"How do I grow faster?"

It's:

"Why won't someone bigger just copy this?"

Answer that, and you're thinking strategically.

Growth matters.

Defensibility matters more.
