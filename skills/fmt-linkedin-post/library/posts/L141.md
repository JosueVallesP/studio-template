## Post

I had a client send me some AI copy yesterday and ask if I could give it an edit. They didn’t tell me it was AI copy (of course not), but I knew by the second sentence. The thing was riddled with the same format: “Not X. Not Y. Not Z. But A." Over and over again for a grand total of 5,283 words. 

And for the first time in many moons, reading it made me feel optimistic. 

While editing those 5,283 words, my eyes got tired. That kind of grammatical repetition feels like staring at the same open plains for dozens of hours while driving cross-country. 

It was the Pantone Cloud Dancer of writing. Pale, anemic, flat. 

Mediocre at best, at worst — and for most discerning readers — utterly cringe. 

So why did it get me excited?

Because every time I see people default to this kind of “writing,” especially in their marketing, I’m reminded just how much more the writing I do snaps, crackles, and pops. 

The competition is getting weaker IF you choose not to fall into the same trap. 

The more brands use Scandi beige AI writing, the more my work stands out in technicolor, brashly and unapologetically. That's always my goal: a careful balance of vibrancy and resonance.

As a brand strategist, I want to write so well that it makes you accidentally smirk on a Zoom call when you’re secretly shopping instead of listening or forget yourself and laugh out loud in a coffee shop. I want it, now and again, to give you goosebumps.

And as a discerning consumer, I’m increasingly interested in brands that are interested in that, too. Brands need to do more, not less, to reach us. 

That kind of storytelling doesn’t have to look complicated; in fact, often, it looks *simpler* than AI writing. Cleaner. 

But don’t be fooled. It takes innate creativity and skill to do it well.

When you stop committing to stand-out writing everywhere, when you start outsourcing it to non-humans, the sensational power of what writing *can* do dilutes and fades. And so does that a-ha feeling that catches hold of us randomly — you, me, your customer, everybody. 

Seems like the wrong direction to be moving.
