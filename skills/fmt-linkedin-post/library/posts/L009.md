## Post

when seasoned editors, writers, and readers claim they can tell when something was written by AI, we’re not being snobby, showing off, or trying to look cool: we are just *very* familiar with narrative structure after years of working with and enjoying it 😉  

today I came across a large-scale study from April, co-authored by Uni of Maryland & Google DeepMind. they analysed 61,000+ stories of ~5,000 words each and found they could tell human-written stories from AI-generated ones with 93.2% accuracy just by looking at structural features. 

or, said in a different way: the now ubiquitous “it’s not X, it’s Y“ and “not this. not this. just that“ are sentence-level tells, but you can also ignore them and tell AI writing based on structure alone, for example: 

• AI stories tend towards predictable plotting, tight causal chains, linear narrative vs. human authors are more likely to lean on moral ambiguity +  temporal complexity

• AI narrators spell out the moral or theme of the story 77% of the time vs. human writers do it half of the time only, leaving you, the reader, to infer it

• AI writing over-indexes on physical sensations and bodily metaphors, 81% vs. 38% human (I found this one hilarious—AI doesn’t have a body... yet why does it rely on it so much?!)   

the graph below shows how AI-generated writing clusters together in a region of space that is... very much *not* where human writing sits.

TLDR: the next time you can tell a piece was written by AI and people roll their eyes at you, send them a link to this paper 😉

PS: the paper is titled StoryScope: Investigating idiosyncrasies in AI fiction. and while, yes, the research was *specific* to fiction, I’m fairly convinced a lot of semi-factual marketing content these days suffers from the same structural tells.
