## Post

The Story of iPod
Part 2: The Room Where iPod Happened.

In Part 1, I told you how Phil asked me to work on a speculative new project with Tony.

Finally, in March 2001, we found ourselves in the Junior Boardroom at Infinite Loop, pitching the idea to Steve. I opened by walking through the market, the competition, and the opportunity.

Then Tony took over. He laid out three possible approaches for building the product:

One used DRAM.
One used removable storage.
One used a built-in 1.8-inch hard drive from Toshiba.

Tony and I already knew which option we believed was right. But part of the process was presenting all the alternatives and discussing the tradeoffs.

Steve rejected the DRAM option. Too expensive, too many experience tradeoffs.

He also rejected the removable storage option. Too big, too expensive, and also too many experience tradeoffs.

But the 1.8-inch hard drive option? That felt right.

Tony then lifted part of the black drape to unveil various components, and like LEGO pieces, showed how they could fit together to create something remarkably small. Then, he pulled off the rest of the drape and revealed wood models that gave a sense of the final product size. They were weighted so you could feel what the device might actually feel like in your hand.

The photo below is one of the early unweighted models Tony had made, which I’ve kept all these years.

He handed one to Steve. Steve couldn’t let go of the model.

That told us a lot.

The conversation then turned to how people would navigate hundreds or thousands of songs. Phil got up to the whiteboard and started drawing a wheel, talking through how it could help people quickly move through their music library without pressing an up or down button hundreds of times.  That idea ultimately became the iPod scroll wheel.

Finally, Steve said he wanted to move forward.

Then he added one more thing.

He wanted it in time for the holidays.

Meeting over.  Decision made.

The entire iPod team at that moment of Tony, Jeff, and I walked down to Caffe Macs to regroup.

For a few moments, nobody said anything. We were trying to process it all.

Then one of us, I think it was Jeff, finally broke the silence.

“I guess we’re doing this.”

The three of us laughed.

And then reality set in.

We had just been given the green light to build a completely new product category for Apple and somehow get it done before the holidays.

Looking back, what I remember most isn’t the meeting itself. It’s that moment afterward.

Three people sitting together, realizing the enormity of what we were taking on, and still finding the resolve to go do it.

History often looks inevitable in hindsight, but in the moment, it rarely feels that way.

Most breakthroughs begin as a small group facing what feels like an impossible mission…and deciding to move forward anyway.

Seven months later, on October 23, 2001, Steve introduced iPod to the world.
