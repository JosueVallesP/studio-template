## Post

Companies don’t stall because of product.
They stall because the story’s weak.

Inside the walls, everyone has a slightly different definition of:
• what you do
• who you serve
• why you’re different
• where you’re going

Outside the walls, the market can’t articulate your value in a sentence.
Investors hear a fuzzy pitch.
Sales teams improvise.
Marketing fills the gaps with “more content.”

And slowly… momentum leaks.

But I’m not letting you go out like that.
So I’m giving you the exact structure I used to advise 23 tech unicorns:

Start with the Shift.
What's changing in the world? New tech, new communication channels, political change, shifts in pop culture, shifts in values. This is the context that makes everything else matter.

Define the Enemy.
Old game vs. new game. Failed mindsets. Conventional wisdom. Siloed workflows. Misbeliefs.
Name what's broken. Make people feel the tension.

Establish your Ethos.
Who you champion. Your beliefs, principles, and most of all vision. The hill you'll die on. The conviction that drives your product. This is where you earn trust.

Now bring in Product.
The bold new category it represents. It’s the only thing that ends the Enemy. Show them what it does. How it works. What it means. 

Finally Customer Outcomes.
Revenue. Market share. Lower costs. Pipeline. Reduced churn. And yes, happiness.

I didn’t put this in the graphic but at the very, very end, you have to add a final bump of swagger.

Everyone’s swagger is different but here’s the vibe:  “our product’s not for everyone, but for the right customer, it moves mountains. We’re currently taking calls to identify best-fit customers.”

End with the brand and a confident tagline, like so:

Stripe
Pay it forward. (or whatever I just made that up)

(Mic drop)

…….

Most teams read this and still can’t actually do it. They know their story is fuzzy, but they don’t know how to sharpen it.

If that’s something you’d want to be part of, DM me ‘Mic Drop.’
