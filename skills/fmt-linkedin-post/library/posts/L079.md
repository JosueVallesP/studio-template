## Post

Apologies if I shock you when I say this…

…but you know, it is actually ok to be an employee.

Don’t know if anyone else feels this, but it seems to me we’ve had about a decade of cultural pressure to be an entrepreneur, to start a business.

The subtext - never directly spoken mind you - always seems to be that if you work for someone else, you’re a bit of a loser. And aside from being untrue, it’s also pretty destructive.

FIRST, we actually don’t need more businesses. There are enough of the buggers, and most bring zero new value to their markets whatsoever.

SECOND, enjoying running a business is actually a pretty niche personality type. You basically have to love selling, as that’s what it mostly comes down to - and most people hate that. Hence why you see all these miserable solopreneurs out there who don’t understand why they can’t just “do their craft” and get paid for it. Only they can, because…

THIRD, being an employee often allows you to practice the thing you’re really good at, in an environment where someone else is responsible for getting customers for it. That’s an awesome deal! Do what you love, with a guaranteed paycheck, while other people do all the stuff you hate. Sweet.

Don’t get me wrong, being an entrepreneur is awesome if that’s your bag.

But those people never needed to be convinced to work for themselves by a bunch of emotionally manipulative online soundbites. They’d have done it anyway.

Ultimately what’s important is that people understand themselves, their unique gifts, where they can bring value to the table, and not be made to feel like cowards because they’re not “doing their own thing”.

If you spend your time doing work that suits you, you ARE doing your own thing.

And 9 times out of 10, that’s got nothing to do with starting a business.
