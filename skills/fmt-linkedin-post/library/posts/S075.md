## Post

Twelve years.
That's how long I spent trying to have a baby. Through IVF, miscarriage, and eventually the decision to stop. I've written and talked about that journey a lot. What I talk about less is what I needed from the people around me while it was happening.
So if someone in your life is going through this right now, a friend, a sibling, here's what actually helped - and what didn't:

-Don't disappear. The instinct when you don't know what to say is to say less, see them less, give them space. Please don't. Staying, even awkwardly, is the support.

-Don't manage your own discomfort by minimising theirs. "You can still keep trying, you never know." "Everything happens for a reason." I understand the impulse behind these. They still land as: please make your pain smaller so it’s manageable for me. Instead, you could try saying “I’m so sorry for what you’re going through. I’m here for you in whatever ways you need” or “I might not understand how this feels, but I want you to have the space to tell me.”

-Ask them what approach they’d like you to take, rather than assuming. I wanted different things from different people at different points. The only way anyone got it right was by asking.

-Show up in unglamorous, practical ways. A meal ordered to their door. A grocery shop. A postcard saying you’re thinking of them. 

-It takes a great deal of bravery to announce you need comfort from outside sources, so the best we can do is meet those who are going through these times half way.
Not trying to fix anything, just not looking away.

If you've supported someone through this, or been supported well, I'd love to hear what helped.
