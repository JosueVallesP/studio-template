## Post

Unilever's Fernando Fernandez says the days of underinvesting in brands to squeeze out more profit are over.

Procter & Gamble’s CFO Andre Schulten puts long-term brand strength at the center of its growth strategy.

Nike's Elliott Hill is redirecting investment towards brand storytelling and consumer connection.

adidas's Bjorn Gulden says top-of-funnel activity has improved performance further down the funnel.

At AB InBev, Michel Doukeris calls creativity a competitive advantage that helps grow the beer category. LVMH's legendary Bernard Arnault thanks brand desirability and creative passion for growth.

Tapestry's Joanne Crevoiserat directly connects Coach’s disciplined brand building and creativity to compounding growth.

Different businesses and categories, some protecting strong positions while others rebuild them. But the people in charge keep coming back to brand.

The compounding effects of proper positioning achieved by creativity and broad-reach emotional storytelling is only becoming more important as AI lowers the barriers to entry and levels the technical playing field.

Specifically the role of advertising in changing behaviour to create future demand. In my research with System1 and Effie Worldwide (The Creative Dividend) we found that long-running distinctive ad campaigns that are emotional are 7.5x more likely to lead to profitable business growth.

We must plan in years, not quarters.

Thanks to Aline Meloni for sharing similar on innovation. I agree, businesses that focus on consumer value creation and long-term brand strategy will win.
