## Post

So many people have been asking me how to break into narrative roles at startups. My answer is the same as it is for any role: share genuinely useful feedback. Some thoughts specifically for narrative/media: 

Pick a few companies you actually want to work at, look carefully at their public presence, and give them good ideas about how to improve it. Where are they missing the mark? What stories should they be telling, how, and where?

If someone emailed us right now with ideas for amazing YouTube shows that were independently interesting and relevant to Clay — and proved they could produce/showrun them — we'd jump to work together! Same goes for any strong ideas about written editorial concepts. It would make it even easier if they had public work that proved that they can think clearly and create quality media (writing, video, etc). 

Most people approach job searching with negativity or dread like they're fighting an uphill battle. The reality is so different. Companies like Clay have gaps we're desperate to fill, and finding the right person is genuinely hard and slow. If you align your taste, quality, and creativity with an understanding of our audience and a few great ideas that show you're serious, we'd be so excited to have you.

Think of it less as banging on a closed door and more like manual focus, where you're trying to bring two images into alignment. We know what we need; your job is to show us you see it too. And we WANT you to make it easy for us so we can welcome you in as fast as possible!

I've been doing this for years, and it's how I've made every role I've loved! (linking below to an essay I wrote 6 years ago after creating my first startup job with no background)
