## Post

Been meaning to write this for a while: After coaching people on building LinkedIn audiences for 7 years, there's a core piece of advice people end up agreeing with and ignoring anyway. And it defeats the whole purpose of posting.

I'm deliberately not gonna sum this up in 1 sentence because it requires some deep thinking to truly internalize and accept.

When people come to me (for coaching or ghostwriting), they usually say "I don't need to go viral - I want the right people to see my posts and do business with me."

So we aim for posts that the CFO of a manufacturing company (or whatever) would see and be impressed with. Except by design, these posts can't "do numbers." Something that speaks to that CFO and offers real insight is something most people DGAF about. So the post doesn't pop off.

Except because that CFO is not going to make a multi-million dollar deal off a single LI post, you don't magically get $1 million, either. Basically you get no signal that it's working. I try to explain this and that the strategy takes months to work.

Inevitably they will stumble into making a viral-ish post that gets them more likes than they've ever seen. One that has nothing to do with them or their expertise but has broad appeal. They get high off the engagement and decide this is their new strategy. Except those posts are empty calories that the CFOs will not care at all about - if anything it will turn them off because you now look more like an influencer than an actual business partner.

In short you forget the whole point of why you're doing this and fall down the same dopamine death loop millions of other people have fallen down since the advent of social media. This leads to burnout and people give up posting altogether.

The core problem here is that the CFO strategy provides little-to-no feedback while the influencer strategy punches you in the face with "this is working! You are great!" But those are false positives. A mirage.

The way to get around this is to commit to posting long-term (at least 3 months) without obsessing over metrics. Around 3-6 months you start getting signals that the CFOs are buying in (key follows, DMs, IRL "hey I love your posts" comments, etc.).

This sounds daunting but in my mind it's a good thing. Because social media is not a get-rich-quick scheme, most people give up and you can stand out simply by outlasting everyone, as long as you're willing to experiment and improve.
