## Post

In 1989, David Ogilvy sold his agency for $864 million and bought a 30-bedroom French castle with the profits. Here's every random job he tried before that:

1. In 1931, at age 20, he was thrown out of Oxford University. He couldn't pass the exams. His GPA was a disaster.

2. He moved to Paris and worked in the Hotel Majestic kitchen. He worked 63 hours a week prepping meals for the hotel dogs. He saw the head chef fire a man for plating a dish 2 minutes late. Learned that "standards" are the only currency that matters.

3. At 24, he returned to Scotland to sell AGA cooking stoves. He knocked on doors for 6 hours a day. He faced hundreds of rejections to sell a single $500 stove.

4. He became the top salesman in the company, so he wrote a 32-page guide for the other reps. Fortune Magazine later called it "The best sales manual ever written." It was his first piece of copy.

5. He emigrated to the US and joined George Gallup’s research firm. He personally conducted 424 interviews. He learned that people say one thing and do another.

6. In 1942, he was recruited by British Intelligence. He worked at Camp X analyzing secret diplomacy. He learned to condense 20-page reports into 3 bullet points for the generals.

7. At age 35, he bought a farm in Pennsylvania. He spent 3 years trying to grow tobacco. He had no electricity. He broke his back working the fields and still couldn't turn a profit.

8. In 1948, at age 38, he moved to New York. He had $6,000 in the bank. He had never written an ad campaign in his life. He launched "Ogilvy & Mather" with zero clients.

9. He had a tiny budget for a shirt client (Hathaway). He bought a $0.50 eyepatch from a drugstore and put it on the model to create "mystery." The shirts sold out in 1 week.

10. For Rolls Royce, he spent 3 weeks reading technical manuals. He found one fact. He wrote: "At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock." Sales jumped 50%.

11. He scaled the agency to 450 offices in 120 countries.

12. In 1989, WPP acquired his agency for $864,000,000. He retired to Château de Touffou in France.

---

Ogilvy didn't become the best despite being a chef, a spy, and a farmer. He became the best because of it.
