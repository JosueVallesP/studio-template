# Shorties: exemplars

> Reference for the `shorties` type (`../shorties.md`). Method by Josué Valles. The posts quoted here are other people's, kept as they were published: learn the move, never copy the wording or the tics (dashes, stacked fragments, labelled takeaways). Every number, name and example in a post the studio writes comes from the person's sources or is a real case marked `borrowed: verify`; the figures in this file show the level of detail, never material to reuse.


Sixteen complete posts from the 358-post library. Every one is a keeper.

At 49 median words there is no room for anything that isn't load-bearing, so these annotations mostly work by **subtraction**, what each post leaves out, and what breaks if you put it back. Where an entry flags a weak line, it is a within-post observation about a specific move, not a verdict on the post.

Read the entries for your construction before drafting. Two pairs are deliberate:

- **294 and 131** make the identical claim, 14 words versus 78. The gap is entirely alibi, and reading them together shows exactly what extra length can and cannot buy.
- **245 and item 9 of 171** are the same parallel construction, one without a gradient and one with.

# The Ratio

## Post 67: 24 words, one sentence, and the argument is the arithmetic

**24 words**

**Why it's here.** The tightest construction in the corpus. Twenty words of setup, then a nine-word verdict delivered deliberately flat, *"your business is not going to make it"*, because the premise already carries all the heat.

**The post never does the math.** It doesn't say "you are burning 13,000× the cost of the subscription." It puts `$200k/year` and `$15/mo` next to each other and the reader multiplies. That is the whole mechanism.

**Swap test.** Replace `$200k/year employees` with "expensive employees" and `a $15/mo Calendly subscription` with "a cheap SaaS tool" and the post reads: *if your expensive employees rebuild cheap software you'll fail.* A shrug. **If you can swap the noun without changing the post, the post has no engine.**

**Note the syntax.** The main-clause verb is terminal. The reader cannot get the point early because the grammar withholds it, that's what a periodic sentence buys you at this length.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
If your $200k/year employees spend their time vibe coding a replacement for a $15/mo Calendly subscription, your business is not going to make it.
```

</details>

## Post 361: False precision, three jokes, 38 words

**38 words**

**Why it's here.** The most mechanically dense joke in the corpus, and the technique is copyable.

**Joke 1 is the second line.** *"Billionaires in the world: about 3,000 / Billionaires on LinkedIn: about 300,000,000."* The mechanism is deadpan false precision, but the load-bearing word is **`about`, repeated**. The fabricated number carries the identical hedge as the true one, in the identical grammatical frame. Drop the second `about` and it becomes a rant.

**Joke 2 is specification.** *"They all have a PDF to share laying out their exact system, and exactly one spot left in their course."* The criticism is never stated. `a PDF to share`, `exact system`, `exactly one spot left` are the genre's own clichés quoted verbatim, **reproducing them is the attack**. And `exactly one spot left` reuses the false-precision joke one clause later.

**Joke 3 is ventriloquism.** *"So what's your excuse??"*: spoken in the mocked party's voice, aimed at the reader. The doubled question mark is the joke. This is not a CTA, and it is worth understanding why: a Shortie that asks for engagement has spent its last line on a request instead of a payload.

**It fails safely.** Strip the comedy and the literal content survives as a legible grievance about fake gurus. The joke is the top layer, never the load-bearing one.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
Billionaires in the world: about 3,000
Billionaires on LinkedIn: about 300,000,000

The best news? They all have a PDF to share laying out their exact system, and exactly one spot left in their course. So what's your excuse??
```

</details>

## Post 350: ANTI-PATTERN RISK, the only post here whose failure mode is inversion

**47 words**

**Why it's here.** The highest-cost item in the corpus and the most dangerous to imitate carelessly.

**Structure: irony, irony, silence, receipt.**

> *"Absolutely amazing job by big companies this year to have zero layoffs due to mismanagement, or to push short-term profits."*
> *"Every single one has been AI's fault."*
> [three blank lines]
> *"(Pay for Fortune 500 CEOs went up 25.6% last year and pay for their employees grew 1.3% adjusted for inflation)"*

**The writer's actual position appears nowhere.** No sentence states it. The claim exists only in the gap between the second line and the parenthetical, and the blank lines are load-bearing whitespace, the pause where the reader does the inference and gets to feel clever.

**The punchline is a quantifier.** *"Every single one"*: no category is 100% anything, and that impossibility is what makes the irony unmistakable.

**The parenthetical is insurance, not evidence.** It exists to retroactively force the ironic reading. This is the one post in the corpus whose misfire mode is inversion: undetected sarcasm reads as praise for the companies it attacks. Both mitigations are deliberate, the excess of *"Absolutely amazing,"* and the receipt at the end.

**What would kill it:** ending on "…which is obviously nonsense given what CEOs were paid." Any clause stating the writer's real view collapses the construction.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
Absolutely amazing job by big companies this year to have zero layoffs due to mismanagement, or to push short-term profits. Every single one has been AI's fault.



(Pay for Fortune 500 CEOs went up 25.6% last year and pay for their employees grew 1.3% adjusted for inflation)
```

</details>

# The Closed Loop

## Post 294: The whole post is one sentence, and nothing was appended

**14 words**

**Why it's here.** Fourteen words. No coda. The only post in its group that resisted the urge to add an encouraging line, and the reason it survives intact.

> *"If you respect your audience's time, they will repay you with more of it."*

**The mechanism is inside the grammar.** The verb is `repay`; the currency is a single noun used as both what's spent and what's earned. That closed loop is what makes it a Shortie rather than a quote-card. The quote-card version, "Respect your audience's time", has no consequence and no exchange.

**Material test for this construction:** would adding "For example…" add anything? If yes, the sentence isn't self-proving and you need a different construction.

**What it trades away.** No number, no proper noun, no witness, so it eats the banality risk directly rather than buying an alibi. That is a legitimate trade, and it's why the shortest posts have to be the tightest. Compare post 131 below, which makes the same claim in 78 words and spends all 64 extra on an alibi.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
If you respect your audience’s time, they will repay you with more of it.
```

</details>

## Post 292: Two rates compared, with no numbers

**20 words**

**Why it's here.** The strongest abstract line in the corpus, and it works by borrowing the Ratio's structure without needing quantities.

> *"The best creators stay ahead because they get bored of their content before their audience does."*

**Two non-obvious moves in sixteen words.** (a) It contains a *comparison of two rates*, creator boredom versus audience boredom, which is a disparity claim with the numbers removed. (b) `get bored` is an involuntary state rather than a discipline, which inverts the advice the reader expected ("keep going even when bored").

**Then the coda.** *"Never stop evolving :)"*: four words of generic imperative, zero information, and an emoticon doing damage control. This is the corpus's clearest instance of the pattern worth internalising: **the urge to add a line of encouragement after the mechanism is the single most reliable way to convert a Shortie into a poster.**

The test is simple. Read the last sentence alone. If it's an imperative with no consequence attached, that's where the post became a quote-card.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
The best creators stay ahead because they get bored of their content before their audience does.

Never stop evolving :)
```

</details>

# The Exhibit

## Post 131: 78 words, and 64 of them are the alibi

**78 words**

**Why it's here.** It makes the same claim as post 294 above, small acts of consideration compound, and it is 64 words longer. **The idea is not bigger. The extra words are not elaboration.** They buy four things:

1. **A witness.** *"i asked my son who his best friends are in kindergarten…"*, four words establishing that this happened.
2. **Attribution armour.** *"he said 'you just have to say something nice to them.'"* The claim is exactly as abstract as 294's, but a five-year-old said it, so it cannot be convicted of being a platitude. **This is the load-bearing purchase.**
3. **A one-word scale-up.** *"he named ten kids. TEN!"* The caps do the work of a full explanatory sentence about how best-friendship is supposed to work.
4. **A deflating exit.** *"except samantha, who said she wasn't his best friend. samantha needs to chill."*

**Beat 4 is what pays for beat 3.** Cut the Samantha line and the post ends on *"may we all say nice things to each other and have 10 best friends"*, which is a greeting card. This post states its moral outright (*"the cure to the loneliness epidemic"*) and then immediately buys it back. **Stating a moral is survivable if you undercut it one line later.**

**And the exhibit is verbatim.** Paraphrase: "he basically said you should be nice to people", leaves the post with nothing at its centre.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
i asked my son who his best friends are in kindergarten...

he named ten kids. TEN!

i said, "wow you're really good at making best friends. what's your secret?"

he said "you just have to say something nice to them."

there you have it people. the cure to the loneliness epidemic. 

may we all say nice things to each other and have 10 best friends. 

except samantha, who said she wasn't his best friend. samantha needs to chill.
```

</details>

## Post 208: Proof by subtraction

**52 words**

**Why it's here.** The cleanest instance of the Exhibit construction, and it introduces a device worth naming.

**The exhibit is a question**, and it would survive with every word of commentary deleted: *"What does our customer actually resent about this category?"*

**Proof by subtraction:** *"Nobody touched the product. Nobody changed the price."* Listing what was *not* needed proves the question's power more cheaply than demonstrating what was. It appears three times in the corpus, here, in 352's asterisk list, and in 314 in its entirety.

**Watch the first and last lines.** First: *"The best brief I ever saw was one question long."* Last: *"That question was the work."* The last line **renames** the first, *brief → question → the work*. A definitional collapse, not a repetition. **If your final line paraphrases your opener, the post never moved.**

**Note what's absent.** No client, no agency, no year, no job title. *"The best brief I ever saw"* is the entire provenance. Adding "at a CPG agency in 2019" would cost words and buy nothing.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
The best brief I ever saw was one question long.

What does our customer actually resent about this category?

Everything else, the messaging, the angle, the campaign, followed from that. 
Nobody touched the product. Nobody changed the price. 
Just one question asked before anyone opened a laptop.

That question was the work.
```

</details>

# The Collision

## Post 149: One sentence, and the grammar makes it impossible to resolve

**36 words**

**Why it's here.** Thirty-six words, one sentence, and the construction's requirement is visible in its syntax.

> *"It's absolutely insane to me that we're 3+ years into a radical cutting of UX jobs **and yet** we're also in the midst of a huge technology shift that really really needs designers and user researchers."*

One stance verb (*"It's absolutely insane to me"*) governing two `that`-complements yoked by **and yet**. The contradiction is grammatical: *and yet* obliges a second half and nothing obliges a third, so the post **cannot** end anywhere else.

**The material's entire value is that it is unresolved.** Add "We need to fix this" and the post dies. This is the one construction where supplying an answer destroys it, which makes it the easiest to ruin in editing, because the instinct to land a point is exactly wrong here.

**No names, no company, no researcher, no evidence.** The absurdity has to be already visible to the reader without a word of explanation. If you have to explain why it's absurd, this construction is closed to you.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
It's absolutely insane to me that we're 3+ years into a radical cutting of UX jobs and yet we're also in the midst of a huge technology shift that really really needs designers and user researchers.
```

</details>

## Post 277: The self-facing collision, and what makes it expensive rather than plaintive

**92 words**

**Why it's here.** The same construction turned inward, and the corpus's second-highest expenditure of the writer's own status.

> *"I don't know whether this makes me feel guilty or lucky."* … *"I did two events last year. One was hers. We grew EBITDA by 45%. Which I was happy with until I read this."* … *"I love my life (and my 'cute' lifestyle business), but I can't help but think how much more business I could have brought in."*

**The numbers are what make it cost something.** `two events`, `45%`, without them this is a founder feeling vaguely inadequate, which is cheap. With them it's a founder publishing a specific comparison in which he comes off worse, next to a named person he calls the hardest-working woman in his industry.

**And it hedges about a feeling, never about a fact.** *"I don't know whether this makes me feel guilty or lucky"* is the only hedge-shaped construction in the entire 21-post corpus, and it qualifies an emotion. There are zero hedges on any factual claim anywhere in the corpus.

**No resolution.** The post ends on *"this woman I respect so much"*, still holding the problem. That is what makes it repeatable in a comment.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
I don’t know whether this makes me feel guilty or lucky. I respect the HELL out of Alina Vandenberghe - hardest working woman in SaaS by FAR. I did two events last year. One was hers. We grew EBITDA by 45%. Which I was happy with until I read this. I love my life (and my “cute” lifestyle business), but I can’t help but think how much more business I could have brought in if I was out there hitting 100s of events and dinners like this woman I respect so much.
```

</details>

## Post 140: Punchline as punctuation

**32 words**

**Why it's here.** Thirty-two words that explain nothing, assert nothing new, and were the corpus's third-highest performer.

> *"You CANNOT TELL ME that role, the one with more than 1000 applicants, which has been posted for months and was reposted again, is Still. Open."*
>
> *"Who, exactly, are you waiting for??"*

**Every fact in it is already in the reader's possession.** The post's only contribution is the indignation and the question.

**The punchline lands inside two words.** A 25-word periodic build with two interpolated relative clauses deferring the verb, then: `is Still. Open.` Three words, two full stops. **The punctuation is doing the timing that a paragraph of explanation would otherwise have to do.**

**It ends less resolved than it began**: statement to question, and the resolution has to happen in the comments. That's a deliberate ending type, not a failure to conclude.

**What it spends:** a named enemy in the second person who can see the post. That is the currency.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
You CANNOT TELL ME that role, the one with more than 1000 applicants, which has been posted for months and was reposted again, is Still. Open.

Who, exactly, are you waiting for??
```

</details>

# The Confessed Pull and the Delayed Swap

## Post 68: The line that separates confession from advice

**71 words**

**Why it's here.** Delete one sentence and this becomes a LinkedIn hiring tip.

> *"A mistake I've painfully made over and over again:"* … *"**My heart almost always defaults to hiring the young, high energy inexperienced person.**"* … *"But time and time again, person A makes my life bettter."*

**Beat three is the construction.** Admitting you made the error is common. Admitting you *still feel the pull toward the wrong choice* is what makes it a confession rather than a lesson delivered from above.

**"Over and over again" and "time and time again" are structural, not stylistic.** A one-time mistake does not qualify for this construction, the repetition is what converts a data point into a rule the writer has been forced to accept against his own instincts.

**The ending is a parenthetical concession:** *"(You also want person A to have a great attitude)"*. It pre-empts the obvious objection and concedes it, which costs the post its hardness on purpose and reads as honest rather than preachy.

**And note the typo.** *"bettter"* sits inside the verdict sentence and the library kept it at 319 reactions. Polish is not load-bearing in this form.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
A mistake I've painfully made over and over again:

Its almost always better to hire people who've already done what you want done at your company vs. hiring someone without the skill or experience but great attitude.

My heart almost always defaults to hiring the young, high energy inexperienced person. 

But time and time again, person A makes my life bettter.

(You also want person A to have a great attitude)
```

</details>

## Post 379: The reader convicts himself before knowing he's on trial

**67 words**

**Why it's here.** The only post in the corpus where the setup (36 words) legitimately exceeds the payload (31 words), because the setup *is* the mechanism, not scene-dressing.

> *"If a candidate for CEO sat in your office and said, 'I have zero self-awareness, I can't manage my emotions under pressure, and I plan to burn out twice a year,' you wouldn't hire them. Obviously."*
>
> *"**Yet** most founders run their own internal operating system exactly like that."*

**The verdict is extracted from the reader before the defendant is named.** *"Obviously"* is the trap closing, the reader has already agreed.

**The test for this construction:** if the reveal line could have opened the post without loss, there was no swap, just a comparison. Here it couldn't, opening with "most founders run their own operating system badly" is a claim you argue with. Arriving at it after *"Obviously"* is a claim you've already conceded.

**The ending instructs**, which nothing else in the corpus does: *"You have to hold yourself to the same standard of performance and sustainability that you demand from your C-Suite."* That is necessary here. A post that convicts the reader and then stops is just an insult.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
If a candidate for CEO sat in your office and said, "I have zero self-awareness, I can't manage my emotions under pressure, and I plan to burn out twice a year," you wouldn't hire them. Obviously. Yet most founders run their own internal operating system exactly like that. 

You have to hold yourself to the same standard of performance and sustainability that you demand from your C-Suite.
```

</details>

# The Chant and The Bank

## Post 245: Cadence carrying the whole load, and what a chant needs to accumulate

**45 words**

**Why it's here.** No evidence, no story, no specifics of any kind. The repetition *is* the argument, and the frame never varies:

> *"One wants comfort. / The other wants growth. / One wants easy. / The other wants better. / One quits when it's hard. / The other keeps going. / You decide which one gets out of bed. / You decide which one wins."*

**The requirement is invariance.** Change one couplet to "The other one, meanwhile, tends toward growth" and the chant stops, and there is nothing underneath it.

**What a chant needs that this one is missing: a gradient.** The three pairs are the same opposition three times with the vocabulary swapped; they shuffle with no loss. Compare the triad inside post 171 below, which does the same move with an escalation:

> *"A great consultant convinces you that the right answer lies with him. / A great teacher shows you that the right answer lies within the right question. / A great master shows you that the right answer lies within you."*

Each term moves the locus of the answer further from the speaker and closer to the reader, **and the verb shifts**, *convinces* → *shows*. That is what parallelism is for. Reach for a chant only when your restatements move somewhere.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
Two versions of you wake up every morning.
One wants comfort.
The other wants growth.
One wants easy.
The other wants better.
One quits when it's hard.
The other keeps going.
You decide which one gets out of bed.
You decide which one wins.
-DM
```

</details>

## Post 171: The Bank, ten items, and only the ends are ordered

**391 words**

**Why it's here.** The corpus's longest post at 391 words, and the compound form: N independently publishable Shorties under a count header.

**Individual items obey the standalone rules, with exactly one relaxation.** Items run 23–55 words, roughly 1.5–2× a standalone Shortie. Item 5 carries a 27-word subject noun phrase before its verb, *"The reason you are saying we don't have time to think right now and we must do something right away **is** that…"* Nothing cold could ask for that much patience. **The count is what buys the relaxation:** a reader who sees "10 ideas" has pre-committed to ten payloads and will tolerate a longer runway on each.

**Several items are complete standalone Shorties, shelved.** Item 7 is a 23-word single sentence with a terminal payload, the same shape as post 67. Item 9 is the triad quoted in the entry above.

**The middle is unordered; the ends are governed.** Item 1 is the most concrete and dyadic. Item 10 escalates to a superlative you cannot top, *"This is our most fundamental cognitive bias"*, which makes it terminal by necessity.

**The weakest item flags itself.** Item 8 needs a self-correcting parenthetical the other nine don't: *"not because logic is art (it is not) but because…"*

**Its header is a container, not a claim.** *"10 ideas for greater clarity in 2026"* is a label with a date. Compare post 26's header, *"Overthinking builds problems, not solutions."* Five words, chiastic, a claim, and itself a complete Shortie. **The reader leaves 26 with a thesis they can repeat and leaves this one with a list they liked.**

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
10 ideas for greater clarity in 2026

1. If you want to tell the story of success, focus on the actions that led to it.

But if you want to achieve success, focus on the thinking that led to it.

2. You can keep chasing 100s of frameworks, mental models, tactics, playbooks, success stories, tools, and templates throughout your career, and yet the fundamental capability that will unleash your product mastery across domains and companies is this: understanding how people think.

3. A subtle reason why smart people make blunders when making career decisions and investment decisions is that they want to feel smart while making the decision and explaining it to others. The seductive appeal of feeling smart trounces the rational goal of making the correct decision.

4. The default reaction of “I already know that” (and the smug satisfaction from saying it and believing it) is the greatest invisible barrier keeping many otherwise-smart people from actually achieving their often-stated but ever-elusive career goals.

5. The reason you are saying we don’t have time to think right now and we must do something right away is that you said the same thing last time and before that and the time before that, and so we are now perpetual pawns of problems that shouldn’t have arisen in the first place.

6. Everyone with high intelligence will violently agree that a clever analogy is never a good reason for why you should do something, and yet in practice we are easily persuaded in meetings when someone uses a clever analogy to justify why we should do something.

7. Most conflict at work, at home, and in society comes from the illusion that of course other people can read your mind.

8. Actually using logic for important decisions is more art than science, not because logic is art (it is not) but because the user has profound flaws and overcoming those flaws is pure art.

9. A great consultant convinces you that the right answer lies with him.

A great teacher shows you that the right answer lies within the right question.

A great master shows you that the right answer lies within you.

10. We explain everything by what is visible, sophisticated, and recent, while the truth is found in what’s invisible, inscrutable, and predetermined. This is our most fundamental cognitive bias.
```

</details>

# The Straight Face

## Post 314: The payload is typographic

**21 words**

**Why it's here.** The corpus's outer limit, and the only post that can fail to render as a post.

> *"Here's a list of ALL the people who built a cult following by outsourcing 100% of their thinking to ChatGPT:"*
>
> [seventeen blank lines]
>
> *"Exactly."*

**`ALL` and `100%` are the same setup move as `about` in post 361**, establishing a register of false rigour for the void to mock.

**It exploits the platform.** The blank lines force the "see more" fold, so the reader has to act to reach the punchline. **This post cannot be paraphrased; there is no sentence in it that carries the claim.**

**Its precondition is unusually strict:** the claim must be a negative universal that a blank space can prove. If one counter-example exists, the whitespace is a lie and a single commenter destroys the post.

**Its failure mode is silence, not offence.** If the reader doesn't scroll, or the platform collapses consecutive newlines, there is no post at all. Nobody is upset; it simply doesn't exist.

**The one weak element is `Exactly.`** It tells the reader they got it, which is the writing equivalent of explaining the joke. The gap was already the content.

<details open>
<summary><b>Full post</b></summary>

```
Here's a list of ALL the people who built a cult following by outsourcing 100% of their thinking to ChatGPT:


















Exactly.
```

</details>
