# Type: shortie

A very short post, usually 15 to 80 words, that compresses one idea and stops. Method by Josué Valles, built from 21 vetted posts (median 49 words, range 14 to 391). At this length nothing on the page isn't carrying weight, so most of this file is about what to leave out. **The failure isn't writing too little. It's adding one more line after the post was already finished.**

## What it's for

An observation, a one-liner, a ratio, a gripe, a line someone said, a contradiction the person hasn't resolved, one hard-won rule, posted as it is rather than expanded. Also for a draft with one good sentence buried in three hundred words of scaffolding: find the sentence and cut the rest. Short posts sit beside the biggest engagement in the library (L067, L131, L113, L007).

## Where it sits in the library

- **Structure:** S7 (the body is the hook), always; the bank path is S1 (the bare list) under a header.
- **Hook moves:** whatever the material is. Most often 7 (the call-out), 8 (the pattern I keep seeing), 13 (the reframe), 9 (the line someone said) for the exhibit, 15 (the admission) for the confessed pull, 11 (the finding) or 10 (news, plus a take) for a ratio, 14 (the honest version) for the straight face.
- **Close:** C6 (no close) almost always; the endings below are the last beat of the post itself.
- **Inside moves:** M2 (the turn) is the whole post; M5 (the side-by-side) carries most ratios.

What this file adds to S7: the one-turn rule, the choice between a ratio and a disposition, seven paths, the coda rule, and what a shortie has to spend.

## Facts and sources

A shortie still states facts. A number about the world is `borrowed: verify` with its source; a number, a line someone said, or an admission about the person comes from the four sources in `CLAUDE.md` rule 1. The exhibit path quotes exactly or not at all. A number that looks measured (a "9 in 10") is measured, with its source, or it's cut. Exaggeration that every reader can see is a joke (card 14's territory) isn't a fact claim; a precise-looking number dropped in for texture is.

## Step 1. Find the one turn

A shortie has exactly one point where the sentence being read revises the one before it, and it can always be pinned to a phrase: "they will **repay** you with more of it"; "before **their audience** does"; the gap between "$200k/year" and "$15/mo"; "is **Still. Open.**"; "**Yet** most founders..."

**Two turns and one is dead weight.** At this length the reader registers one. Delete the first; if the post still works, it was scaffolding.

**No turn and it's a statement, not a post.** That's a routing signal, not a failure: a statement with evidence behind it is an `op-ed`; one with a procedure behind it is a `super-specific-how`.

## Step 2. Ratio or disposition

The register isn't a matter of taste. One question decides it: **does the material contain two quantities?**

**If the idea is a ratio, specificity is required.** Every specific shortie in the corpus is about a gap: $200k against $15 a month (13,000 times); 25.6% against 1.3%; 95% against 5%. There's no abstract way to say 13,000 times. Take the numbers out of the first and you get "if your expensive employees rebuild cheap software you'll fail", which is a shrug.

**If the idea is a disposition, specificity would be false.** Respecting people's time, getting bored of your own work, being useful rather than charismatic. These have no two quantities, and adding some would falsify them: "I got bored of my content after 40 posts" is one person's data, not a rule.

**So it's specificity or cadence, one of the two.** The only two corpus posts with neither a number nor a proper noun are also the only two with a repeating sentence frame. That's the substitute.

What each buys: specific buys the chance to be wrong, which is what makes a post read as a position rather than a mood (you can argue with the Calendly post; nobody argues with "respect your audience's time"). Abstract buys portability: the one-liner works in a talk, a bio, a deck. Posts with at least one number had a median of 907 reactions; posts with none, 158, though on 21 posts the numbered ones were also the funny and risky ones. The fair reading: **abstract shorties are library assets, specific ones are performance assets.** A feed of only the first reads as a fortune-cookie factory.

A number in a shortie does one of two jobs: it's the **weapon** (the number is the reasoning; the sentence only holds it), or the **receipt** (in parentheses, stated flat, after the joke; the other order turns the post into an op-ed with a snide tail).

## Step 3. Route

| The material is | Path | Material test |
|---|---|---|
| Two quantities whose gap is the argument | **The ratio** | Do the numbers convict before a word of comment? If you have to explain why the ratio is bad, this path is closed. |
| One sentence whose surprise comes from a "because" or an "if" inside it | **The closed loop** | Would adding "for example" add anything? If yes, the sentence doesn't prove itself. |
| An exact sentence someone else said | **The exhibit** | Could every word of the person's own be deleted and a reader still get it? A paraphrase disqualifies. |
| Two things both true that shouldn't coexist | **The collision** | Is it still unresolved for the person? If they have a resolution, this path is closed. |
| A wrong call they've made more than once, and still feel pulled toward | **The confessed pull** | Do they still want to make the wrong choice? A one-time mistake doesn't qualify. |
| A pairing they can restate three or more times | **The chant** | Does each restatement move somewhere, or is it the same opposition in new words? |
| Several items on one subject, each publishable alone | **The bank** | Would removing any one weaken nothing? If item 5 needs item 4, it's a chopped-up essay. |

Plus one way of delivering a ratio, an exhibit or a collision: **the straight face**, where the person's actual position appears nowhere in the post and exists only in the gap the reader closes (step 5).

The harder calls:

- **The collision is the easiest to ruin in editing,** because the instinct to land a point is exactly wrong. Its value is that it's unresolved. Add "we need to fix this" and it dies. The absurdity has to be visible to the reader without explanation.
- **The confessed pull turns on its third beat.** Admitting the error is common; admitting they still feel pulled toward it is what makes it a confession. "My heart almost always defaults to hiring the young, high-energy, inexperienced person." Delete that and what's left is a hiring tip. The pull has to be in the person's sources; it's a feeling, and house rule H24 says a feeling is never supplied.
- **The chant needs a gradient.** House rule H29 flags triads, and a chant earns one only when each line moves. The corpus contrast: one triad is the same opposition three times in new words (shuffle it and nothing is lost); the other is "A great consultant convinces you that the right answer lies with him. / A great teacher shows you that the right answer lies within the right question. / A great master shows you that the right answer lies within you." Each line moves the answer away from the speaker and toward the reader, and the verb shifts from "convinces" to "shows".
- **The bank relaxes one limit, time to payload, and the count pays for it.** Items can run one and a half to two times a standalone shortie, because a reader who sees "10 ideas" has signed up for ten. The middle can be shuffled; the ends can't. Item 1 defines or gives permission; the last raises to a stake that can't be topped. The header is itself a complete shortie, a claim, not a label with a date. Nine reads as the number that survived; ten reads as a target filled.

## Step 4. Draft, then cut to the turn

Three things hold across all 21 posts.

1. **No hedges.** Not one "I think", "arguably", "kind of" or "in my experience" in about 1,800 words. One post hedges about a feeling ("I don't know whether this makes me feel guilty or lucky") and never about a fact. A hedge at 30 words spends a tenth of the post weakening the only thing in it. (This is the reverse of `borrowed-story`, where hedges predicted which posts survived checking, because a borrowed story asserts checkable facts about real people and a shortie asserts a position.)
2. **No middle.** A shortie is claim plus payoff. The only connectives in the corpus are "but", "and yet", "yet", "while", "except", "instead", "vs." Not one "the reason this matters is" or "which shows that". Nine of 21 turn on a single "but" or "yet" in the last third: a contrast carries two ideas in the space of one.
3. **Nothing survives that isn't the payload.** The corpus's best framing device is five words: "The best brief I ever saw." No client, no agency, no year. Adding "at a CPG agency in 2019" costs words and buys nothing.

**The swap test, at this length:** replace the key noun with a general one. "$200k/year employees" becomes "expensive employees", "a $15/mo Calendly subscription" becomes "a cheap tool". A post that survives the swap was never about anything.

**Sentences that work this short:**

- **Save the verdict for the end.** Load the setup first and the main verb last, so the reader can't get the point early. "If your $200k/year employees spend their time vibe coding a replacement for a $15/mo Calendly subscription, your business is not going to make it."
- **Abstract subject, physical verb.** "The best creators stay ahead because they get bored of their content before their audience does." The noun version ("audience fatigue precedes creator fatigue") is dead.
- **Proof by subtraction.** Listing what wasn't needed is cheaper than showing what was. "Nobody touched the product. Nobody changed the price."
- **Punctuation as timing.** "is Still. Open." Three words and two full stops carry what a paragraph would.

### Where to end

Nine kinds of ending appear, and they're the least standard and most important thing in the set: a verdict that names the thing ("That question was the work."); a prediction ("your business is not going to make it"); the contradiction restated and handed over unresolved; a pointed question at a named offender ("Who, exactly, are you waiting for??"); a parenthetical fact the reader uses to finish the argument; a line that undercuts the post's own sentiment, which is what pays for an earnest middle; a conceded objection, which costs hardness on purpose; a prescription, the only instructing ending, needed after a post that convicts the reader; and a P.S. that turns the observation into an action available now.

**No ending in the corpus** sums up the post, asks for agreement, tags anyone, or explains the joke. No post under about 90 words asks the reader for anything.

### The coda rule

**When it's finished, stop.** The surest way to turn a shortie into a poster is one line of encouragement after the mechanism. In three of the shortest pieces the weakest line is the last, and each reads as added after the post was done: a sixteen-word mechanism, then "Never stop evolving :)"; a real substitution ("You don't have to be the most charismatic, just the most useful"), then a sentence that adds nothing (stronger at 12 words than at 26); a precise observation, then "Do it. Trust me." **The test: read the last sentence alone. If it's an instruction with no consequence attached, that's where the post became a quote card.** A shortie holds a mechanism (an exchange, a substitution, a rate, a rejected alternative, a ratio); a quote card holds a value.

## Step 5. What it spends

A shortie has no evidence, no story and no procedure, so it can't buy agreement with proof, witness or use. **What's left is what saying it costs the person.** The reader reasons: they wouldn't say that unless they meant it. A statement that was free to make says nothing about what the writer believes, which is why free ones read as filler even when the sentence is good.

Four things it can spend: a named target who can see the post; a political price (a line that can't be written by someone who wants to be hired by the companies it mocks); self-incrimination (a repeated professional error admitted by someone whose job is not to make it); format risk (one corpus post is seventeen blank lines). The trap is **risk-shaped without risk**: scare quotes around "doesn't scale" or the "stupid" idea, the shape of a fight with no exposure.

This is also how a shortie meets check 17 (the writer is present): ten of the 21 have no "I", and the person is present in what the post costs them. A shortie with neither a first person nor a stake fails it.

### If it's a joke

Five of 21 are. A joke names a target and takes a position while keeping the exit of "it was a joke", and a failed 40-word post costs one day of feed, which is why so many shorties are jokes. How each kind fails:

- **Safely:** strip the comedy and the content survives as a clear grievance. **The design rule: the joke is the top layer, never the one carrying the weight.**
- **Dangerously:** sustained irony, where undetected sarcasm reads as agreement with the thing attacked. The corpus guards it two ways: praise too excessive to be sincere, and a flat checkable fact at the end that forces the ironic reading. That fact is insurance, and it's `borrowed: verify` like any fact.
- **Silently:** a typographic joke that doesn't render, or that nobody scrolls far enough to see.
- **Softly:** into a merely pleasant post.

Set up rigour to give the absurdity something to break ("ALL the people", "exactly one spot left"), but only in a register every reader can see is a joke. A longer comic piece is `humor-writing`.

## Checks the judge adds

On top of the stage's checks, each a FAIL with the line quoted. On this type, check 6 (required parts) reads against S7, which has none:

- **SH1. One turn,** locatable to a phrase. Two turns, or none.
- **SH2. Register.** A ratio has its numbers, sourced; a disposition has no invented numbers.
- **SH3. Path test** passes (the exhibit is verbatim, the collision unresolved, the pull still felt and sourced, the chant has a gradient, the bank's items stand alone).
- **SH4. No hedge** on the central claim.
- **SH5. No middle:** no sentence that's neither claim nor payoff, no supplied "because".
- **SH6. The coda.** The last line isn't an instruction with no consequence, a summary, or a repeat of the first line.
- **SH7. The swap test** on the key noun.
- **SH8. Something spent.**
- **SH9. The straight face** holds: no clause states the real view; irony is backed by a checkable fact.
- **SH10. A negative universal** ("nobody", "never") that's true; one counterexample in the comments ends it.

## Things that look like rules and aren't

Capitalization (one top post is all lowercase, another shouts in caps), how emphasis is done, sign-offs, emoji, line breaks, typos, whether there's a narrator, credentials (19 of 21 establish no standing), attached media, whether the moral is stated, and length: 14 and 391 words are both keepers. Length is a result of the construction, never a target.
