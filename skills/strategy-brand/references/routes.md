# Routes and their defaults

The goal picks the route. The route sets the defaults; the person's canon and capacity adjust them. The reasoning behind each default is in `docs/strategy-method.md`, section 4. Nothing here is about one person.

## Picking the route

| If the goal is mainly | Route |
| --- | --- |
| Clients for a service, advisory or coaching practice, sold to a narrow group at a high price | route-clients |
| Pipeline for a company the person leads or represents | route-pipeline |
| Many buyers at lower prices: courses, a community, a book, media | route-buyers |
| A role, a board seat, investors or partners | route-role |
| The reader isn't on the channel, or the business already runs on referrals | route-referrals |

"The channel" below means wherever the reader reads (the segment's `reads` field), which for business buyers is usually LinkedIn; the defaults transfer to another channel with the numbers re-checked. Mixed goals: pick the one the goal needs first and let the second ride on it as a weighting in the content plan. Don't show the person two systems.

## route-clients: clients for a practice

Two paces. **Clients soon:** run the network design defaults hard. **Body of work first:** the person isn't in a rush and wants the brand built on their frameworks and thesis. Most people get a weighted split, written as one plan.

| Choice | Clients soon | Body of work first | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Known for | One target association from the value types and the uniqueness map | Same, carried by the thesis and branches | The network has to be able to repeat what the person does |
| Reader | Hand-picked lists: Dream100, peers and creators, ICP list of 1,000 to 5,000, two or three champions per Dream100 target; 70/20/10 toward founder-led and scale-up companies | Same lists, built more slowly | Reverse audience building: pick the people before publishing |
| Mix | Deep, 75% or more; hyper-personalized pieces for named targets carry most results | Deep, mostly branches (frameworks, essays, the origin piece) | Wide content brings in people who won't buy and the buyers tune out |
| Off the table | Topic-popular posts (HR, workplace, the platform itself) | Same | Attention and revenue come from different topics otherwise |
| Channels | Feed for proof and the lists; DMs and collaborative series for the relationship; newsletter or essays for depth | Same, with essays doing more of the work | Relationship is the unit of progress, not the follower |
| Cadence | One or two targeted pieces a week for three months before judging, plus up to three ordinary pieces if they want reach; follow up at two weeks, then a month | Two or three pieces a week, most from the branches, one targeted piece every week or two | Judge research quality before response rates |
| Signals | Referrals, private thank-yous, mentions in rooms the person isn't in, language adopted, conversation quality, calls booked | Same, plus which branches draw replies | Followers and likes don't predict clients here |

## route-pipeline: pipeline for a company

| Choice | Default | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Known for | A take that differs from accepted practice in the buyer's world, with the company as the live case proving it | The person has to have something to say that a figurehead doesn't; otherwise the account is a distribution channel for announcements, which is a different and legitimate plan |
| Reader | The buyer, narrowed to title, company size and stage; connected to on purpose (100 to 250 best prospects, 10 to 20 requests a day, no automation; in an account motion, five above and ten below the decision maker) | The platform shows posts to people like the ones engaging |
| Mix | Tangential pain the buyer has next to the product, credibility stories from the career, the enemy storyline, build-in-public with stakes; proprietary data where the company has it; personal posts only where the buyer cares | Posts that advertise the product don't get read; pain the product sits next to does |
| Channels | Feed as the acquisition channel, profile as the landing page, DMs after engagement, the executive's voice in outbound and renewals once the brand exists | All traffic comes from people who read several posts and then look the person up |
| Selling | Give six to twelve months; product as a character; one link in many posts; campaigns in bursts | The reader should be most of the way through before any sale shows |
| Cadence | Three a week, four if growth needs it | Three excellent beats seven okay; stopping costs more than the extra posts gain |
| Signals | Self-reported attribution at signup, connection accept rate, ICP comments and DMs in the first 90 days, dark social mentions in the next 90, share of pipeline after six months; plus recruiting, investors, partners, speaking | A month or two before the content works in a real way; the business result takes longer, and no demos in year one is normal |

Paid promotion: the sources for this route use it; the studio leaves it out for now.

## route-buyers: an audience business

| Choice | Default | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Known for | Interest pillars (broad topics the business touches) weighted over expert pillars underneath | Reach outside the buying audience counts as social proof here |
| Reader | The ideal customer for the offer, reached through broad topics and pulled into the owned list | The list survives the algorithm |
| Mix | Educational depth actionable within minutes; carousels and cheat sheets for dwell time; the Slack test (can a reader share this at work); 70/20/10 across proven formats, iterations and swings | People follow what taught them something they then did |
| Channels | Feed as top of funnel, newsletter as the trust layer and the real asset, lead magnet posts to move readers over, then one offer at a time (free, lead magnet, low, mid, high ticket) | Posts can't go deep; trust comes before the transaction |
| Cadence | Four or more a week, raised on a schedule; on a new platform, about 12 posts in four weeks to learn what the audience calls quality, then more effort per piece | Audiences don't forgive a cut; volume is for learning |
| Signals | Reposts, saves, sends; list growth; conversion rate per platform rather than lead count | A 20% repost rate in the first hour meant a hit for one source; views alone mislead |

## route-role: a career, a seat or capital

| Choice | Default | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Known for | The same target association, with the career arc as the proof; the credibility-first opening runs longer | Nobody knows the person yet, and the arc is the evidence |
| Reader | Target-company founders, the person who'd be the boss, recruiters, employees who can refer; or customers who talk about the problem in public, investors, partner CEOs, analysts | The Dream100 by goal |
| Mix | Deep on the one thing; the student frame where the person is moving into something new; low personal share | Credibility first, then the point of view |
| Channels | Feed plus DMs and the real-world network | A referral business can have its best year with no audience growth |
| Cadence | Once or twice a week, held for a year | Held beats a burst; once a month beats not starting |
| Signals | Introductions, conversations started, who's reading from the lists | Followers don't matter here |

## route-referrals: the reader isn't on the channel, or the business runs on referrals

| Choice | Default | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Investment | Light. A minimum viable presence, targeted connections, a test of a couple of months before committing years | Recruiting, partners and investors still look |
| Feedback | Find where the reader actually talks (another platform, newsletters, podcasts) and learn there first | You can't start from scratch with no feedback mechanism |
| Signals | Referrals from the real-world network | Reach is the wrong measure |
| Channels | If another channel holds the reader, this whole method still applies; only the channel files change | |

## Every route

- Reputation check once or twice a year: what people say first when they hear the name, against the target association.
- The trust test for any move: will this make the audience trust the person more or less. If less, don't.
- Time horizon written into the plan: a month or two before it works in a real way, nothing judged before then, and a longer horizon for a business outcome.
