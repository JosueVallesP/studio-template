# Dana's LinkedIn posts (export, 2025)

## Post, March 2025
The first 1:1 I ran as a manager lasted eleven minutes. I asked "how's it going?", he said "fine", and I talked about the sprint for ten minutes. I did that every week for four months. Then he quit, and in the exit interview he said nobody had asked him what he wanted to work on in two years.

A 1:1 isn't a status meeting. If you can get the update from Jira, don't spend the 1:1 on it.

## Post, May 2025
I've coached 140 new engineering managers since 2021. The one thing almost all of them do in month two: they keep the hardest technical work for themselves "because it's faster."

It is faster. For about six weeks. Then you're the bottleneck on every important ticket and your team learns that the interesting work isn't for them.

## Post, July 2025
A head of engineering told me last week: "I promoted my best engineer and lost two people." She didn't lose them because he was a bad person. She lost them because nobody told him that his job had changed from writing the best code to making sure other people could.

We train people for months to become senior engineers. We give them a weekend to become managers.

## Post, September 2025
Things I wish someone had told me in my first year managing:
- Your calendar is now your job. Protect two mornings a week for thinking or you'll spend the year reacting.
- Feedback you save for the performance review is feedback you failed to give.
- Your team copies how you behave in incidents, not what you say in retros.
- "I'll take that" is the most expensive sentence a new manager says.

## Post, November 2025
At one client, we ran my cohort for 9 new managers at once instead of one by one. After six months, regretted attrition on their teams was 4%, against 15% for the managers promoted the year before without it. Same company, same pay, same CTO.
