## Post

In 2005, two amateur chess players won a tournament where grandmasters were also allowed to play with computers.

They had no titles and three computers, and the way I see it they won because they split the work better than the teams they played.

The computers calculated, and the two of them chose which positions the computers should look at.

The result came down to how the work was divided, more than to who knew the most chess.

Your work splits the same way.

Some of it is objective. Is the post under the character limit, are there typos, did it go out on time? Ask two smart people and they'll tell you the same thing.

Some of it is subjective. Is the idea worth a post? Will the people you want to reach care? Does it sound like you? Ask two smart people and you might get two answers.

AI is really, really good at the first half, and it's pretty cheap now.

On the second half it falls flat, because a model gives you the most likely answer. When there's one right answer, the most likely one is usually it. When the question is about taste, the most likely answer is the average one, and you don't want average on a post with your name on it.

So the way I split it is pretty simple:

Anything I can write a rule for, AI does.
Anything in the middle, we do together.
Anything that depends on my experience, my values or my taste, I do myself.

And to be clear, I'm not saying AI can't help with the hard calls. It can push back and show you what you missed before you decide.

Pick one task you gave AI this week and ask whether two smart people would come back with the same answer.

If they wouldn't, take it back.
