# Final

Two of my clients ran below two months of cash in the same quarter of 2024. Both had revenue growing 60 percent a year.

Both had hired ahead of the cash: new people on payroll before the money to carry them was in the bank. Hiring ahead isn't the problem by itself. Hiring two quarters before the cash can carry it is, and in bootstrapped software that's the failure I see most, more than bad product.

One of them made payroll that quarter because the founder lent the company the money.

The monthly P&L can't warn you about this. It reports the month that already happened. It has no line for the payrolls coming in the next three months.

Both now run a 13-week view. One page. We look at it every Monday for twenty minutes. Neither has gone below four months of cash since.

Across my 11 clients, the median went from 2.8 months of cash to 5.1 within a year of running the weekly view. It doesn't prevent every cash crunch. It shows you the problem while it's still cheap to fix.

Revenue is the story you tell. Cash is the story that's true.

If you run a bootstrapped software company, do one thing before Monday. Take the cash in the bank and divide it by what you burn net in a normal month, money in minus money out. That's your cash months. Reply with your cash months and I'll tell you what I'd look at first.
