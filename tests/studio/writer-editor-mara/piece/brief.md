# Brief

## The one idea
Two companies with growing revenue ran out of cash in the same quarter because they hired before the cash could carry it; a weekly cash view would have shown it months earlier.

## Fractal shape
- Claim: Revenue growth hides a cash problem until it's too late to fix cheaply.
- Why it's true: Hiring ahead of cash is the common failure in bootstrapped software (P4); the monthly P&L shows it a quarter late (P1).
- Evidence: S1 (two clients, both growing 60%, both under two months of cash the same quarter), R1 (median cash months across 11 clients went from 2.8 to 5.1 within a year of the weekly view)
- What it changes for the reader: They look at cash months this Monday instead of waiting for the month's close.

## The reader
- Segment: G1, bootstrapped SaaS founder, 2 to 10M ARR, 8 to 40 people, no finance hire
- Desire: D1, with WILL "I find out about problems from the bank balance." and WON'T (confirmed) "I'm scared to look."
- The problem they already feel, in their words: "Payroll week still makes me nervous."

## Purpose
Trust with the reader; proof that the 13-week view (B1) catches what the P&L misses. From the content plan's branch mix (B1 leads).

## Material
- STORY S1: "Two clients, both growing ARR 60% year on year, both ran below two months of cash in the same quarter because they'd hired ahead. One made payroll by a founder loan. Both now run a 13-week view; neither has gone below four months since." (permission: anonymize)
- RESULT R1: "Across 11 bootstrapped SaaS clients (2M to 9M ARR), median cash runway went from 2.8 months to 5.1 months within a year of adopting the weekly cash view." (permission: public, aggregated only)
- OPINION P4: "The common failure isn't bad product; it's hiring two quarters before the cash can carry it." Rejects "hire ahead of growth" advice.
- OPINION P1: "Bootstrapped founders should run the company off a 13-week cash forecast, not monthly P&L."
- LINE T: "Revenue is the story you tell. Cash is the story that's true."

## Outcome
The reader checks their cash months before Monday's close and understands why the monthly P&L showed nothing wrong.

## Not saying
Not about fundraising (X1). Not naming either client (X5). Not claiming the weekly view prevents every cash crunch.
