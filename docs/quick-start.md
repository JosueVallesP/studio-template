# Quick start

This page tells you what to do first, what you can use right away, and what has to wait until the studio knows you. If you only read one thing, read the first section. `/start` will also tell you the next step every time you ask.

## Day one: things you can use today

None of this needs the studio to know anything about you yet. It takes about an hour in total, and you can do it in one sitting or spread it across the week.

**1. Answer the setup questions (about five to ten minutes).** Say "set up my studio", or fill in the form if you were sent one. A few questions about facts: what you do, what you want, where you publish, how many hours you have, your files, and your brand if you have one. Everything else waits for the interviews, where the studio can ask a follow-up. *Why:* so the quick wins work today, and the studio never asks you something it could already know. You get back a one-page guide to your own studio (`YOUR-STUDIO.md`). This is the one step you can't skip, because everything else reads it.

**2. Connect your tools, if you use them (about 5 minutes).** Figma and Notion are optional. If you use them, connect them in Claude's settings, under Connectors. That's what lets the studio build things inside them for you. You don't need to set anything up inside either app. Without them, the studio describes pictures in words and `/status` shows what's waiting on you.

**3. See how your ideas connect (none of your time while it reads, then about 20 minutes).** Hand over what you've already made, public and private: posts, essays, talks, a book, call transcripts, memos (paste them into the chat or attach the files). Then say "/map". The studio reads all of it in one sitting, finds the claims you make and how they connect, and shows you what's hard to see from inside your own work: the few claims the rest hangs from, the same idea said many ways (so you can pick the best wording), two ideas that make a new one, claims you make all the time and never back up, places where you pull against yourself, and what you tell clients and have never published. Every finding quotes your own lines, and you get a picture of the whole map you can open in any browser. Then you go through the ten strongest, about 20 minutes, and say what's true. *Why:* you're too close to your own work to see its shape, and nobody, including you, has read all of it at once. It also means the interviews start from what your work already says instead of from zero. Private lines show on your map, marked private, and never go into a post without your yes. *If you skip it:* the interviews ask from zero and take more sessions.

**4. Get your visual kit, if you use Figma (about 10 minutes).** Say "set up my Figma". You open a link, click "Duplicate" in Figma, and send the studio the link to your copy. *Why:* so you can have pictures for your posts from day one. The kit isn't a set of forms to fill in. When you ask for a picture, the studio starts from your idea, works out its shape (two things that meet, a change over time, steps in order) and then draws it from the parts in the kit: dots, lines, boxes, curves, figures, labels. The file also shows twenty-two proven ways to draw an idea, like the 2x2, the before and after, the cycle and the funnel, as examples of how the parts go together. It also holds every other place your look shows up: your LinkedIn profile banner and photo, newsletter covers, your Substack logo, header and featured images, each at the right size with notes on how the platform crops it. If you gave the studio your brand colors and fonts, the kit comes back in them. *If you skip it:* the studio describes pictures in words until you want the kit. Nothing else depends on it.

**5. Get your dashboard, if you use Notion (about 10 minutes).** Say "set up my dashboard". You open a link and click "Duplicate" in Notion; the studio finds your copy and fills it. *Why:* one place to see what's waiting on you and what's moving, without opening files. It's empty today and fills as you work. *If you skip it:* "/status" shows the same thing inside Claude.

**6. See your libraries (about 10 minutes).** Say "/libraries". Your studio came with three libraries: real LinkedIn posts, real Substack essays and proven ways to draw an idea, each sorted by the job it does for the reader, like proving you're worth listening to, or turning a common belief on its head. The studio reads what you've given it so far and shows you the few moves your own material can carry today, each with a real example, and the visual moves in your own Figma kit if you set it up. *Why:* so you see what the studio builds with, and that it picks a move from what you have instead of handing you templates to fill. *If you skip it:* nothing changes; the studio uses the libraries on every piece either way.

**7. Get your first post (about 15 minutes).** Paste something you've already made into the chat, or attach the file to your message (drag it into the box where you type): an old post, notes from a talk, a call transcript, a long email you wrote. Then say "write a post from this". (If it's something you want the studio to keep drawing on, say "save this to my material" and it files it with the rest of what you've made.) The studio picks the strongest kind of post your material can carry, writes it, checks it in a separate pass, and makes a picture for it from your kit. Every fact about you comes from that material, so no story, client or number gets made up; the reasoning and the framing around them are the studio's own writing. *Why:* so you see the studio work on your own material before you give it hours of interviews.

You can skip any of these and come back to it. If a tool isn't working, move to the next one.

## Weeks one and two: the interviews

*Why the interviews:* the studio can only write what it knows. Until it knows what you believe, your stories, your proof and how you talk, every post depends on what you hand it that day. The interviews are where it learns all of that, and nothing it writes about you gets used until you've said it's right.

If you made your map, the first session is its review: the ten strongest findings, one at a time. If you skipped it and have handed over material since, say "/map" first.

Then say "start my brand tree", or `/interview` and a layer. Each session covers one layer of what you believe (your values, how you work, your opinions about your field, the idea that ties them together, the one claim your work proves) and takes 20 to 30 minutes. The studio doesn't ask you to describe yourself. It asks about things that happened: work you turned down, a project that went well for a reason nobody expected, the last thing you had to un-teach a client. Then it shows you what it sees in your answers, with your own lines under it, and you say whether it's right. With the map done, the first session is mostly you saying "yes, that's me" or "no, that's not". You can stop at any point. Nothing it writes about you gets used until you've said it's right.

There's no fixed order, and each layer is useful the day it's confirmed: your values and habits shape how your drafts sound, your opinions give your posts a stance, and the idea underneath them makes essays possible. About eleven or twelve sessions reach what the pipeline and the full strategy need, and `/post` and `/essay` work from your material before that; the map shortens it. `/start` says how many items of the studio's minimum list are done (the list lives in your `canon/progress.md`).

## After the interviews

**The strategy (one session).** Say "build my strategy". The studio works out what you should be known for, who for, on which channels, in what shape and how often, and shows you every choice with its reason. You say yes or no to each.

**The manifesto (one session, optional), once your thesis is confirmed.** Say "/manifesto". Why you do what you do and why others should care, in one piece: a full version the studio draws from, and a public one in the form your brand calls for. The look gets built after it, from it.

**Make it yours (one session).** Say "build my look". The studio uses what the interviews found to build your look, or to check and tune the one you have, and puts it on your visual kit and your dashboard. It also draws your two or three main ideas once, so the same pictures can come back across your posts.

**The check (about 20 minutes).** Say "/check". It confirms everything is working and runs one piece through every step with your own files: something that happened this week, a brief you approve, a draft and a review. Shipping it is your call, later.

## If you skip the interviews

The studio still works, the same way your first post did: you hand it something, and it writes from that and nothing else. Here's what you don't get:

- "/strategy" still gives you a draft plan from your material, but nothing you've confirmed stands behind it, so its picks for what to write next are guesses from your files.
- Every post needs you to hand something over first.
- Your voice gets guessed from each file, so drafts need more editing.
- The review checks the writing and the facts in your material, but it can't catch a draft that says something you don't believe.
- Your posts don't add up to a body of work, because nothing ties them to one main idea.
- Your look stays as it is, because building it from who you are needs what the interviews find.

You can start the interviews whenever you want: say "start my brand tree".

## Your week, once you're set up

Minutes, not hours. The studio exists to make good work faster and more structured, not to become a second job. A working rhythm, which the studio adjusts to how you actually work:

- **Whenever something happens:** capture it. About two minutes. A client result, a conversation, an opinion. Talk it into your phone if that's easier; your own words are the best raw material the studio gets.
- **Once or twice a week, ten minutes:** journal. Today's story in one line, or one thing to make sense of.
- **Once a week, about an hour:** the production session. "/shape" on what came in, pick, make, refine, decide.
- **Once a week:** say "/status", answer what's waiting on your ideas, decide on the pieces. About twenty minutes.
- **Every quarter:** the changes the studio has suggested to your files, your content plan, and another interview session if you want one.

Setup takes a week or two. After that, the content itself takes a month or two to work in a real way. Judge nothing before then.

## If you get stuck

- Say "/start" during setup and "/status" after it. Both give you one next step.
- If a tool can't be reached, check it's connected in Claude's settings, under Connectors. The studio keeps working in files in the meantime.
- If a post contains a fact, a client or a number you don't recognize, that's a bug. Kill the piece and say so.
