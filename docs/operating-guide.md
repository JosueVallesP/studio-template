# How to run the studio

One page. Read it once after setup; after that `/status` tells you what to do. Your own copy, written for how you said you work, is `YOUR-STUDIO.md` in your folder.

## What you say

The job commands at the top do one thing now, with no gates, and none of them waits for the interviews. They read `material/` (what you've already made), `inbox/` (what you handed over in the chat) and whatever your files hold, and they get better as the interviews fill your files in. The commands below them keep the garden: what's ready to make, your decisions, and what the studio learns from what you shipped.

## Three things you do

Everything you do with the studio is one of these.

**Capture.** Something happened: a client result, a conversation, an opinion you had in the shower, a document you wrote. Tell the studio. The shortest version is "write a post, here's what happened" followed by what happened. Don't tidy it up first. The studio sorts it and asks for what's missing.

**Decide.** The studio stops at two gates and waits for you. Before a draft, it shows you a brief: the one idea, who it's for, what material it uses and what it leaves out. You approve it, change it or kill it. After the draft and review, it shows you the piece: you ship it, send it back, hold it or kill it. The studio never publishes anything. You do.

**Review.** Now and then the studio finds something new about you, like a POV it heard you state three times or a story it keeps reaching for. It writes that down as a draft in your files, which feeds nothing until you confirm it. A change to something you've already confirmed, or a belief that contradicts an older one, waits in `proposals.md` until you say yes. Nothing about you changes without your say-so.

## The rhythm

- **Daily, or whenever it happens:** capture. Two minutes.
- **End of a session:** `/end`. Two minutes; it says back what you made and decided, lists the choices worth keeping (the `/learn` step inside it) and saves.
- **Weekly:** `/status`, answer what's waiting on your ideas, decide on the pieces, give the ready ones a day ("queue it for Tuesday"). Twenty minutes. `/coach` when you don't know what to pick.
- **Quarterly:** go through the proposals you've been putting off, read your content plan, and run "continue my brand tree" to confirm the drafts that have been sitting there.

## Where your things live

`studio.manifest.yaml` lists which folders are yours and which are the studio's. Yours hold your files about you, your material, your pieces and your settings. When the studio updates, it replaces its own folders and never touches yours. Your work is saved after every reply (a commit, and a push when your folder is on GitHub). You never type git.

## What runs on its own

Six things happen without a command: your studio's state is read out when you open it, each crew role is held to its own files (none of them can change your files about you), each crew role is held to its own short list of commands, every draft gets the mechanical checks the moment it's written, every reply is checked against the house rules before it reaches you, and your work is saved after every reply. `docs/hooks.md` lists them. End a session with `/end`; it writes down what you decided, which the save can't know.

## The studio thinks with you

It never makes up a fact about you, and it's always looking for what your facts add up to. Say `/think` and it reads everything you've given it and tells you what it sees that you haven't named: three beliefs that are one, a method you keep using without writing it down, a shape your ideas take, a belief every piece of yours assumes, two pieces that add up to a third. Each comes with the lines it was drawn from and one question, and nothing changes until you say it's true. Say `/scout` and it goes outside for your point of view: a real case, the best argument against you, evidence, and what your reader is talking about this week, filed with sources so a piece can use them. The interviewer, the strategist and the librarian do the same as they go.

## Two rules worth knowing

Every fact about you in a piece comes from your files or from what you gave it: your stories, your numbers, your clients, your words. If the studio needs one it doesn't have, it asks or stops. If a piece ever contains a fact, a client or a number you don't recognise, that's a bug, not a feature. Kill the piece and say so. The rest of the piece is the studio's writing: why the idea is true, how it works, what it means for the reader, an example from the world when yours would do better with one. That part isn't copied from your files, and it shouldn't be; the editor checks it for truth instead. If the studio reads a belief into your material that you haven't stated, it marks it and names it to you before the piece ships, so you can say whether it's true.

Your material and what you tell the studio are used as they are. Beliefs the studio wrote down from an interview wait until you confirm them. So if a belief you've stated isn't showing up in your pieces, the likely reason is that it's still a draft. `/status` tells you how many are waiting.
